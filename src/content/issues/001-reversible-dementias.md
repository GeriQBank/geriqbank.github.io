## This week’s repeat

> **Paper III, question 29 (7 marks, asked 3 times):** What are the reversible / treatable dementias? How would you investigate and manage them, and what investigations rule them out?

### The 7-mark skeleton

1. **Define it:** cognitive impairment caused by a treatable condition. Early treatment can halt or improve it, though full reversal is uncommon.
2. **List the causes:** drugs (anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, opioids), depression, hypothyroidism, vitamin B12 deficiency, normal pressure hydrocephalus, chronic subdural haematoma, neurosyphilis and HIV, alcohol and thiamine deficiency, metabolic disturbance, obstructive sleep apnoea.
3. **Investigate:** blood count, electrolytes, calcium, glucose, renal and liver function, TSH and B12; syphilis and HIV serology where indicated; CT or MRI brain.
4. **Manage:** treat the cause, deprescribe, treat depression, and reassess cognition afterwards, because improvement is often partial.

## One guideline change worth knowing

The 2024 ESC hypertension guidelines added an “elevated blood pressure” category (120–139/70–89 mmHg) and set a treated systolic target of 120–129 mmHg, if tolerated.

For people aged 85 or over, or with moderate-to-severe frailty, symptomatic orthostatic hypotension or limited life expectancy, they advise a more lenient target: as low as reasonably achievable.

<p class="hand">Quote this in any question on hypertension in the very old, and pair it with a standing blood pressure.</p>

<p class="small muted">Source: 2024 ESC Guidelines for the management of elevated blood pressure and hypertension.</p>

## Carry this into the hall

*Check TSH and B12 in every new cognitive complaint. They are cheap, quick, and occasionally the whole answer.*
