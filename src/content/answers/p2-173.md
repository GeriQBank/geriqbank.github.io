## Answer at a glance

3. History and examination.
4. Investigations.
5. Management: the seizures, the cause, AF, diabetes and general care.

## Immediate care

- ABCDE, recovery position, oxygen, IV access.
- **Check glucose at once:** hypoglycaemia from insulin or a sulfonylurea is common and treatable.
- If seizures recur or last over 5 minutes, follow the status protocol.

## Why is she still confused?

- **Prolonged post-ictal state, or non-convulsive status.**
- **Vascular:**
  - Cardioembolic stroke from AF, especially if not anticoagulated.
  - Intracerebral haemorrhage (anticoagulant, hypertension).
  - Subdural haematoma (falls plus anticoagulant).
  - Todd’s paresis can mimic a stroke.
- **Metabolic:** low glucose; hyperglycaemic hyperosmolar state (which causes focal seizures); **low sodium** from a thiazide; low calcium or magnesium; uraemia; liver failure.
- **Drugs:** tramadol, some antibiotics, benzodiazepine or alcohol withdrawal, digoxin toxicity.
- **Infection:** meningitis, encephalitis, or sepsis with delirium.
- **Cardiac:** arrhythmia causing convulsive syncope (AF with pauses, VT), or MI.

## Assessment

- **History from family:** onset, previous seizures and falls, baseline cognition, and every drug: which anticoagulant, antidiabetics, antihypertensives, digoxin, anything new.
- **Examination:** GCS, focal signs, neck stiffness, tongue bite, injuries, fundi.

## Investigations

- Glucose, sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, renal and liver function, blood count.
- INR and coagulation, troponin, ECG, blood gas, digoxin level.
- Blood cultures if febrile.
- **Urgent CT brain** for bleed, stroke or subdural. Add CTA if she might be a thrombectomy candidate; a seizure at onset doesn’t rule this out.
- MRI later.
- **EEG** if she isn’t improving (non-convulsive status).
- Lumbar puncture after imaging if infection is suspected.

## Management

- **Seizures:** a benzodiazepine if ongoing, then **levetiracetam** (dosed for the kidneys). Prefer it to phenytoin, which interacts with warfarin and DOACs and can cause arrhythmias.
- **Treat the cause:**
  - **Hypoglycaemia:** IV dextrose, with prolonged monitoring after sulfonylureas (octreotide if needed).
  - **Hyponatraemia:** hypertonic saline if seizing.
  - **Intracerebral haemorrhage:** reverse the anticoagulant (PCC plus vitamin K for warfarin; andexanet or PCC for Xa inhibitors; idarucizumab for dabigatran) and lower systolic pressure towards 140.
  - **Ischaemic stroke:** thrombolysis or thrombectomy if eligible.
  - **Subdural:** neurosurgery.
  - **Infection:** antibiotics and aciclovir.
- **AF:** rate control; hold anticoagulation if bleeding, and plan when to restart.
- **Diabetes and blood pressure:** avoid hypoglycaemia; review the sulfonylurea; avoid over-lowering blood pressure in ischaemic stroke; stop a thiazide if sodium is low.
- **General care:** manage delirium, take aspiration precautions (swallow screen), give VTE prophylaxis, and support nutrition.

## After recovery

- Long-term antiseizure medication if there’s a structural cause, such as an old stroke.
- A full medication review.
- Weigh anticoagulation risk against benefit, including falls and HAS-BLED.
- Falls and safety plan, caregiver education, advance care planning, and driving advice.

## One-liner

After a seizure in an anticoagulated 80-year-old, check the sugar and the sodium first, then scan for a bleed.
