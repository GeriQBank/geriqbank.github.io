## Answer at a glance

3. Assessment of the patient and the wound.

## Definition

Localised damage to skin and underlying tissue over a bony prominence, from pressure with or without shear. The current term is “pressure injury”.

## Risk factors

Immobility, sensory loss, incontinence, malnutrition, low BMI, frailty, diabetes, vascular disease, cognitive impairment, sedation, hypotension.

## Common sites

Sacrum, heels, ischial tuberosities, trochanters, occiput, and ears under medical devices.

## Staging (NPIAP)

- **Stage 1:** non-blanching redness of intact skin.
- **Stage 2:** partial-thickness loss, with dermis exposed.
- **Stage 3:** full-thickness skin loss, with fat visible.
- **Stage 4:** full-thickness tissue loss, with bone, tendon or muscle exposed.
- **Unstageable:** the base is hidden by slough or eschar.
- **Deep tissue injury:** persistent deep maroon or purple skin.
- Also device-related and mucosal injuries.

## Assessment

- **Patient:** risk scores (Braden, 18 or below means at risk; Waterlow; Norton), nutrition (MNA), continence, pain, comorbidity, goals.
- **Wound:** site, size, depth, undermining and tunnelling, tissue type, exudate, odour, edges, surrounding skin, signs of infection.
- Track progress with the PUSH tool and photographs.
- Suspect osteomyelitis if the wound probes to bone; check ESR and MRI.

## Management

- **Offload pressure:** reposition every 2–4 hours, 30° lateral tilt, float the heels.
  - High-specification foam mattresses; alternating or low-air-loss surfaces for stage 3–4.
  - Limit sitting and use a proper cushion.
- **Nutrition:** protein 1.25–1.5 g/kg a day, energy 30–35 kcal/kg a day, fluids, and arginine-zinc-antioxidant supplements if malnourished with stage 2 or worse.
- **Moisture:** manage incontinence and look after the skin.
- **Wound bed preparation (TIME):** tissue debridement, infection control, moisture balance, edge advancement.
  - Debride by sharp, autolytic, enzymatic or larval methods, but leave a stable, dry heel eschar alone.
  - Dressings: films or hydrocolloids for stage 2, foams or alginates for exudate, hydrogels for dry wounds.
  - Negative-pressure therapy for selected stage 3–4 ulcers.
- **Infection:** systemic antibiotics for cellulitis, osteomyelitis or sepsis; topical antiseptics for biofilm.
- Pain relief, and flap surgery for selected patients.
- When healing isn’t realistic, set comfort-focused goals.

## Treatment by stage

- **Stage 1:** relieve pressure, protect the skin (barrier film or cream), moisturise, and manage moisture. Don’t massage.
- **Stage 2:** clean with saline, then a moist-healing dressing (hydrocolloid, film or foam); manage incontinence.
- **Stage 3:** debride slough and necrosis, fill dead space (alginate, hydrofibre or foam), treat infection, and consider negative-pressure therapy.
- **Stage 4:** as for stage 3, plus assessment for osteomyelitis (probe to bone, MRI), surgical debridement, and flap repair in selected patients.
- **Unstageable:** debride to reveal the stage, except a stable, dry heel eschar, which should be left alone.
- **Deep tissue injury:** offload completely, protect the skin, and watch how it evolves.
- **At every stage:** offloading, nutrition and pain relief.

## Prevention

- Risk assessment on admission and regularly after.
- Daily skin checks, a repositioning schedule, support surfaces, heel protection.
- Moisture control, nutrition, mobilisation, and care around devices.
- Staff and family education.

## Examiner’s traps

## One-liner

Turn, offload, feed and keep dry, because prevention is easier than healing.
