## Answer at a glance

1. Why falls matter.

3. Interventions that work.

## Why it matters

About 1 in 3 people over 65 fall each year. Falls are the leading cause of injury death in older adults and drive fear, immobility and care-home admission.

## A risk-stratified approach

World Falls Guidelines 2022, as in the [falls assessment answer](../64/):

- Screen everyone with three questions.
- **Low risk:** education and exercise.
- **Intermediate risk:** a strength and balance programme.
- **High risk:** a multifactorial assessment with tailored interventions.

## Interventions that work

- **Exercise:** progressive balance and strength training, about 3 hours a week (Otago, tai chi). It cuts the rate of falls by about a quarter (Cochrane 2019).
- **Medication review:** reduce or stop fall-risk drugs (sedatives, antipsychotics, antidepressants, opioids, anticholinergics, antihypertensives causing postural drops), using STOPPFall.
- **Home hazard modification by an OT:** lighting, rails, removing rugs, non-slip mats. Effective in high-risk people.
- **Vision:** first-eye cataract surgery; avoid multifocal glasses when walking outdoors.
- **Feet:** footwear and podiatry.
- **Medical causes:** postural hypotension, cardiac syncope (pacing for cardioinhibitory carotid sinus syndrome), nocturia, and vitamin D deficiency (supplements prevent falls only if deficient).
- **Fear of falling:** CBT plus exercise.
- **Walking aids,** prescribed and fitted by a physiotherapist.
- **Hospitals and care homes:** multifactorial programmes, low beds, no restraints, hip protectors; bed alarms alone don’t work.
- **After a fall:** teach how to get up, provide a personal alarm, and treat osteoporosis to prevent fractures.

## One-liner

Strength and balance training works for almost everyone; everything else depends on the person’s own risks.
