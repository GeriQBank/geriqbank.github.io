## Answer at a glance

1. Define and classify (ESC 2023).
2. Highlight what matters in older adults.
3. Pathophysiology by type.
4. Clinical features.
5. Investigations.
6. Treatment by type, through an older-adult lens.

## Definition

Heart muscle that is structurally or functionally abnormal without enough coronary, hypertensive, valvular or congenital disease to explain it.

## Classification (ESC 2023, by phenotype)

Hypertrophic (HCM), dilated (DCM), non-dilated left ventricular (NDLVC), arrhythmogenic right ventricular (ARVC) and restrictive (RCM). Takotsubo is a separate, usually reversible syndrome.

## What matters in older adults

- **Wild-type transthyretin (ATTR) amyloid:** typically an older man with HFpEF, carpal tunnel syndrome, lumbar canal stenosis, and a low-voltage ECG despite thick walls.
- **HCM:** often diagnosed late, or mislabelled as hypertensive heart disease.
- **DCM:** from alcohol, chemotherapy (anthracyclines, trastuzumab), tachycardia or late-onset genetic disease.
- **Takotsubo:** in older women after emotional or physical stress.

## Pathophysiology

- **DCM:** a dilated, weak ventricle with neurohormonal activation.
- **HCM:** hypertrophy, diastolic dysfunction, dynamic outflow obstruction, ischaemia and arrhythmia.
- **RCM:** stiff ventricles, raised filling pressures and large atria.

## Clinical features

Breathlessness, oedema, atrial fibrillation, syncope, emboli and sudden death. In the elderly, often just fatigue, falls or reduced activity.

## Investigations

- ECG, NT-proBNP, troponin.
- **Echocardiography:** ejection fraction, wall thickness and strain; apical sparing suggests amyloid.
- Cardiac MRI with late gadolinium enhancement.
- Exclude coronary disease.
- **Suspected amyloid:** serum and urine immunofixation with free light chains, plus bone scintigraphy. Strong uptake with no monoclonal protein diagnoses ATTR without a biopsy.
- Genetic testing where relevant; Holter monitoring.

## Treatment

- **DCM or HFrEF:** the four pillars (ARNI or ACE inhibitor/ARB, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2 inhibitor), started low and titrated slowly; diuretics for congestion; ICD or CRT by criteria, weighed against frailty and goals.
- **HCM:** beta-blocker or verapamil; avoid dehydration, nitrates and vasodilators; mavacamten for obstructive HCM; septal reduction if obstruction is refractory.
- **ATTR amyloid:** stabilisers (tafamidis, acoramidis) or a TTR silencer (vutrisiran), with cautious diuresis.
- **Atrial fibrillation in HCM or amyloid:** anticoagulate regardless of the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score.

## The older-adult lens

Watch for hypotension, kidney function, falls and polypharmacy. Discuss goals early, and involve palliative care in advanced disease.

## Examiner’s traps

- Calling LVH hypertensive without considering amyloid or HCM
- Giving nitrates in obstructive HCM

## One-liner

HFpEF, carpal tunnel and a low-voltage ECG in an older man mean ATTR amyloid until proven otherwise.
