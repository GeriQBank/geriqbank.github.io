## Answer at a glance

1. Assessment: lipids, secondary causes, cardiovascular risk.
2. LDL targets.
3. Treatment in steps, including high triglycerides.
4. Monitoring and statin side effects.
5. Older adults.

## Assess

- Lipid profile (fasting isn’t needed), non-HDL cholesterol, ApoB, and Lp(a) once.
- **Secondary causes:** hypothyroidism, diabetes, nephrotic syndrome, CKD, cholestasis, alcohol, drugs (thiazides, steroids, antipsychotics).
- **Cardiovascular risk:** WHO South Asia charts or QRISK3; Lipid Association of India risk categories.

## LDL targets

- **ESC/EAS:** very high risk below 55 mg/dL with a 50% or greater fall; high risk below 70; moderate below 100.
- **Lipid Association of India:** stricter goals for very-high-risk Indians (below 50 mg/dL, and 30 or below for extreme risk).

## Treatment

- **Lifestyle:** less saturated and trans fat and refined carbohydrate; more fibre and nuts; exercise; weight loss; no smoking; less alcohol.
- **Statins first:** high intensity (atorvastatin 40–80 mg, rosuvastatin 20–40 mg) lowers LDL by 50% or more.
- **Then, in steps:**
  - Add ezetimibe.
  - Then a PCSK9 inhibitor (evolocumab, alirocumab) or inclisiran (twice-yearly siRNA).
  - Bempedoic acid for statin intolerance (CLEAR Outcomes).
- **High triglycerides:**
  - Lifestyle, glucose control, stop alcohol.
  - Fenofibrate if triglycerides are over 500–1,000 mg/dL (pancreatitis risk).
  - Icosapent ethyl for residual risk (REDUCE-IT); mixed omega-3 products don’t help.

## Monitoring

- Lipids 4–12 weeks after starting or changing treatment, then every 3–12 months.
- Baseline LFT; CK only if symptoms appear.

## Statin side effects

Myalgia (largely nocebo, as SAMSON showed); rare myopathy or rhabdomyolysis; a small rise in diabetes risk; rare transaminase rises.

## Older adults

- **Secondary prevention:** continue statins at any age.
- **Primary prevention:** reasonable at 70–75. Over 75, individualise by frailty, life expectancy and pill burden; a calcium score helps (trials such as STAREE and PREVENTABLE are underway).
- Stopping statins near the end of life is safe.

## One-liner

For LDL, lower is better and statins come first; in the very old, let frailty and life expectancy decide primary prevention.
