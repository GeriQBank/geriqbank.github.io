## What they are

Gut hormones released after meals that boost **glucose-dependent** insulin secretion:

- GLP-1, from L cells in the ileum and colon.
- GIP, from K cells in the duodenum.

## The incretin effect

Oral glucose triggers far more insulin than the same glucose given IV, about half or more of the post-meal response. The effect is reduced in type 2 diabetes.

## Actions

- **GLP-1:** raises insulin, lowers glucagon, slows gastric emptying, increases satiety.
- **GIP:** raises insulin and affects fat metabolism.
- DPP-4 breaks native GLP-1 down within about 2 minutes.

## Incretin-based drugs

- **DPP-4 inhibitors (gliptins):** sitagliptin, vildagliptin, saxagliptin, linagliptin (no renal adjustment), teneligliptin (widely used in India), alogliptin.
  - Oral, weight-neutral, low hypoglycaemia risk; HbA1c falls by about 0.5–0.8%.
  - Cardiovascularly neutral (TECOS, CARMELINA), though saxagliptin raised heart failure admissions (SAVOR).
  - Side effects: rare pancreatitis, joint pain, bullous pemphigoid.
  - **Well suited to frail older adults.**
- **GLP-1 receptor agonists:** see the [GLP-1 receptor agonists answer](../../paper-4/177/).
- **Dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist:** tirzepatide. Triple agonists such as retatrutide are in trials.

## One-liner

Incretins make insulin release glucose-dependent, which is why incretin drugs rarely cause hypoglycaemia on their own.
