## Answer at a glance

## What it is

A 4-repeat tauopathy, usually starting in the 60s.

## Richardson syndrome (classic)

- Early postural instability, with backward falls in the first year.
- Vertical supranuclear gaze palsy: downgaze is affected first, and slow vertical saccades appear early.
- Axial rigidity with neck extension, and a staring face with reduced blinking.
- Dysarthria, dysphagia, frontal dysfunction and apathy; the “applause sign”.

## Variants (MDS 2017)

PSP-parkinsonism, progressive gait freezing, corticobasal and speech/language forms.

## MRI

Midbrain atrophy, seen as the “hummingbird” sign on sagittal views and “Mickey Mouse” on axial views; the MR parkinsonism index.

## Management

There is no disease-modifying treatment.

- Try levodopa, though response is usually poor; amantadine.
- Physiotherapy, with a weighted walker for falls.
- Speech and swallowing therapy, and discuss feeding options early.
- Eye lubricants; botulinum toxin for blepharospasm.
- Treat depression, and involve palliative care.

## Prognosis

Median survival is about 6–7 years, and aspiration pneumonia is the usual cause of death.

## Examiner’s trap

## One-liner

Early backward falls plus slow vertical saccades point to PSP, not Parkinson’s.
