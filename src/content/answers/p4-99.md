## Answer at a glance

1. Why older adults are more vulnerable.
2. Effects and ill effects.
3. Detection and screening.
4. Types of drinking problems in later life.
5. Management: brief intervention, detoxification, relapse prevention and de-addiction services.

## Why older adults are more vulnerable

Less body water and lean mass mean higher blood alcohol levels for the same intake. Older adults also metabolise alcohol more slowly, their brains are more sensitive to it, and they take more interacting drugs.

## Effects

The old idea of a “protective” moderate intake hasn’t held up. WHO now states that no level of drinking is safe for health.

## Ill effects

- Falls, fractures and injuries.
- **Brain:** cognitive decline, alcohol-related dementia, Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome, delirium in intoxication or withdrawal, depression and suicide, poor sleep.
- **Heart:** hypertension, atrial fibrillation, cardiomyopathy.
- **Gut:** liver disease, GI bleeding, pancreatitis.
- **Nutrition and bone:** malnutrition (thiamine, folate) and osteoporosis.
- **Other:** several cancers, hypoglycaemia with sulfonylureas or insulin, neuropathy, incontinence, self-neglect and isolation.
- **Drug interactions:** benzodiazepines and opioids (sedation), warfarin (unstable INR), paracetamol (liver toxicity), metronidazole.

## Detection

Alcohol problems are often missed, presenting as falls, confusion, poor self-care, or an unexplained high MCV or GGT.

- Screen with AUDIT-C, AUDIT (8 or more), CAGE or SMAST-G, and ask about quantity and frequency.
- **Blood clues:** MCV, GGT, an AST/ALT ratio above 2, CDT.

## Types

Early-onset (lifelong, more complications) and late-onset (triggered by bereavement, retirement, loneliness, pain or insomnia, with a better outlook).

## Management

- **Brief intervention** using the FRAMES approach (Feedback, Responsibility, Advice, Menu, Empathy, Self-efficacy); it works in older adults.
- Treat the triggers: depression, loneliness, pain, insomnia. Involve the family.
- **Detoxification:** older adults have a higher risk of complicated withdrawal (seizures, delirium tremens), so admit heavy drinkers or those with comorbidities.
  - Give symptom-triggered benzodiazepines guided by CIWA-Ar, preferring lorazepam or oxazepam in the elderly or in liver disease.
  - Give parenteral thiamine before glucose.
  - Correct fluids, magnesium, potassium and phosphate.
- **Relapse prevention:**
  - Psychological: CBT, motivational interviewing, peer groups such as AA, family support.
  - Drugs: acamprosate (dose for the kidneys) or naltrexone (not with liver failure or opioids). Avoid disulfiram in older adults.
- **De-addiction services in India:** government de-addiction centres, NGOs, and Tele-MANAS (14416) for mental health support.

## One-liner

In an older person with falls, confusion or a high MCV, ask about alcohol, because nobody else will.
