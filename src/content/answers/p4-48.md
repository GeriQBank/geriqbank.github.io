## Definition (WHO)

Interventions that optimise functioning and reduce disability in people with health conditions, in interaction with their environment. Geriatric rehabilitation restores function, independence and quality of life through a multidisciplinary, goal-directed, time-limited process.

## Framework (ICF)

Body structures and functions, activities and participation, plus environmental and personal factors.

## Principles

- Start early, and set SMART goals with the patient.
- Interdisciplinary teamwork based on CGA.
- Treat comorbidities; prevent complications (deconditioning, pressure ulcers, contractures).
- Involve the family; plan discharge from day one.
- A continuum from acute to subacute to community care.

## Evaluation

- **Medical:** diagnoses, medicines, pain, nutrition, continence, mood, cognition (delirium, dementia), vision and hearing.
- **Function:** ADL (Barthel, Katz, FIM) and IADL (Lawton).
- **Mobility and balance:** timed up-and-go, gait speed, SPPB, Berg balance scale.
- **Cognition and mood:** MoCA or MMSE, GDS.
- **Social and environmental:** home, carers, finances, safety.
- **Prognosis:** premorbid function, cognition, motivation, support.

## Components (the team)

- Geriatrician or physiatrist; rehabilitation nurse.
- **Physiotherapist:** strength, balance, gait, endurance, transfers.
- **Occupational therapist:** ADL training, assistive devices, home modification, cognitive rehabilitation.
- **Speech and language therapist:** communication, swallowing.
- Dietitian, psychologist, social worker, orthotist or prosthetist, pharmacist.

## Interventions

Progressive resistance, balance and aerobic exercise; ADL retraining; aids and home adaptation (see the [mobility aids answer](../57/)); pain, nutrition and continence programmes; cognitive rehabilitation; psychological support; carer training.

## Settings

Inpatient units, intermediate care, day hospitals, home-based care, community-based rehabilitation, telerehabilitation. In India, NPHCE district rehabilitation units.

## Outcomes

Change in function (Barthel), discharge destination, quality of life, readmissions.

## Special areas

Stroke, hip fracture (orthogeriatrics), cardiac and pulmonary rehabilitation, amputees, Parkinson’s disease, frailty and sarcopenia.

## One-liner

Geriatric rehabilitation is goal-setting with the patient, delivered by a team, measured in function, and age is never the reason not to start.
