## Answer at a glance

1. Advances in diagnosis.
2. Better levodopa delivery, and apomorphine.
3. Newer add-on drugs, and treatments for non-motor symptoms.
4. Devices.
5. Disease modification, still in research.
6. Older adults.

## Diagnosis

The α-synuclein seed amplification assay (CSF, skin) has enabled biological definitions of Parkinson’s disease (SynNeurGe, NSD-ISS 2024). DAT-SPECT helps in unclear parkinsonism.

## Better levodopa delivery

- Extended-release carbidopa-levodopa capsules (IPX203, 2024).
- Continuous subcutaneous foslevodopa–foscarbidopa (2024).
- Levodopa–carbidopa intestinal gel through a PEG-J tube.
- Inhaled levodopa for “off” episodes.

## Apomorphine

Rescue injection, or continuous subcutaneous infusion for motor fluctuations.

## Newer add-ons for “off” time

Opicapone (once-daily COMT inhibitor), safinamide (MAO-B inhibitor), istradefylline (adenosine A2A antagonist); extended-release amantadine for dyskinesia.

## Non-motor symptoms

Pimavanserin for psychosis, droxidopa for neurogenic postural hypotension, rivastigmine for Parkinson’s disease dementia.

## Devices

- Deep brain stimulation (STN or GPi), now including adaptive, closed-loop DBS (2025).
- MR-guided focused ultrasound (thalamotomy for tremor, pallidotomy).

## Disease modification (still research)

- Anti-α-synuclein antibodies (prasinezumab, mixed results).
- GLP-1 agonists: lixisenatide slowed motor progression slightly (2024), but exenatide failed in phase 3 (2025).
- LRRK2 and GBA-targeted drugs; stem-cell-derived dopamine neuron transplants in early trials.
- Aerobic exercise may slow progression.

## Older adults

- Device therapy is limited by age, cognition and frailty; DBS needs good cognition.
- Keep regimens simple, and watch for psychosis, postural hypotension and falls.

## One-liner

Levodopa is still the backbone; what’s new is smoother delivery, smarter devices and the first biological tests.
