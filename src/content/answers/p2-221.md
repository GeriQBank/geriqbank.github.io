## DOTS (WHO, 1995)

Five parts: political commitment, bacteriological diagnosis, standardised supervised treatment with patient support, a reliable drug supply, and monitoring.

## India’s National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP)

It builds on DOTS with:

- Daily fixed-dose combinations by weight band.
- Universal drug-susceptibility testing with GeneXpert or Truenat.
- Ni-kshay registration and digital adherence support.
- Nutrition support through Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana, now ₹1,000 a month.

## Drug-sensitive TB

2HRZE, then 4HRE. In older adults:

- Watch for liver toxicity.
- Dose ethambutol and pyrazinamide for kidney function.
- Give pyridoxine with isoniazid.
- Check rifampicin interactions (warfarin, DOACs and many others).

## Definitions (WHO 2021)

- **RR-TB:** resistant to rifampicin.
- **MDR-TB:** resistant to at least isoniazid and rifampicin.
- **Pre-XDR:** MDR or RR plus fluoroquinolone resistance.
- **XDR:** pre-XDR plus resistance to bedaquiline or linezolid.

## Diagnosis

GeneXpert (MTB/RIF Ultra) or Truenat detects TB and rifampicin resistance. Line probe assays test for isoniazid and fluoroquinolone resistance, and liquid culture DST confirms.

## Treating MDR/RR-TB

- **BPaLM** (bedaquiline, pretomanid, linezolid, moxifloxacin) for 6 months. WHO recommended it in 2022, and India introduced it in 2024. BPaL (without moxifloxacin) is used for pre-XDR.
- A shorter all-oral bedaquiline regimen (9–11 months) where suitable.
- Longer individualised regimens (18–20 months) built from Group A drugs (a fluoroquinolone, bedaquiline, linezolid), then B (clofazimine, cycloserine) and C.

## Monitoring

- ECG for QT prolongation (bedaquiline, moxifloxacin, clofazimine).
- Blood counts, neuropathy and vision checks (linezolid).
- Liver and kidney function.
- In older adults, QT risk, polypharmacy and linezolid toxicity need special care.

## One-liner

Test every TB patient for rifampicin resistance at diagnosis; MDR-TB now takes 6 months with BPaLM, not 2 years.
