## Answer at a glance

1. Causes in the elderly.
2. First steps and risk scores.
3. Resuscitation and drugs.
4. Endoscopy.
5. Afterwards: H. pylori, NSAIDs and restarting antithrombotics.

## Causes in the elderly

Peptic ulcer (NSAIDs, aspirin, H. pylori, anticoagulants), erosive disease, varices, Mallory–Weiss tear, cancer, angiodysplasia.

## First steps

- ABC, two large-bore cannulas.
- Blood count, crossmatch, urea, creatinine, liver function, coagulation.
- Note that beta-blockers can hide a tachycardia.

## Risk scores

Glasgow–Blatchford before endoscopy (a score of 0–1 can be managed as an outpatient), Rockall after.

## Resuscitation

- Restrictive transfusion: haemoglobin threshold 7 g/dL, or 8 g/dL with cardiovascular disease.
- Correct coagulopathy, and reverse anticoagulants for life-threatening bleeds.

## Drugs

- IV PPI.
- **Suspected varices:** terlipressin (or octreotide) plus antibiotics such as ceftriaxone.
- IV erythromycin before endoscopy to clear the stomach.

## Endoscopy

- Within 24 hours, sooner if unstable after resuscitation.
- Dual endoscopic therapy for ulcers; band ligation for varices.
- TIPS, embolisation or surgery if these fail.

## Afterwards

Test for and treat H. pylori; stop NSAIDs; restart aspirin for secondary prevention within days, with a PPI; restart anticoagulants once haemostasis is secure (an individual decision).

## One-liner

Resuscitate first, and let the Glasgow–Blatchford score decide who needs admission.
