## Answer at a glance

## What it is

Aspirating liquid marrow for cytology, usually with a trephine biopsy for architecture and cellularity.

## Indications

- Unexplained cytopenias, or blasts in the blood.
- Suspected leukaemia, MDS, myeloma or lymphoma (staging).
- FUO, with cultures for TB, leishmania and brucella.
- Marrow infiltration; iron stores (the gold standard); monitoring response to treatment.

## Contraindications

Few. Severe uncorrected bleeding disorders, and infection at the site. Thrombocytopenia alone is not a contraindication; pause or adjust anticoagulants.

## Sites

- **Posterior superior iliac spine:** preferred and safest.
- **Anterior iliac crest.**
- **Sternum:** aspiration only. Avoid it in older adults with osteoporosis because of the risk of perforation and tamponade.

## Procedure

1. Consent, lateral position, asepsis, local anaesthetic down to the periosteum.
2. Rotate the needle (with its stylet) through the cortex, then aspirate 0.5–1 mL quickly for smears, and more for flow cytometry, cytogenetics, molecular tests or culture.
3. Spread smears at once, looking for spicules.
4. Take a trephine core of at least 1.5–2 cm.
5. Apply pressure and a dressing.

## Reading it

- Cellularity (roughly 100 minus age, as a percentage).
- M:E ratio (normally about 2–4:1).
- Blasts (under 5% is normal; 20% or more means AML).
- Plasma cells (10% or more clonal supports myeloma).
- Megakaryocytes; iron (Perls’ stain, ring sideroblasts); infiltrates.

## “Dry tap”

Fibrosis, packed marrow (leukaemia, hairy cell leukaemia), metastases or technique, so the trephine decides.

## Complications

Pain, bleeding or haematoma, infection; rarely needle breakage or sternal perforation.

## One-liner

Aspirate for cells, trephine for architecture, and treat a dry tap as a diagnosis waiting for its biopsy.
