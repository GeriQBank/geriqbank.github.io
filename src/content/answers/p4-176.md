## What it is

A structured, multicomponent psychological treatment targeting what keeps insomnia going. It is **first-line for chronic insomnia** in all adults, including older adults (ACP 2016, AASM 2021).

## Components

- **Sleep restriction:**
  - Limit time in bed to the average actual sleep time from a 2-week diary, but not below 5–5.5 hours, with a fixed wake time.
  - Add 15–30 minutes when sleep efficiency exceeds 85–90%; cut back if it falls below 80–85%.
  - In frail older adults, use gentler “sleep compression”.
- **Stimulus control:**
  - Bed only for sleep and sex; go to bed only when sleepy.
  - Get up if awake for about 20 minutes.
  - Same wake time daily; no naps.
- **Cognitive therapy:** challenge unhelpful beliefs (“I must get 8 hours”) and reduce worry and clock-watching; paradoxical intention.
- **Relaxation:** progressive muscle relaxation, breathing, mindfulness.
- **Sleep hygiene** education, which doesn’t work on its own.

## Delivery

- 4–8 weekly sessions, individual or group.
- Brief behavioural treatment (BBTI, about 4 sessions).
- Digital CBT-I apps; nurses can deliver it.

## Evidence

- Faster sleep onset, less waking and better sleep efficiency.
- **Benefits last for months to years,** unlike hypnotics.
- Works alongside pain, depression or cancer, and helps people come off hypnotics.

## Cautions

Sleep restriction causes daytime sleepiness, so take care with falls risk and driving. It can trigger mania in bipolar disorder and seizures in epilepsy. Treat sleep apnoea first.

## One-liner

CBT-I changes what the patient does in bed and thinks about sleep; slower than a pill, but it lasts.
