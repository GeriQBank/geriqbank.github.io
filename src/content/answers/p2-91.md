## Answer at a glance

1. Start for symptoms, not a number.
2. Chronic indications.
3. Acute indications (AEIOU).
4. Older adults: benefit, decision-making and modality.
5. The Indian context.

## Start for symptoms, not a number

The IDEAL trial found no benefit from starting early (eGFR 10–14 vs 5–7). Most start at eGFR 5–10.

## Chronic indications

- Uraemic symptoms: anorexia, vomiting, itch, fatigue, cognitive decline.
- Serositis (pericarditis is urgent), encephalopathy, bleeding.
- Refractory fluid overload or hypertension.
- Refractory hyperkalaemia or acidosis.
- Progressive malnutrition.

## Acute indications (AEIOU)

Acidosis, electrolytes (potassium), intoxications (lithium, methanol, salicylate), overload, and uraemic complications.

## Older adults

- Dialysis often adds little survival over 75–80 with high comorbidity or poor function. In US nursing-home residents, most died or lost function within a year of starting.
- Decide using comprehensive geriatric assessment, prognosis and the person’s values. Offer conservative kidney management, or a time-limited trial of dialysis.
- **Haemodialysis vs peritoneal dialysis:** PD is home-based with less haemodynamic stress, but may need caregiver help (“assisted PD”). Fistulas mature less reliably in older adults.

## India

- Cost and access are the biggest barriers, and many patients drop out.
- The Pradhan Mantri National Dialysis Programme (2016) provides free dialysis for the poor in district hospitals, and added PD in 2019.
- PM-JAY and state schemes (for example Tamil Nadu’s CMCHIS) cover dialysis.
- Late presentation and CKD of unknown cause are common, and transplantation is limited.

## One-liner

Start dialysis for symptoms, not a number; in the frail very old, ask whether it adds life or only hospital days.
