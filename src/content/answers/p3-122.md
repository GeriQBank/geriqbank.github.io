## What it is

Focal, excessive and disorganised bone remodelling. Overactive osteoclasts are followed by chaotic woven bone, in lytic, then mixed, then sclerotic phases.

## Who

Over 55, mostly of European descent; **rare in India**. SQSTM1 mutations; a viral hypothesis.

## Sites

Pelvis, spine, skull, femur, tibia; one bone or many.

## Features

- Often silent: a raised ALP found by chance.
- Bone pain; deformity (bowed femur or tibia, an enlarging skull); warmth over bone; fissure fractures; secondary osteoarthritis.

## Complications

Deafness, cranial nerve compression, basilar invagination, spinal stenosis, rare high-output heart failure, hypercalcaemia on immobilisation, **osteosarcoma (under 1%)**.

## Investigations

- **Raised ALP with normal calcium and phosphate.**
- **X-ray:** cortical thickening, coarse trabeculae, expanded bone, “cotton-wool” skull, osteoporosis circumscripta, “picture-frame” vertebra, a lytic “blade of grass” front.
- **Isotope bone scan** for extent.

## Treatment

- **Zoledronic acid 5 mg IV, once:** the most effective, with long remissions. Oral risedronate is an alternative.
- **Indication:** bone pain from active disease, or before elective surgery on pagetic bone. Treating to normalise ALP alone brought no benefit (PRISM).
- **Replete vitamin D and calcium first** (hypocalcaemia risk).
- Analgesia; orthopaedic surgery for fractures, joint replacement or osteotomy.

## One-liner

A raised ALP with normal calcium and a bowed tibia is Paget’s until proven otherwise; one dose of zoledronic acid, and suspect osteosarcoma if the pain changes.
