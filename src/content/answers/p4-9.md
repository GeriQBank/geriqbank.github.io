## Answer at a glance

## Healthy ageing (WHO 2015)

Developing and maintaining the functional ability that enables well-being in older age. Functional ability is intrinsic capacity plus the environment, and how the two interact.

**UN Decade of Healthy Ageing (2021–2030),** four action areas: age-friendly environments, combating ageism, integrated care, long-term care.

## A life-course approach

Capacity built in youth and kept through middle age sets the ceiling in old age.

## Methods: individual

- **Physical activity (WHO 2020):**
  - 150–300 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity.
  - Muscle strengthening on 2 or more days.
  - Balance-focused multicomponent activity on 3 or more days.
- **Nutrition:** enough protein (1.0–1.2 g/kg/day), fruit and vegetables, less salt and sugar, calcium and vitamin D, fluids.
- **Habits:** no tobacco, little or no alcohol, good sleep, managing stress.
- **Mind and purpose:** learning, hobbies, social contact, volunteering.

## Methods: health care

- Regular checks of blood pressure, glucose, lipids and weight; individualised cancer screening.
- Vision and hearing correction; oral health.
- Bone health and falls prevention; medication review and deprescribing; depression screening.
- WHO ICOPE screening of intrinsic capacity: cognition, mobility, nutrition, vision, hearing, mood.

## Methods: environment and society

- Age-friendly housing, transport and cities.
- Income security and pensions; caregiver support; protection from elder abuse.
- **India:** NPHCE, National Policy on Older Persons, the Maintenance Act 2007, PM-JAY for everyone 70 and over, Elderline 14567.

## Special note: vaccination

- **Why:** immunosenescence makes infections more frequent and severe, and an infection often costs function.
- **Schedule:**
  - Influenza every year (high-dose or adjuvanted from 65 where available).
  - Pneumococcal once (PCV20/21 alone, or PCV13/15 then PPSV23 a year later).
  - Recombinant zoster: 2 doses, 2–6 months apart, from 50.
  - Tdap once, then Td every 10 years and for tetanus-prone wounds.
  - RSV: one dose at 75 and over, or from 60 if at higher risk.
  - COVID-19 per national guidance.
  - By risk: hepatitis B, hepatitis A, typhoid.
- **Principles:** vaccinate at every contact, give several vaccines on one day, keep a vaccine card, no live vaccines if immunocompromised.
- **India:** there’s no national adult programme, and uptake is low. Cost, awareness and doctors not recommending vaccines are the barriers.

## One-liner

Healthy ageing is built over a lifetime: keep moving, eat well, stay connected, correct the senses, and vaccinate.
