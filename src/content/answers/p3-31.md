## Answer at a glance

1. State the purpose of testing.
2. Brief screens, then standard tests.
3. Tools for low literacy and Indian settings.
4. Informant and staging tools.
5. Screen for delirium first, and know the pitfalls.

## Purpose

Screening, supporting diagnosis, staging and monitoring. Choose the tool by setting, time available, education and language.

## Brief screens (under 5 minutes)

- **Mini-Cog:** 3-word recall plus a clock; below 3 of 5 is abnormal.
- **Abbreviated Mental Test:** 10 items; below 8 is abnormal.
- **Others:** clock drawing, 6CIT, GPCOG.

## Standard tests

- **MMSE:** 30 points; below 24 is typical. It misses MCI and executive deficits and is education-biased.
- **MoCA:** 30 points; below 26 is abnormal, with 1 point added for 12 or fewer years of education. Better for MCI and executive function.
- **ACE-III:** 100 points, with cut-offs of 88 and 82.

## Low literacy and Indian settings

Hindi Mental State Examination, RUDAS.

## Informant and staging tools

- **Informant:** AD8 (2 or more is abnormal), IQCODE.
- **Staging:** Clinical Dementia Rating (0–3), Global Deterioration Scale and FAST (1–7).

## Screen for delirium first

Use the 4AT or CAM before interpreting any cognitive score.

## Pitfalls

Poor vision or hearing, low education, language, depression and delirium all lower scores. Use formal neuropsychological testing when screens conflict.

## One-liner

Screening tests only screen; dementia is diagnosed by cognitive decline plus loss of function.
