## Answer at a glance

2. Pattern.
3. Older adults: exclude secondary causes.

## What it is

A trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia.

## Features (ICHD-3)

- Strictly one-sided, severe orbital or temporal pain lasting 15–180 minutes, from every other day up to 8 times a day.
- Same-side autonomic features: tearing, red eye, runny or blocked nose, eyelid swelling, ptosis and miosis, sweating.
- Restlessness during attacks.

## Pattern

Commoner in men, usually starting at 20–40. Attacks come in bouts with clockwork timing and are triggered by alcohol during a bout. It is episodic in about 90% and chronic in the rest.

## In older adults

A new headache after 50 needs secondary causes excluded first: GCA, pituitary tumour, carotid dissection, cavernous sinus lesions, glaucoma. Get an MRI (with pituitary views) and ESR/CRP.

## Acute treatment

- **100% oxygen** at 12–15 L/min by non-rebreather mask for 15–20 minutes. It’s the safest option in older adults, unless they retain CO₂.
- **Subcutaneous sumatriptan 6 mg**, or intranasal zolmitriptan. Triptans are contraindicated with coronary disease, stroke or uncontrolled hypertension, which are common in older adults.

## Bridging treatment

Greater occipital nerve block, or a short course of prednisolone.

## Prevention

- **Verapamil**, with ECG monitoring for heart block, which is important in older adults.
- Lithium (kidney, thyroid and toxicity monitoring), topiramate, galcanezumab.

## Refractory cases

Occipital nerve stimulation, non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation.

## One-liner

Oxygen is the safest cluster treatment in older adults, and a new cluster pattern after 50 needs an MRI and an ESR first.
