## Answer at a glance

1. Define and stage AKI (KDIGO).
2. Why older adults are vulnerable.
3. Causes: prerenal, renal, postrenal.
4. Evaluation.
5. Management.

## Definition (KDIGO)

Creatinine rise of 0.3 mg/dL or more within 48 hours, or 1.5 times baseline within 7 days, or urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours. Staged 1–3.

## Why older adults

Less renal reserve, more drugs, dehydration, and low muscle mass, which makes creatinine underestimate the injury.

## Causes

- **Prerenal:** dehydration, diarrhoea, sepsis, heart failure, and the “triple whammy” of diuretic plus ACE inhibitor or ARB plus NSAID.
- **Renal:** acute tubular necrosis (sepsis, contrast, aminoglycosides); interstitial nephritis (PPIs, antibiotics, NSAIDs); glomerulonephritis or ANCA vasculitis; myeloma; rhabdomyolysis; atheroemboli after angiography.
- **Postrenal:** in an older man, prostatic obstruction is a leading cause; also stones and tumours.

## Evaluation

- Drug chart and volume status.
- Bladder scan or catheter.
- Urine dipstick and microscopy (casts, blood, protein).
- Potassium, acid–base, CK, calcium.
- Serum electrophoresis and free light chains for myeloma; ANCA and complement if nephritic.
- Renal ultrasound for size and hydronephrosis.

## Management

- Treat the cause; give fluids carefully if hypovolaemic.
- Stop nephrotoxins and pause ACE inhibitors, ARBs, metformin and SGLT2 inhibitors.
- Catheterise obstruction and adjust drug doses.
- Manage hyperkalaemia, acidosis and overload.
- Dialysis for refractory acidosis, potassium, poisoning, overload or uraemia.
- Follow up for later CKD.

## One-liner

In an older man with AKI, scan the bladder first.
