## Answer at a glance

1. Definition.
2. Evidence from animals and humans.
3. Mechanisms, mimetics and alternatives.
4. Caution in older adults.

## Definition

Eating 20–40% fewer calories than usual, without malnutrition.

## Evidence

- It extends lifespan in yeast, worms, flies and rodents, first shown in rats by McCay (1935).
- In monkeys, survival results were mixed (Wisconsin vs NIA), but both studies showed healthier ageing.
- In humans, CALERIE 2 (about 12% restriction for 2 years) improved cardiometabolic risk and slowed a DNA-methylation measure of the pace of ageing. Okinawans offer observational support.

## Mechanisms

Lower insulin/IGF-1 signalling, mTOR inhibition, AMPK and sirtuin activation, more autophagy, and less oxidative stress and inflammation.

## Mimetics and alternatives

Rapamycin, metformin, spermidine and resveratrol (the evidence is weak), plus intermittent fasting and time-restricted eating.

## In older adults

Be cautious, because it risks sarcopenia, bone loss, frailty and malnutrition. It isn’t advised for frail older people. When an obese older adult needs weight loss, combine a modest deficit with exercise and enough protein.

## One-liner

Calorie restriction slows ageing in mice; in frail older people, it mostly speeds up sarcopenia.
