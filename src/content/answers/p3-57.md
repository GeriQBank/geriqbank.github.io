## Burden and physiology

Most people over 70 have hypertension.

- Arterial stiffness causes isolated systolic hypertension and a wide pulse pressure.
- Other features: low renin, salt sensitivity, impaired baroreflexes (postural drops, variability), declining kidney function, white-coat effect.

## Evidence for treating

- SHEP, Syst-Eur.
- **HYVET (80 and over):** fewer strokes, heart failure and deaths.
- SPRINT (75 and over subgroup) and STEP (60–80): intensive targets cut cardiovascular events.

## Measure properly

Both arms, seated and **standing (at 1 and 3 minutes)**, and home or ambulatory readings to catch white-coat and masked hypertension.

## Evaluate

- Risk and organ damage: ECG, echo, eGFR, urine ACR, fundi.
- **Secondary causes:** atherosclerotic renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, sleep apnoea, drugs (NSAIDs, steroids, decongestants, venlafaxine), thyroid.
- Frailty and cognition (CGA).

## Targets (ESC 2024)

- Systolic **120–129 if tolerated**.
- At 85 or over, or if frail, treat from 140/90 and aim **as low as reasonably achievable**.
- Avoid symptomatic postural drops, and diastolic below about 60–70 in coronary disease.

## Non-drug measures

Salt under 5 g/day, potassium-rich diet (salt substitutes reduced stroke in SSaSS, but not in CKD or hyperkalaemia), DASH diet, weight, exercise, less alcohol, no smoking.

## Drugs

- **First line:** thiazide-like diuretic (indapamide, chlorthalidone), dihydropyridine CCB (amlodipine), or ACE inhibitor or ARB. Single-pill combinations help adherence; **start with one drug at a low dose if 85 or over, or frail**.
- **Compelling indications:**
  - Heart failure: the four pillars.
  - CKD with albuminuria: ACE inhibitor or ARB, plus SGLT2 inhibitor.
  - After MI: beta-blocker, ACE inhibitor.
  - AF: rate-controlling drugs.
- **Resistant hypertension:** check adherence and white coat, then add spironolactone (PATHWAY-2).
- **Elderly-specific harms:**
  - Thiazides: low sodium and potassium, gout, incontinence.
  - CCBs: ankle oedema.
  - ACE inhibitors and ARBs: hyperkalaemia, AKI.
  - **Avoid alpha-blockers as first line, and central agents** (postural drops, falls; Beers).

## Special situations

- Postural hypotension with supine hypertension: treat cautiously.
- Dementia risk: blood pressure lowering reduced MCI (SPRINT MIND).
- After stroke: aim below 130/80.

## Follow-up

Standing BP; electrolytes and creatinine 2–4 weeks after diuretic or RAAS changes. **Deprescribe** if BP falls low or frailty advances.

## One-liner

Treat blood pressure in the elderly, even over 80, but measure it standing, start low, and let frailty set the floor.
