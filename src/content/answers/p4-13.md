## Answer at a glance

## Definition

A multidimensional, interdisciplinary process that identifies a frail older person’s medical, psychological and functional problems and abilities, in order to build a coordinated plan for treatment and follow-up.

## Who needs it

Frail older adults; multimorbidity or geriatric syndromes (falls, delirium, incontinence, weight loss); recent decline; before major surgery or chemotherapy (G8 screening in oncology); admission to hospital. It adds little for the very fit or the imminently dying.

## Domains

- **Medical:** problem list, comorbidity (CIRS-G, Charlson), medication review (Beers, STOPP/START), nutrition (MNA), continence, vision, hearing, teeth, pain, sleep.
- **Functional:** ADL (Katz, Barthel), IADL (Lawton), gait and balance (Timed Up and Go, gait speed, SPPB), falls.
- **Psychological:** cognition (Mini-Cog, MMSE, MoCA), mood (GDS-15), delirium (4AT, CAM).
- **Social:** living situation, caregiver, finances, elder abuse screen, advance directives.
- **Environment:** home safety and access.
- **Values:** goals, preferences, spiritual needs.

## Process

Screening → assessment by the team (geriatrician, nurse, physiotherapist, occupational therapist, dietitian, social worker, pharmacist) → team meeting → agreed plan → carrying it out → review.

## Settings

Geriatric wards, consultation teams, clinics, the home, perioperative services, geriatric oncology.

## Evidence

CGA on a dedicated hospital ward makes older patients more likely to be alive and at home 3–12 months later, and less likely to need a care home (Cochrane 2017). Advice-only consultation without follow-through helps less.

## Usefulness

It finds hidden problems, cuts inappropriate drugs, improves function, reduces delirium, guides surgery and chemotherapy decisions, clarifies goals of care, and reduces readmissions and costs.

## Limitations

It needs time, a trained team and follow-through.

## One-liner

CGA turns a list of diagnoses into a plan for a person.
