## Answer at a glance

1. Principles.
2. Assessment.
3. Rehabilitation by domain.
4. After discharge.
5. Older adults.

## Principles

- **Organised stroke unit care** reduces death and dependency.
- Start early once stable, but **avoid very early, high-dose mobilisation within 24 hours** (harm in AVERT).
- Interdisciplinary goal-setting; more therapy means better recovery; **task-specific, repetitive practice**.

## Assessment

- NIHSS for severity.
- **Swallow screen before anything by mouth.**
- Mobility, cognition, mood, communication, continence, skin.
- Track outcomes with Barthel or mRS.

## Domains

- **Motor:**
  - Task-specific training.
  - **Constraint-induced movement therapy** if some wrist and finger extension remains.
  - Mirror therapy; functional electrical stimulation; robotic and treadmill gait training.
- **Spasticity:** positioning, stretching, splints, botulinum toxin. Protect the shoulder (supports; never pull the arm).
- **Dysphagia:** SLT assessment, texture modification, videofluoroscopy or FEES; NG or PEG feeding if needed.
- **Communication:** aphasia and dysarthria therapy.
- **Cognition and perception:** neglect (scanning training), apraxia, memory.
- **Mood:** post-stroke depression affects about a third. Screen, then treat with SSRIs and psychological support.
- **Others:** continence, nutrition, fatigue, sleep, central post-stroke pain, falls prevention.
- **Secondary prevention:** see the [TIA answer](../../paper-2/174/).

## After discharge

Early supported discharge, home modification, carer training, advice on driving and work, community rehabilitation, telerehabilitation.

## Older adults

Comorbidity, frailty and pre-stroke dementia still allow benefit, with realistic goals. Most recovery happens in 3–6 months, but gains continue beyond.

## One-liner

Stroke rehab works through repetition and teamwork; screen the swallow first, mobilise early but not too early, and keep practising real tasks.
