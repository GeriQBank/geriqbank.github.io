## Answer at a glance

1. Principles: CGA-based assessment, surgery within 36 hours, orthogeriatric care.
2. Before surgery: baseline, heart, COPD, diabetes, blood pressure, antithrombotics, bloods, analgesia.
3. During surgery.
4. After surgery.
5. Follow-up: bone health, falls and rehabilitation.

## Principles

Assessment based on comprehensive geriatric assessment: frailty, cognition, function, nutrition, polypharmacy, goals and consent (capacity). Surgery within 36 hours, with orthogeriatric co-management.

## Before surgery

- **Baseline:** cognition (4AT or AMTS) and delirium risk; function; why he fell (syncope? drugs?).
- **Heart:** ECG and RCRI. Don’t delay for a routine echo unless there’s a murmur or heart failure is suspected.
- **COPD:**
  - Optimise bronchodilators and exclude an exacerbation or infection.
  - Blood gas if severe; chest X-ray; incentive spirometry; stop smoking.
  - Prefer spinal anaesthesia.
- **Diabetes:**
  - HbA1c; keep glucose around 6–10 mmol/L (108–180 mg/dL).
  - **Stop SGLT2 inhibitors 3 days before** (4 days for ertugliflozin), because of DKA risk.
  - Hold metformin on the day.
  - Reduce basal insulin by about 20%, or use a variable-rate insulin infusion.
- **Hypertension:** continue beta-blockers and hold ACE inhibitors or ARBs on the morning of surgery. Watch for hypotension.
- **Antithrombotics:** continue aspirin; clopidogrel doesn’t delay surgery; reverse warfarin with vitamin K (or PCC); time DOACs by the last dose and renal function.
- **Bloods:** blood count, electrolytes, glucose, coagulation, group and save. Correct anaemia (IV iron), sodium and potassium.
- **Analgesia:** an early fascia iliaca or femoral nerve block, regular paracetamol, cautious opioids, no NSAIDs.
- **Also:** fluids, nutrition, pressure-area care, and avoid catheters.

## During surgery

Spinal and general anaesthesia give similar outcomes (REGAIN), but spinal suits COPD. Avoid hypotension, and watch for bone cement implantation syndrome.

## After surgery

- **Delirium prevention bundle:** orientation, glasses and hearing aids, sleep, hydration, and avoiding deliriogenic drugs.
- Mobilise on day 1 with full weight-bearing.
- Pain, bowel and bladder care (remove the catheter early), nutritional supplements.
- **COPD:** bronchodilators, chest physiotherapy, oxygen target 88–92% if he retains CO₂.
- **Diabetes:** restart oral agents once eating (SGLT2 inhibitors once eating and drinking normally). Restart antihypertensives as blood pressure allows.
- LMWH prophylaxis for about a month.
- Watch for MI, pneumonia, AKI and UTI.

## Follow-up

- **Secondary fracture prevention:** vitamin D and calcium, then zoledronic acid from 2 weeks after surgery.
- Falls assessment, rehabilitation and a home visit.
- Medication review and advance care planning.

## One-liner

Operate within 36 hours, block the nerve early, and start bone and falls care before discharge.
