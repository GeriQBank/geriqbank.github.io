## Answer at a glance

1. Symptomatic drugs.
2. Anti-amyloid antibodies: benefit, eligibility and risks.
3. Other newer drugs and the pipeline.
4. What goes alongside drugs.

## Symptomatic drugs

- **Cholinesterase inhibitors** (donepezil, rivastigmine oral or patch, galantamine) for mild to moderate disease, and donepezil in severe disease too. Watch for nausea, weight loss, bradycardia, syncope and vivid dreams; check pulse and ECG.
- **Memantine** for moderate to severe disease, alone or with donepezil.
- These help modestly but don’t stop progression.

## Anti-amyloid antibodies

- **Lecanemab** (CLARITY-AD): about 27% slower decline over 18 months; infusions every 2 weeks.
- **Donanemab** (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2): about 35% slower decline with low to intermediate tau; monthly infusions, stopped once amyloid clears.
- Aducanumab was withdrawn in 2024.
- **For whom:** early disease (MCI or mild dementia) with confirmed amyloid.
- **Risk:** ARIA (brain swelling or microbleeds), highest in APOE ε4 homozygotes. It needs MRI monitoring and caution with anticoagulants.
- Availability and cost limit their use in India.

## Other newer drugs

Brexpiprazole for agitation in Alzheimer’s dementia; suvorexant for insomnia.

## Pipeline

Drugs against tau, inflammation and synapse loss; subcutaneous and blood-test-guided anti-amyloid treatment.

## Alongside drugs

Control vascular risk, treat depression and hearing loss, stop anticholinergics, and support caregivers. Non-drug care remains the foundation.

## One-liner

Anti-amyloid antibodies slow early Alzheimer’s by roughly a quarter to a third, at the cost of infusions, MRI scans and the risk of ARIA.
