## Answer at a glance

1. What it is.
2. Clinical features.
3. Investigations.
4. Same-day steroids, then taper.
5. Complications.

## What it is

Granulomatous vasculitis of the aorta and its branches, in people over 50.

## Features

- New temporal headache, scalp tenderness, and jaw claudication (the most specific sign).
- **Visual symptoms:** amaurosis, diplopia, sudden loss from arteritic ischaemic optic neuropathy.
- Constitutional symptoms; PMR in 40–50%.
- **Large-vessel disease:** limb claudication, bruits, aortic aneurysm.

## Investigations

- ESR and CRP (CRP is more sensitive); high platelets.
- Temporal artery ultrasound showing the “halo” sign, first line in fast-track clinics.
- Biopsy of at least 1 cm, within 2 weeks of starting steroids.
- PET-CT or angiography for the large vessels.

## Treatment

- **Start steroids the same day on suspicion**; don’t wait for the biopsy.
- Prednisolone 40–60 mg a day. With visual loss, IV methylprednisolone 500–1,000 mg a day for 3 days.
- Taper over 12–18 months; tocilizumab spares steroids.
- Bone protection; screen for aortic aneurysm later.

## Complications

Permanent visual loss (15–20%), stroke, aortic aneurysm or dissection.

## One-liner

Suspect GCA and start steroids today, because the eye can’t wait for the biopsy.
