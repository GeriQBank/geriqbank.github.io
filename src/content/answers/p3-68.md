## Answer at a glance

1. Definition.
2. Causes, intrinsic and extrinsic.
3. Features.
4. Diagnosis.
5. Management, including pacing.

## Definition

Sinus node dysfunction. It includes:

- Sinus bradycardia, pauses or arrest.
- Sinoatrial exit block.
- Chronotropic incompetence.
- **Tachy-brady syndrome:** paroxysmal AF or flutter alternating with bradycardia.

Mainly a disease of the elderly.

## Causes

- **Intrinsic:** degenerative fibrosis of the sinus node (commonest), ischaemia, amyloid, sarcoid, cardiac surgery.
- **Extrinsic:**
  - Drugs: beta-blockers, verapamil and diltiazem, digoxin, amiodarone, ivabradine, **cholinesterase inhibitors**, lithium, clonidine.
  - Other: hypothyroidism, hyperkalaemia, hypothermia, sleep apnoea, high vagal tone.

## Features

Dizziness, presyncope, syncope, **falls**, confusion, fatigue, exercise intolerance, palpitations; strokes from AF. Symptoms are intermittent.

## Diagnosis

- Match symptoms to rhythm: ECG, Holter, event recorder, or an implantable loop recorder for rare episodes.
- Exercise test for chronotropic incompetence.
- Exclude drugs, thyroid and electrolyte causes.

## Management

- Stop or reduce culprit drugs; treat hypothyroidism.
- **Permanent pacemaker for symptomatic sinus node dysfunction:** dual-chamber pacing that minimises ventricular pacing (DANPACE).
- **Tachy-brady:** pacing makes rate-control drugs safe. **Anticoagulate by CHA₂DS₂-VA.**
- Asymptomatic bradycardia (sleep, athletes) needs no pacing.
- Acute symptomatic bradycardia: atropine, adrenaline or isoprenaline infusion, temporary pacing.

## One-liner

Dizzy spells plus pauses on the Holter: stop the culprit drugs, then pace, and remember the “tachy” half needs anticoagulation.
