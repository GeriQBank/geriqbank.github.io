## Answer at a glance

1. Is he unstable? Cardiovert at once if so.
2. Classify the ECG: narrow or broad, regular or irregular.
3. Investigations.
4. Treatment by rhythm.
5. Points specific to older adults.

## Is he unstable?

- ABCDE, monitor, 12-lead ECG, IV access.
- Adverse features: shock (systolic below 90), syncope, myocardial ischaemia, severe heart failure.
- If any are present: synchronised DC cardioversion under sedation (up to 3 attempts), then amiodarone 300 mg IV if it fails.

## Classify the ECG

- **Narrow (QRS under 120 ms) and regular:**
  - Sinus tachycardia, AVNRT, AVRT, atrial tachycardia.
  - **Atrial flutter with 2:1 block:** a rate of exactly 150 is the classic clue. Look for sawtooth waves in II, III and aVF; vagal manoeuvres or adenosine unmask them.
- **Narrow and irregular:**
  - AF: no P waves, irregularly irregular.
  - Flutter with variable block.
  - Multifocal atrial tachycardia: 3 or more P-wave shapes, typical in COPD.
- **Broad and regular:** **VT until proven otherwise** in an older man with heart disease.
  - Clues: AV dissociation, capture or fusion beats, QRS over 160 ms, concordance, extreme axis.
  - Alternatives: SVT with bundle branch block or aberrancy.
- **Broad and irregular:** AF with bundle branch block, pre-excited AF (WPW), polymorphic VT or torsades.

## Investigations

Potassium, magnesium, calcium, TSH, troponin, blood count, renal function; digoxin level if on it; echo; Holter later.

## Stable narrow regular tachycardia

- Modified Valsalva (strain, then lie flat with legs raised; REVERT). Avoid carotid massage if there’s a bruit.
- Adenosine 6 mg, then 12 mg, then 18 mg rapid IV (not in asthma; use verapamil or diltiazem instead).
- Recurrent: catheter ablation.

## AF or flutter

- **Rate control:**
  - Beta-blocker, or diltiazem/verapamil if there’s no HFrEF.
  - Digoxin in heart failure or sedentary patients.
  - Amiodarone if compromised.
- **Rhythm control** if onset was under 48 hours, or after 3 weeks of anticoagulation or a TOE.
- **Anticoagulate** by CHA₂DS₂-VA (ESC 2024) with a DOAC. Falls alone aren’t a reason to withhold it.
- **Treat triggers:** sepsis, thyrotoxicosis, electrolytes, PE, alcohol.

## Stable broad regular tachycardia (VT)

- Amiodarone 300 mg IV over 10–60 minutes, or synchronised cardioversion.
- Correct potassium and magnesium; look for ischaemia; consider an ICD.

## Torsades

IV magnesium 2 g; stop QT-prolonging drugs; correct potassium; consider overdrive pacing.

## Pre-excited AF

**No AV-nodal blockers** (adenosine, digoxin, verapamil, beta-blockers); use cardioversion or procainamide.

## Older adults

- Tachy-brady (sick sinus) syndrome may need a pacemaker before rate control.
- Digoxin toxicity causes atrial tachycardia with block.
- Dose drugs for the kidneys; giddiness means falls risk.

## One-liner

A regular narrow tachycardia at exactly 150 in an older person is flutter until proven otherwise, and a broad one is VT.
