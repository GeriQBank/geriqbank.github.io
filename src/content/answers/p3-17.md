## Answer at a glance

1. Why gait matters.
2. History.
3. What to observe, and the patterns to recognise.
4. Standard tests.
5. Look for causes and refer.

## Why it matters

Slow gait predicts falls, disability and death. Gait speed is often called the sixth vital sign.

## History

Falls and near-falls, fear of falling, dizziness, pain, footwear, walking aids, drugs.

## What to observe

Gait initiation, step length and height, base width, symmetry, path deviation, turning, arm swing, and dual-tasking (walking while talking).

## Patterns to recognise

- **Parkinsonian:** shuffling, reduced arm swing, freezing.
- **Frontal (“magnetic”):** as in normal pressure hydrocephalus.
- **Hemiparetic:** circumduction.
- **Sensory ataxic:** wide-based and stamping, with Romberg positive.
- **Cerebellar.**
- **Steppage:** foot drop.
- **Waddling:** proximal myopathy.
- **Antalgic.**
- **Cautious.**

## Tests

- **Gait speed:** below 0.8 m/s is poor.
- **Timed Up and Go:** 13.5 s or more suggests fall risk.
- Five times sit-to-stand, Romberg, single-leg stance (under 5 s is abnormal).
- **Tinetti POMA:** out of 28; below 19 is high risk.
- **Berg Balance Scale:** out of 56; below 45 is high risk.
- Short Physical Performance Battery.

## Then

Look for causes (vision, vestibular disease, neuropathy, arthritis, drugs, orthostatic hypotension) and refer to physiotherapy.

## One-liner

Time the walk; a slow gait is a warning in itself.
