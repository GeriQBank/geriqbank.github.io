## Hospice

Holistic, comfort-focused care for people in the last phase of a life-limiting illness, and their families. It can be at home, in a hospice or in hospital.

- **Origin:** the modern hospice movement began with Dame Cicely Saunders at St Christopher’s Hospice, London, in 1967. She also described “total pain”: physical, psychological, social and spiritual.
- **Team:** doctors, nurses, social workers, counsellors, chaplains and volunteers.
- **Care:** symptom control, psychosocial and spiritual support, and bereavement care for the family.

## Hospice vs palliative care

Palliative care can start at diagnosis, alongside curative treatment; hospice is palliative care at the end of life, once cure is no longer the aim.

## Hospice care in India

Shanti Avedna Sadan (Mumbai, 1986) was the first hospice. Other examples are CanSupport’s home care (Delhi), Karunashraya (Bengaluru), and Kerala’s community palliative care network. The National Programme for Palliative Care started in 2012, and the 2014 NDPS amendment eased access to oral morphine.

## Respite care

Short-term care of the dependent person, to give the family caregiver a break.

- **Forms:** in-home sitters, adult day care, short residential stays (days to weeks), and emergency respite.
- **Benefits:** less caregiver burnout, time for the caregiver’s own health, and delayed institutional care.
- **Barriers:** few services, cost, and caregivers’ guilt.
- **India:** limited; mainly day-care centres under Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana and dementia day care run by ARDSI.

## One-liner

Hospice cares for the patient at the end; respite cares for the carer along the way.
