## Answer at a glance

1. Older women: indications, timing, regimen, risks, contraindications.
2. Older men: testosterone, only for confirmed deficiency.
3. What not to use for ageing itself.

## Older women: menopausal hormone therapy

- **Indications:** troublesome hot flushes; genitourinary syndrome of menopause, for which low-dose vaginal oestrogen is preferred; fracture prevention in women under 60, or within 10 years of menopause, who can’t take other drugs.
- **Timing:** benefits outweigh risks under 60 or within 10 years of menopause. Starting systemic therapy later raises the risk of coronary events, stroke, venous thromboembolism and dementia.
- **Regimen:** oestrogen alone after hysterectomy; add a progestogen if the uterus is intact. Transdermal oestradiol carries less VTE and stroke risk. Use the lowest effective dose and review yearly.
- **Risks:** VTE, stroke, breast cancer (combined therapy, rising with duration), endometrial cancer if oestrogen is unopposed, gallbladder disease.
- **Contraindications:** breast or endometrial cancer, previous VTE or stroke, coronary disease, active liver disease, undiagnosed vaginal bleeding.
- Not for preventing heart disease or dementia.

## Older men: testosterone

- Only for symptoms plus two low morning total testosterone levels. The benefits are modest: sexual function, anaemia, bone density.
- Monitor haematocrit (stop above 54%) and PSA.
- Avoid in prostate or breast cancer, high haematocrit, untreated severe sleep apnoea and uncontrolled heart failure.
- TRAVERSE (2023) found no excess of major cardiovascular events.

## Not for ageing itself

Growth hormone and DHEA.

## One-liner

Low-dose vaginal oestrogen suits most older women with genitourinary symptoms, even when systemic therapy doesn’t.
