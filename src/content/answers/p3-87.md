## Answer at a glance

1. Burden.
2. Transient causes (DIAPPERS), then established types.
3. Evaluation.
4. Management by type.
5. Complications.

## Burden

Common (a third or more of older women), under-reported, and a “geriatric giant”.

## Transient causes (DIAPPERS)

- Delirium, infection, atrophic vaginitis.
- Pharmaceuticals: diuretics, anticholinergics, sedatives, opioids, alpha-blockers in women.
- Psychological (depression), excess urine output (diabetes, hypercalcaemia, heart failure).
- Restricted mobility, stool impaction.

## Established types

- **Urge:** overactive bladder.
- **Stress:** urethral hypermobility or sphincter weakness; in women, or men after prostatectomy.
- **Mixed.**
- **Overflow:** BPH, stricture, or a weak detrusor from diabetes or drugs.
- **Functional:** mobility or cognition barriers.

## Evaluation

- History: type, triggers, volume, nocturia, fluids, drugs.
- A 3-day bladder diary.
- Examination: prolapse, atrophy, cough stress test, rectal exam (prostate, impaction, tone), neurology, cognition and mobility.
- Urinalysis; post-void residual by bladder scan (over 200 mL is significant); renal function, glucose and calcium as needed.
- Urodynamics before surgery or when unclear.

## Management

Treat transient causes first.

- **Urge:** bladder training and pelvic floor exercises; adjust fluids and caffeine.
  - Beta-3 agonists (mirabegron, vibegron) are preferred in older adults, as they carry no anticholinergic burden.
  - Use antimuscarinics cautiously, trospium if needed.
  - Vaginal oestrogen.
  - Refractory: bladder botulinum toxin (risk of retention) or tibial or sacral nerve stimulation.
- **Stress:** supervised pelvic floor training for at least 3 months, weight loss, a pessary, vaginal oestrogen. Surgery: mid-urethral sling, colposuspension, bulking agents. For men, an artificial sphincter or male sling.
- **Overflow:** relieve obstruction (alpha-blocker, 5-alpha reductase inhibitor, surgery), clean intermittent self-catheterisation, and an indwelling catheter only as a last resort.
- **Functional:** prompted voiding, toilet access, commodes, easy clothing.

## Complications

Skin breakdown, falls from nocturia and urgency, depression, isolation, institutional care.
