## Definition

A transient episode of neurological dysfunction from focal brain, spinal cord or retinal ischaemia, **without infarction** on imaging. Most resolve within an hour.

## Why urgent

The risk of stroke is highest in the first 48 hours, so treat every suspected TIA as an emergency.

## Features

Sudden focal deficits: weakness, speech disturbance, amaurosis fugax, or brainstem symptoms (vertigo with other signs, diplopia).

## Mimics in older adults

Syncope, seizures (Todd’s paresis), migraine aura, hypoglycaemia, subdural haematoma, tumour, peripheral vertigo, transient global amnesia.

## Triage

ABCD2 (age, blood pressure, clinical features, duration, diabetes) is no longer used for triage. NICE advises seeing everyone within 24 hours.

## Investigations

- Urgent MRI with DWI (or CT).
- Carotid imaging (Doppler, CTA or MRA).
- ECG, plus prolonged monitoring for AF (7-day Holter or loop recorder).
- Echo.
- Glucose, lipids, blood count, coagulation, and ESR if GCA is possible.

## Management

- **Aspirin 300 mg immediately.** For high-risk TIA or minor stroke, give aspirin plus clopidogrel for 21 days (CHANCE, POINT), or ticagrelor plus aspirin (THALES).
- **AF:** start a DOAC early.
- **Symptomatic carotid stenosis of 50–99%:** endarterectomy within 2 weeks.
- **High-intensity statin,** targeting LDL below 70 mg/dL (Treat Stroke to Target).
- **Blood pressure below 130/80** after the acute phase.
- Diabetes control, stopping smoking, and driving advice.

## One-liner

A TIA is a warning shot; see it within 24 hours, image the brain and carotids, and start the antiplatelet today.
