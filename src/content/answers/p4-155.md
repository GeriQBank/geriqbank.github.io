## Why vaccinate

Immunosenescence makes infections more common and more severe. Vaccines prevent hospital stays, disability and deaths, including the loss of function that follows an infection.

## Schedule for a fit adult aged 60 and over

- **Influenza:** every year, before the local flu season. High-dose or adjuvanted versions are preferred from 65 where available; high-dose cut confirmed influenza by about a quarter compared with standard dose.
- **Pneumococcal:** once. Either a 20- or 21-valent conjugate vaccine alone, or PCV13/PCV15 followed by PPSV23 at least a year later. CAPiTA showed PCV13 prevented vaccine-type pneumonia in people over 65. The US now advises it from 50.
- **Herpes zoster:** the recombinant (non-live) vaccine, 2 doses 2–6 months apart from 50. It is over 90% effective, even over 70.
- **Tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis:** one Tdap, then Td or Tdap every 10 years and for tetanus-prone wounds.
- **RSV:** one dose for everyone aged 75 and over, and from 60 (50 in the US) for those at higher risk, where available.
- **COVID-19:** updated doses per current national guidance.
- **By risk:** hepatitis B (diabetes, dialysis, health workers), hepatitis A, typhoid, rabies.

## Principles

Vaccinate at every opportunity; several vaccines can be given the same day; keep a vaccine card; avoid live vaccines in the immunocompromised; vaccinate household contacts too.

## Status in India

There’s no national immunisation programme for older adults, and uptake is very low in national surveys. The barriers are awareness, cost, and doctors not recommending vaccines. Professional bodies such as the Association of Physicians of India publish adult schedules.

## One-liner

Every older adult needs at least four: influenza every year, pneumococcal once, zoster twice, and tetanus every ten years.
