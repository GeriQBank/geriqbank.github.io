## Answer at a glance

1. Older human insulins, for comparison.
2. Rapid-acting analogues.
3. Long-acting (basal) analogues.
4. Premixed analogues and combinations.
5. Advantages, disadvantages, and use in older adults.

## Older human insulins, for comparison

- **Regular (soluble):** onset 30–60 minutes, peak 2–4 hours, lasts 6–8 hours. It must be taken 30 minutes before meals.
- **NPH:** onset 1–2 hours, peak 4–8 hours, lasts 12–16 hours. Its peak causes night-time hypoglycaemia.

## Rapid-acting analogues

Lispro, aspart and glulisine: onset 10–15 minutes, peak 1–2 hours, last 3–5 hours. Faster formulations of aspart and lispro act even sooner. They can be given right before a meal, or just after it in older adults with unpredictable eating, dosing by what was eaten.

## Long-acting (basal) analogues

- **Glargine U100:** onset 1–2 hours, no marked peak, about 24 hours.
- **Detemir:** 12–24 hours, dose-dependent; often twice daily.
- **Glargine U300:** flatter, up to about 36 hours.
- **Degludec:** over 42 hours, with flexible timing.
- **Icodec:** once weekly, approved in some countries.

## Premixed analogues and combinations

Biphasic aspart 30/70, lispro 25/75 and 50/50, and a degludec-aspart co-formulation. There are also fixed combinations of a basal insulin with a GLP-1 receptor agonist.

## Advantages over older insulins

- More physiological profiles and flexible meal timing.
- **Less hypoglycaemia**, especially at night and severe episodes (degludec and glargine vs NPH).
- Less weight gain with detemir.
- Simple once-daily basal regimens, and easy pen devices.

## Disadvantages

Cost (falling with biosimilar glargine in India); HbA1c is no lower than with human insulin; cold-chain storage.

## In older adults

- A once-daily basal analogue is usually preferred, keeping the regimen simple.
- Avoid sliding scales alone.
- Relax targets (HbA1c below 7.5–8% depending on health), and make avoiding hypoglycaemia the priority.
- Use pens with large dials, involve caregivers, and consider continuous glucose monitoring.

## One-liner

Analogues don’t lower HbA1c more; they lower hypoglycaemia, which in older adults matters more.
