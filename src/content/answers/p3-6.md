## Answer at a glance

1. Define it, strength first (EWGSOP2), and use the Asian cut-offs (AWGS 2019).
2. Etiology: primary or secondary.
3. Pathogenesis.
4. Evaluation: find, test strength, confirm mass, grade severity, seek causes.
5. Close with management in brief.

## Definition

A progressive, generalised loss of muscle strength and mass that raises the risk of falls, fractures, disability and death. EWGSOP2 (2019) puts strength first:

- Low strength means **probable** sarcopenia.
- Adding low muscle quantity or quality **confirms** it.
- Adding poor physical performance makes it **severe**.

## Asian criteria (AWGS 2019), for use in India

- Handgrip below 28 kg in men, below 18 kg in women.
- Gait speed below 1.0 m/s, five chair stands taking 12 s or more, or SPPB of 9 or less.
- DXA appendicular muscle mass below 7.0 kg/m² in men, below 5.4 kg/m² in women.

## Etiology

Primary (age-related) or secondary:

- **Inactivity:** bed rest, a sedentary life.
- **Disease:** heart, lung, kidney or liver failure, cancer, inflammatory disease, diabetes.
- **Poor nutrition:** low protein intake, malabsorption, anorexia of ageing.
- **Drugs:** long-term glucocorticoids.

## Pathogenesis

- Loss of motor units, with type II fibre atrophy
- Anabolic resistance to protein and exercise
- Chronic inflammation (IL-6, TNF-α)
- Falling testosterone, IGF-1 and vitamin D
- Mitochondrial dysfunction and myostatin
- Fat infiltration of muscle, which lowers its quality

## Evaluation

1. **Find:** SARC-F of 4 or more, or calf circumference below 34 cm in men and 33 cm in women.
2. **Test strength:** handgrip or chair stands.
3. **Confirm mass:** DXA or bioimpedance.
4. **Grade severity:** gait speed, SPPB, Timed Up and Go.
5. **Seek causes:** nutrition screen, drug review, vitamin D, thyroid, diabetes, kidney function.

## Management in brief

Progressive resistance training 2–3 times a week, protein 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day (more during illness), vitamin D if deficient, and treatment of the cause. No drug is approved.

## One-liner

Low grip strength alone is enough to call probable sarcopenia and start treatment.
