## Answer at a glance

1. What they are.
2. Evaluation, using the DICE approach.
3. Non-drug management first.
4. Treating causes.
5. Drugs only for severe distress or danger.
6. Regular review.

## What they are

- Agitation, aggression, wandering, apathy, depression, anxiety.
- Delusions and hallucinations, disinhibition, sleep disturbance, sundowning, repetitive calling out.
- Up to 90% of people with dementia have them at some point.

## Evaluation (DICE: describe, investigate, create, evaluate)

- **Describe** the behaviour precisely with an ABC chart (antecedent, behaviour, consequence): frequency, severity, risk.
- **Investigate causes:**
  - Delirium; **pain** (use the PAINAD scale); infection; constipation; urinary retention; hunger or thirst.
  - Sleep loss; hearing or vision loss.
  - Drugs: anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, steroids, dopaminergics.
  - Environment: noise, overstimulation, unfamiliar places, changed routines, how carers communicate.
  - Depression, anxiety and psychosis.
- **Scales:** Neuropsychiatric Inventory (NPI or NPI-Q), Cohen-Mansfield Agitation Inventory, Cornell Scale for Depression in Dementia.

## Non-drug management first

- Person-centred care, and caregiver education and support.
- Routines and meaningful activity; music, exercise, reminiscence; light therapy for sleep.
- A calm environment; redirect rather than confront.

## Treat causes

A regular paracetamol trial reduced agitation in care-home residents with dementia.

## Drugs only for severe distress or danger

- **Antipsychotics:**
  - Risperidone (UK-licensed for up to 6 weeks for persistent aggression in Alzheimer’s).
  - Aripiprazole; brexpiprazole (US-approved for Alzheimer’s agitation, 2023).
  - Low-dose quetiapine in Lewy body or Parkinson’s dementia.
  - Use the lowest dose, review at 6–12 weeks and try withdrawal. They **raise the risk of stroke and death** (about 1.6–1.7 times).
- **Citalopram** reduced agitation (CitAD), but watch the QT interval and cognition.
- **Cholinesterase inhibitors,** especially for hallucinations in Lewy body dementia; memantine.
- **Avoid benzodiazepines** (falls, paradoxical agitation) except briefly in a crisis.
- **Sleep:** low-dose trazodone has some evidence; melatonin little.

## Review

Review regularly; involve the family; respect capacity and consent.

## One-liner

Behaviour is communication; look for pain, delirium and unmet needs before reaching for an antipsychotic.
