## Answer at a glance

1. Meaning, and the problem in India.
2. Constitutional basis.
3. The six parts of the system: formal pensions, informal pensions, social assistance, health security, welfare, and reform.
4. Gaps and the way forward.

## Meaning

Protection against loss of income and the costs of illness in old age, through pensions, health cover and welfare services.

## The problem

- About 9 in 10 Indian workers are informal and have no pension, so most older people depend on family or keep working.
- Older women, especially widows, are the most exposed.
- Families are smaller and more scattered, and health costs are rising.

## Constitutional basis

Article 41 (public assistance in old age); old age pensions and social security are on the Concurrent List.

## 1. Formal sector (contributory)

- Employees’ Provident Fund and the Employees’ Pension Scheme (1995).
- **Government employees:**
  - The old defined-benefit pension, for those who joined before 2004.
  - The National Pension System (defined contribution), since 2004.
  - The Unified Pension Scheme (from 2025), which assures 50% of the average basic pay of the last 12 months after 25 years’ service.
- Gratuity; ESI for health.

## 2. Informal sector (contributory)

- **Atal Pension Yojana (2015):** ₹1,000–5,000 a month from 60.
- **PM Shram Yogi Maan-dhan (2019):** ₹3,000 a month from 60; **PM Kisan Maan-dhan** for small farmers.
- The National Pension System, which is open to all.

## 3. Social assistance (non-contributory)

The National Social Assistance Programme (1995) covers old age, widow and disability pensions, family benefit and Annapurna, plus state pensions.

## 4. Health security

PM-JAY (everyone 70 and over since 2024), NPHCE, CGHS, and state schemes such as Tamil Nadu’s Chief Minister’s Comprehensive Health Insurance Scheme.

## 5. Welfare and protection

Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana (old age homes, day care, Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana), Elderline 14567, the 2007 Maintenance Act, savings schemes and tax benefits.

## 6. Reform

The Code on Social Security (2020) combines nine laws and extends cover to gig and platform workers.

## Gaps

- Low coverage.
- Tiny pensions: the central ₹200 hasn’t risen since 2011.
- Exclusion by paperwork and digital barriers.
- Fragmented schemes and poor awareness.
- High out-of-pocket health spending, and almost no long-term care cover.

## Way forward

- A universal minimum pension linked to inflation.
- Wider health and long-term care cover.
- Single-window enrolment.
- A focus on women and widows.
- Better enforcement of the 2007 Act.
- Helping people save during their working lives.

## One-liner

In India the family is still the main pension scheme, so social security has to catch those it fails.
