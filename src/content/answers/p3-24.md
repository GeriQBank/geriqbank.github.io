## Answer at a glance

1. Define dementia and its burden.
2. Types and risk factors.
3. Assessment and investigations.
4. Non-drug management, then drugs.
5. Behavioural symptoms and advanced dementia.

## Definition

Acquired decline in one or more cognitive domains, enough to affect independence, and not due to delirium or another mental disorder (major neurocognitive disorder in DSM-5).

## Burden

Prevalence roughly doubles every 5 years after 65. LASI-DAD (2023) estimated 7.4% of Indians aged 60 and over.

## Types

Alzheimer’s disease (60–70%), vascular, mixed, Lewy body, frontotemporal, Parkinson’s disease dementia, and reversible causes.

## Risk factors

Age and APOE ε4, plus 14 modifiable factors (Lancet Commission 2024):

- Less education, hearing loss, high LDL, depression, head injury, inactivity, diabetes, smoking.
- Hypertension, obesity, excess alcohol, social isolation, air pollution, untreated vision loss.
- Together these account for about 45% of dementia.

## Assessment

- History from both patient and informant: onset, course, domains affected, function.
- Examination, including senses.
- Cognitive testing (MMSE, MoCA, ACE-III, HMSE), ADL and IADL, mood and behaviour.
- Screen for delirium.

## Investigations

- Blood count, electrolytes, calcium, glucose, renal and liver function, TSH, B12; syphilis and HIV where indicated.
- CT or MRI.
- In selected cases: FDG-PET, CSF or blood amyloid and tau biomarkers (required before anti-amyloid therapy), DAT scan, EEG.

## Non-drug management

- Disclose the diagnosis and educate the family.
- Caregiver training and support, routines, cognitive stimulation therapy, exercise.
- Correct hearing and vision.
- Safety: driving, finances, wandering.
- Early advance care planning while capacity remains.

## Drugs

- **Cholinesterase inhibitors** (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) for mild to moderate Alzheimer’s, Lewy body and Parkinson’s disease dementia. Watch for bradycardia, syncope and GI upset.
- **Memantine** for moderate to severe Alzheimer’s.
- **Anti-amyloid antibodies** (lecanemab, donanemab) for early Alzheimer’s with confirmed amyloid. Benefit is modest, ARIA is a risk that needs MRI monitoring, and access and cost are limited.

## Behavioural and psychological symptoms

Look for triggers first, such as pain, constipation, infection or noise. Use non-drug measures first. Antipsychotics only for severe distress or risk, at the lowest dose and for the shortest time, because they raise stroke and death risk. Avoid benzodiazepines and anticholinergics.

## Advanced dementia

Careful hand feeding rather than a feeding tube, treatment of infections within agreed goals, and palliative care.

## Examiner’s traps

- Reaching for an antipsychotic first
- Forgetting the caregiver
- Missing delirium

## One-liner

In dementia, the caregiver is the second patient.
