## Answer at a glance

1. Scale of the problem, and why older adults are vulnerable.
2. Mechanisms.
3. Culprit drugs.
4. Recognition.
5. Management and prevention.

## Scale

Drugs are the commonest reversible cause, contributing to roughly a third of cases.

## Why older adults

- Pharmacokinetics: lower renal clearance and more body fat.
- Pharmacodynamics: heightened brain sensitivity, a leakier blood–brain barrier, low cholinergic reserve.
- Polypharmacy.

## Mechanisms

Cholinergic deficit (anticholinergics), dopamine excess (dopaminergic drugs), GABA effects (benzodiazepines and their withdrawal), serotonin excess; and indirect causes such as hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia and hypotension.

## Culprits

- **Anticholinergics:** oxybutynin, tricyclics, chlorpheniramine, promethazine, diphenhydramine, dicyclomine, paroxetine, trihexyphenidyl.
- **Sedatives:** benzodiazepines and Z-drugs, and withdrawal from them or from alcohol.
- **Opioids:** especially pethidine and tramadol; morphine metabolites build up in CKD.
- **Antiparkinsonian drugs:** levodopa, dopamine agonists, amantadine.
- **Steroids.**
- **Antimicrobials:** fluoroquinolones, cefepime and carbapenems in CKD, metronidazole, aciclovir in CKD.
- **H2 blockers** in CKD; **antiepileptics.**
- **Cardiac:** digoxin toxicity, antiarrhythmics, beta-blockers.
- **Indirect:** sulfonylureas (hypoglycaemia); diuretics and SSRIs (hyponatraemia); lithium toxicity; serotonin syndrome (tramadol, linezolid with SSRIs).

## Recognition

- Confirm delirium (4AT, CAM), then match its timeline to drug starts, dose changes and falling kidney function.
- Drug levels: digoxin, lithium, antiepileptics.
- Score anticholinergic burden (ACB scale).

## Management

- Stop, reduce or substitute the culprit. Taper benzodiazepines rather than stopping abruptly.
- Dose for the kidneys; treat withdrawal properly.
- Non-drug delirium care; avoid adding sedatives.
- Antipsychotics only for severe distress or danger, at the lowest dose for the shortest time.

## Prevention

Medication reconciliation, avoiding Beers-listed drugs, pharmacist review, regular deprescribing.

## One-liner

In every delirium read the drug chart line by line; the cure is often in the “stop” column.
