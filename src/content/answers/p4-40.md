## Definition

Voluntary authorisation by a person with capacity, after disclosure and understanding.

## Elements

- **Capacity** for this decision, using the four abilities (Appelbaum and Grisso).
- **Disclosure:**
  - What to cover: the condition, the proposed intervention, its benefits, **risks that matter to this patient**, and alternatives including no treatment.
  - **Legal standard:** India’s Samira Kohli judgment (2008) requires real, specific consent; the UK’s Montgomery ruling (2015) sets a patient-centred standard.
- **Understanding** (teach-back).
- **Voluntariness.**
- **Authorisation,** documented.

## Types

Implied, verbal and written (procedures, anaesthesia, research).

- Consent is **specific**: consent for a diagnostic procedure isn’t consent for an extended operation (Samira Kohli).
- Blanket consent isn’t valid.

## Older adults

- Adapt for hearing and vision loss; use plain language and the patient’s own language.
- Assess capacity for each decision, choosing the best time of day if capacity fluctuates.
- Families often lead decisions in India, but a capable patient’s choice still prevails. Avoid ageism.

## Without capacity

Follow an advance directive (Common Cause, 2018 and 2023), a nominated representative (Mental Healthcare Act 2017 for psychiatric treatment) or next of kin, in the patient’s best interests. In emergencies, treat under the doctrine of necessity.

## Research

ICMR National Ethical Guidelines (2017); audio-visual recording of consent for clinical trials involving vulnerable participants.

## Withdrawal

Consent can be withdrawn at any time.

## One-liner

Consent is a conversation, not a signature; check capacity for this decision, explain the risks that matter to this person, and let them say no.
