## Answer at a glance

1. Appropriate indications, and alternatives first.
2. Choosing the catheter and drainage system.

4. Complications.

## Appropriate indications

- Chronic retention that can’t be managed otherwise (outlet obstruction unfit for surgery, or a neurogenic bladder when intermittent catheterisation isn’t possible).
- Healing stage 3–4 pressure ulcers in incontinent patients (temporarily).
- Comfort at the end of life.
- The patient’s informed preference when other methods have failed.
- **Never** for the convenience of managing incontinence.

## Alternatives first

Clean intermittent self-catheterisation (the lowest infection risk), sheath catheters in men, pads, toileting programmes, and treating the cause of retention.

## Choices

- **Suprapubic vs urethral:** suprapubic is preferred long-term, with less urethral damage and easier hygiene.
- **Material:** silicone or hydrogel-coated catheters for long-term use (up to 12 weeks).
- **Size:** the smallest that drains well (12–16 Fr), with a 10 mL balloon.
- A closed system, with a leg bag by day and a night bag. Catheter valves keep bladder capacity in people able to use them.

## Care

- Aseptic insertion and a closed system.
- Bag below the bladder and off the floor; secure the catheter to the thigh.
- Daily soap-and-water meatal hygiene.
- Fluids 1.5–2 L a day; avoid constipation.
- Change the catheter as the manufacturer advises, or when blocked, and change leg bags weekly.
- **No** routine bladder washouts or antibiotic prophylaxis.

## Complications

- **Infection:** bacteriuria is universal after about a month, so treat only symptoms (fever, rigors, delirium, loin pain). Change the catheter before taking a culture and starting antibiotics.
- **Blockage:** encrustation from Proteus (which makes struvite).
- **Bypassing:** from blockage, constipation or bladder spasm.
- **Trauma:** urethral injury and erosion, strictures.
- Bladder stones, haematuria, purple urine bag syndrome.
- Squamous bladder cancer with very long use.
- Suprapubic site infection.

## Documentation

The reason, insertion date, size, balloon volume, next change date, and planned trials without the catheter.

## One-liner

Every long-term catheter needs a reason, a review date and a plan to remove it.
