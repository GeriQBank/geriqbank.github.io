## Definition

A structured, multidisciplinary programme of exercise training, education, risk-factor control and psychosocial support after a cardiac event.

## Indications

- After MI or ACS, PCI, CABG, valve surgery or TAVI.
- Stable angina; chronic heart failure (HFrEF and HFpEF); after heart transplant.
- Supervised exercise for peripheral artery disease.

## Benefits

- Fewer cardiovascular deaths, MIs and hospital admissions (Cochrane 2021).
- Better exercise capacity and quality of life.
- In older adults admitted with heart failure, a multidomain programme improved physical function (REHAB-HF, 2021).

## Phases

- **I, inpatient:** early mobilisation and education.
- **II, early outpatient:** supervised exercise 2–3 times a week for 6–12 weeks.
- **III, maintenance:** community or home.

## Components

- **Assessment and risk stratification:** exercise test or 6-minute walk.
- **Exercise prescription (FITT):**
  - Aerobic exercise 3–5 days a week, 20–60 minutes, at moderate intensity (RPE 11–14, or 40–80% of heart rate reserve).
  - Resistance training 2–3 days a week.
  - Balance and flexibility for older adults.
- **Risk factors:** lipids, blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, weight, diet.
- **Support:** medication adherence; depression and anxiety screening; advice on work, driving and sex.

## Contraindications

Unstable angina, uncontrolled arrhythmia, decompensated heart failure, severe symptomatic AS, blood pressure over 180/110, acute illness.

## Access

- Older adults and women are under-referred; transport, cost and frailty get in the way.
- Home-based and hybrid or digital programmes work about as well as centre-based ones.
- India: few centres. Yoga-based rehabilitation (Yoga-CaRe) improved quality of life and return to activities, though not major cardiac events.

## One-liner

Cardiac rehab is a treatment with no side effects that most older patients never get; refer everyone after an event.
