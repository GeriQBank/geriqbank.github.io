## Definition

Low bone mass with deteriorated microarchitecture, leading to fragility fractures. Diagnosed by a DXA T-score of −2.5 or below, or by a hip or vertebral fragility fracture.

## Burden

About 1 in 3 women and 1 in 5 men over 50 will have a fragility fracture.

## Pathology

Resorption outpaces formation (oestrogen loss drives RANKL, and osteoblasts age), causing trabecular thinning and cortical porosity.

## Risk factors

- Age, female sex, early menopause, low BMI, previous fracture, parental hip fracture.
- Smoking, alcohol, glucocorticoids, rheumatoid arthritis, immobility, low calcium and vitamin D.
- **Secondary causes:** thyroid and parathyroid disease, hypogonadism, CKD, malabsorption, diabetes, and drugs (anticonvulsants, aromatase inhibitors, androgen deprivation, long-term PPIs).

## Clinical picture

Silent until a fracture of the spine (height loss over 4 cm, kyphosis), hip, wrist or humerus. About a quarter of people die within a year of a hip fracture.

## Investigations

- DXA, FRAX, and vertebral imaging.
- **Secondary work-up:** blood count, calcium, phosphate, ALP, creatinine, liver function, vitamin D, PTH, TSH; testosterone in men; electrophoresis; coeliac serology if indicated.
- Bone turnover markers (CTX, P1NP) for monitoring.

## Non-drug care

Falls prevention, resistance and balance exercise, calcium 1,000–1,200 mg a day (diet first), vitamin D 800–1,000 IU a day, enough protein, no smoking, less alcohol, and hip protectors in care homes.

## Drugs

- **Bisphosphonates first:** weekly alendronate, risedronate, or yearly IV zoledronic acid (if creatinine clearance is 35 or more).
- **Denosumab** every 6 months: usable in CKD, but **never stop it abruptly**, as vertebral fractures rebound. Follow it with a bisphosphonate.
- **Very high risk** (recent or multiple vertebral fractures, T-score −3.0 or below): anabolic drugs first. Options are teriparatide (usually up to 2 years), abaloparatide, or romosozumab for 12 months (avoid after recent MI or stroke), each followed by an antiresorptive.
- **Raloxifene** protects the spine only and raises VTE risk.
- Review after 5 years of oral or 3 years of IV bisphosphonate and consider a drug holiday.
- **Rare risks:** atypical femur fracture, osteonecrosis of the jaw. Correct vitamin D before zoledronic acid or denosumab.

## Fracture liaison

A fracture liaison service after every fragility fracture.

## One-liner

Every fragility fracture is a sentinel; treat the bone and prevent the next fall.
