## Answer at a glance

1. Definition and forms.
2. Causes, and the neurogenic clue.
3. Evaluation.
4. Non-drug treatment first, then drugs.

## Definition

A sustained fall of 20 mmHg systolic or 10 mmHg diastolic within 3 minutes of standing. With supine hypertension, a systolic fall of 30 mmHg. There are also initial (within 15 s) and delayed (after 3 minutes) forms.

## Causes

- **Drugs:** diuretics, alpha-blockers, nitrates, vasodilators, antipsychotics, tricyclics, levodopa.
- Low volume and prolonged bed rest.
- **Neurogenic:** Parkinson’s disease, MSA, Lewy body dementia, diabetic or amyloid autonomic neuropathy, pure autonomic failure.
- Post-prandial hypotension, heart failure.

## Neurogenic clue

The heart rate barely rises; a rise in beats per minute less than half the fall in systolic mmHg.

## Evaluation

- Lying and standing blood pressure at 1 and 3 minutes.
- Symptoms: dizziness, falls, “coat-hanger” neck pain, fatigue, slowed thinking.
- Drug review, volume status, ECG, glucose and B12.
- Tilt table and ambulatory blood pressure (for supine hypertension) as needed.

## Non-drug treatment

- Remove culprit drugs.
- Fluids of 1.5–2 L a day (unless in heart failure), and more salt.
- Drinking 500 mL of water quickly, which raises blood pressure within minutes.
- Raise the head of the bed 10–20°.
- Stand up slowly and use counter-manoeuvres (crossing legs, squatting).
- Compression garments or an abdominal binder.
- Small, low-carbohydrate meals; avoid heat and alcohol.

## Drugs

- **Midodrine:** the last dose at least 4 hours before bed, because of supine hypertension.
- **Fludrocortisone:** watch potassium, oedema and heart failure.
- Droxidopa for neurogenic orthostatic hypotension; pyridostigmine for mild cases.

## One-liner

Always measure blood pressure standing, and treat the symptoms, not the number.
