## Definition

A whole-joint disease: cartilage loss, subchondral bone remodelling (sclerosis, cysts), osteophytes, low-grade synovitis, and ligament and meniscal damage. It’s the commonest arthritis; knee OA in older Indian women is especially common.

## Aetiology

- **Primary risk factors:**
  - Age and female sex.
  - Obesity (knee, and hand through metabolic effects); genetics.
  - Previous injury, malalignment (varus knee), muscle weakness.
  - Occupational loading (kneeling, squatting, lifting).
- **Secondary:** trauma, inflammatory arthritis, CPPD, haemochromatosis, Paget’s disease, avascular necrosis.

## Pathogenesis

- Repair and breakdown of cartilage fall out of balance. Activated chondrocytes release MMP-13 and aggrecanases (ADAMTS-4/5), degrading collagen II and proteoglycans.
- IL-1 and TNF drive synovitis.
- Subchondral bone sclerosis and marrow lesions develop, and osteophytes form (TGF-β).
- Chondrocyte senescence and abnormal mechanical load accelerate the process.

## Clinical features

- Pain with use, eased by rest; stiffness under 30 minutes, or “gelling” after inactivity.
- Crepitus, bony swelling, a cool effusion, reduced movement, quadriceps wasting, deformity.
- **Hands:** Heberden’s (DIP) and Bouchard’s (PIP) nodes, and squaring at the thumb base.
- **Hip:** groin pain with reduced internal rotation.

## Diagnosis

- Clinical when typical (NICE: 45 or over, activity-related pain, morning stiffness of 30 minutes or less).
- ACR clinical criteria for the knee.
- **X-ray:** asymmetric joint-space narrowing, osteophytes, sclerosis, cysts; Kellgren–Lawrence grade 0–4.
- **Bloods:** ESR and CRP are normal.
- **Joint fluid:** non-inflammatory (under 2,000 white cells/mm³). Aspirate if crystals or infection are possible.

## Management (OARSI, ACR, NICE 2022)

- **Core for everyone:** education, exercise (strengthening, aerobic, tai chi), and weight loss of 5–10% or more for knee OA.
- **Aids:** a stick in the opposite hand, braces, footwear, a thumb splint.
- **Drugs:**
  - Topical NSAIDs first for knee and hand OA, especially from 75.
  - Oral NSAIDs at the lowest dose for the shortest time, with a PPI. Avoid them in CKD, heart failure, GI bleeding and with anticoagulants.
  - Paracetamol has little benefit, and long-term opioids should be avoided.
  - Duloxetine and topical capsaicin are options.
  - Intra-articular steroids give short-term relief in flares.
- **Not recommended:** glucosamine, hyaluronic acid injections, PRP or stem cells, arthroscopic washout.
- **Surgery:** joint replacement for severe pain and disability despite non-surgical care. Age alone isn’t a barrier; prehabilitation helps.
- No disease-modifying drug yet; the anti-NGF antibody tanezumab was rejected because of rapidly progressive OA.

## One-liner

In OA, exercise is the best drug, weight loss the best adjunct, and a topical NSAID the safest pill.
