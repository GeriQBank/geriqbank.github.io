## Definition

Systolic 140 mmHg or more with diastolic below 90.

## Mechanism

Stiffening of the large arteries speeds up the pulse wave and brings the reflected wave back early, which widens the pulse pressure.

## Why it matters

It is the commonest form of hypertension in older adults, and a strong predictor of stroke, heart failure, CKD and cardiovascular death.

## Evaluation

- Confirm with home or ambulatory readings.
- Measure standing blood pressure.
- Exclude other causes of a wide pulse pressure: aortic regurgitation, anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, AV fistula, Paget’s disease.
- Assess organ damage.

## Evidence

SHEP (chlorthalidone), Syst-Eur (nitrendipine), and HYVET (indapamide with or without perindopril, in people aged 80 and over; fewer strokes and deaths).

## Treatment

- Lifestyle: salt, weight, exercise, alcohol.
- **First line:** a thiazide-like diuretic (chlorthalidone, indapamide) or a dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker (amlodipine); ACE inhibitor or ARB as needed. Beta-blockers only for another reason.
- Start low and go slow; monitor sodium, potassium and standing blood pressure.

## Target (ESC 2024)

Systolic 120–129 mmHg if tolerated. At 85 or over, or when frail, as low as reasonably achievable. Be careful when diastolic falls below about 60–70 mmHg in coronary disease.

## One-liner

In older adults the systolic number matters most; check standing blood pressure before you intensify treatment.
