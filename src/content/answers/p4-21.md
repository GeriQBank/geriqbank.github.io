## Answer at a glance

1. The National Policy on Older Persons (1999).
2. Schemes for income and food.
3. Health schemes.
4. Welfare schemes and legal protection.

## National Policy on Older Persons (1999)

From the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment. It defines older persons as 60 and over.

- **Areas:** financial security, health care and nutrition, shelter, education, welfare, and protection of life and property.
- It encourages people to plan for their own old age, supports family care, involves NGOs, and set up a National Council for Older Persons.
- A newer senior citizens’ policy was drafted in 2011 but never formally adopted.

## Income

- **Indira Gandhi National Old Age Pension Scheme** (under NSAP): ₹200 a month at 60–79 and ₹500 from 80, for people below the poverty line, plus state top-ups.
- Senior Citizens’ Savings Scheme (limit ₹30 lakh), reverse mortgage, and tax benefits.

## Food

The Annapurna scheme gives 10 kg of free food grains a month to eligible older people without a pension.

## Health

- NPHCE.
- **Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY:** ₹5 lakh a year of hospital cover, extended in 2024 to everyone aged 70 and over regardless of income (the Ayushman Vay Vandana card).
- CGHS for central government pensioners, and state insurance schemes.

## Welfare (Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana, the umbrella scheme)

- Old age homes, day-care centres and mobile medicare units run by NGOs.
- **Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana:** free aids and devices, such as walking aids, hearing aids, spectacles, dentures and wheelchairs.
- **Elderline 14567.**
- The SACRED re-employment portal, and SAGE for elder-care start-ups.

## Legal

The Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007.

## One-liner

NPOP set the vision in 1999; the schemes are how it reaches the older person’s pocket, plate and prescription.
