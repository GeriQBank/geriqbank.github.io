## Atherosclerosis

A chronic inflammatory disease of large and medium arteries.

**Stages:** endothelial dysfunction → LDL entry and oxidation → monocytes become foam cells (fatty streak) → smooth muscle forms a fibrous cap over a necrotic core → calcification → rupture or erosion with thrombosis.

## Risk factors

Age, male sex, family history, smoking, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, obesity, CKD, inflammation, air pollution, and South Asian ancestry (earlier coronary disease).

## Biomarkers

- **Lipids:** LDL cholesterol (causal), non-HDL cholesterol, apolipoprotein B (particle number), triglycerides. **Lipoprotein(a)** is genetic and causal, often high in South Asians; measure it once.
- **Inflammation:** hs-CRP (above 2 mg/L means higher risk, as in JUPITER), IL-6.
- **Glycaemia and kidney:** HbA1c, eGFR, urine ACR.
- **Heart stress and injury:** hs-troponin, NT-proBNP.
- **Weaker or not recommended:** homocysteine, fibrinogen, Lp-PLA2.

## Imaging markers

- Coronary artery calcium score: zero means low risk; 100 or more favours a statin.
- Carotid plaque on ultrasound.
- Ankle-brachial index below 0.9.

## In older adults

Calcium scores are often high, but a score of zero can support not starting a statin in someone over 75 without known disease.

## One-liner

LDL causes it, Lp(a) is inherited, CRP shows inflammation, and a calcium score of zero is the most reassuring test of all.
