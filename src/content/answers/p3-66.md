## Answer at a glance

1. Epidemiology and types.
2. Causes and features.
3. Evaluation.
4. Management (AF-CARE): stroke prevention, rate control, rhythm control, risk factors.
5. Older adults.

## Epidemiology

The commonest sustained arrhythmia, affecting about 1 in 10 people over 80.

## Types

First-diagnosed; paroxysmal (under 7 days); persistent (over 7 days); long-standing persistent (over 12 months); permanent.

## Causes

Hypertension, ischaemic heart disease, heart failure, valve disease (rheumatic mitral stenosis in India), thyroid disease, alcohol, sleep apnoea, sepsis, surgery, lung disease.

## Features

Palpitations, breathlessness, fatigue, dizziness, heart failure; often silent, sometimes first presenting as stroke. Irregularly irregular pulse with a pulse deficit.

## Evaluation

ECG (no P waves, irregular RR); echo; TSH, renal, liver function and blood count; Holter for paroxysmal AF.

## Management (ESC 2024 “AF-CARE”)

- **Stroke prevention (CHA₂DS₂-VA):**
  - Anticoagulate at 2 or more; consider at 1.
  - **DOAC over warfarin,** except moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis or mechanical valves (use warfarin).
  - Fix modifiable bleeding risks rather than withholding treatment. **Falls alone aren’t a reason to stop.**
  - Left atrial appendage occlusion if anticoagulation is impossible. Aspirin doesn’t prevent AF strokes.
- **Rate control** (lenient target: resting rate below 110; RACE II):
  - Beta-blocker, or diltiazem/verapamil (not in HFrEF).
  - Digoxin for heart failure or sedentary patients.
  - AV-node ablation with pacing if refractory.
- **Rhythm control** for persistent symptoms, early AF (EAST-AFNET 4) or heart failure:
  - Cardioversion after 3 weeks of anticoagulation or a TOE, and continue anticoagulation for 4 weeks after.
  - Antiarrhythmics: amiodarone (thyroid, lung and liver toxicity); flecainide only without structural heart disease.
  - Catheter ablation in selected older adults.
- **Risk factors:** blood pressure, weight, alcohol, sleep apnoea, diabetes.

## Older adults

- **Apixaban 2.5 mg twice daily** if 2 of: age 80 or over, weight 60 kg or less, creatinine 1.5 mg/dL or more.
- Edoxaban 15 mg helped very frail octogenarians (ELDERCARE-AF).
- Watch interactions and adherence with cognitive impairment.

## One-liner

In older adults with AF the stroke is the enemy; anticoagulate by CHA₂DS₂-VA, don’t let falls scare you off, and control the rate gently.
