## Mechanisms

Raised hydrostatic pressure (heart failure), low oncotic pressure (low albumin, nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis), increased capillary permeability (infection, cancer, inflammation), blocked lymphatics (cancer), or fluid crossing from the abdomen (hepatic hydrothorax).

## Causes in older adults

- Heart failure (the commonest).
- Pneumonia and empyema.
- Cancer (lung, breast, lymphoma, mesothelioma after asbestos).
- **TB** (common in India).
- PE, low albumin, cirrhosis, CKD, hypothyroidism.
- Drugs (amiodarone, methotrexate), after cardiac surgery, rheumatoid disease.

## Clinical features

- Breathlessness, pleuritic pain, cough.
- Stony dullness, reduced breath sounds and vocal resonance; tracheal shift away with large effusions.

## Imaging

- Chest X-ray: a blunted angle and meniscus.
- Ultrasound: more sensitive, shows septations, and guides the tap.
- Contrast CT: pleural thickening, cancer, empyema.

## Approach

In obvious bilateral heart failure, treat the failure first, and tap only if atypical (unequal sides, fever, pain, no response). Otherwise, do a diagnostic tap under ultrasound.

## Fluid analysis

- Appearance, protein, LDH, glucose, pH.
- Cell count and differential.
- Gram stain and culture (in blood culture bottles).
- AFB smear and GeneXpert.
- **ADA** (above 40 U/L suggests TB).
- Cytology (about 60% sensitive, so repeat once).
- NT-proBNP (above 1,500 pg/mL suggests heart failure), triglycerides (chylothorax), haematocrit (haemothorax).

## Light’s criteria

The fluid is an exudate if any of these apply:

- fluid-to-serum protein ratio above 0.5;
- fluid-to-serum LDH ratio above 0.6;
- fluid LDH above two-thirds of the upper limit of serum LDH.

Diuretics can make a transudate look exudative; a serum-minus-fluid albumin gradient above 1.2 g/dL points back to a transudate.

## Infected effusions

pH below 7.2, glucose below 60 mg/dL, pus or a positive Gram stain need a chest drain. Intrapleural tPA with DNase helps (MIST-2).

## Undiagnosed exudate

Pleural biopsy (CT-guided or thoracoscopic) has a high yield for TB and cancer.

## Management

- Treat the cause.
- Therapeutic taps of up to about 1.5 L at a time, to avoid re-expansion oedema.
- **Malignant effusions:** talc pleurodesis or an indwelling pleural catheter (good for the frail, or a trapped lung).
- **TB:** anti-TB treatment.
- **Empyema:** antibiotics and drainage, with surgery (VATS) if fit.
- **Heart failure:** diuretics.
- In older adults, consider anticoagulant bleeding risk, delirium and goals of care.

## One-liner

Tap under ultrasound and apply Light’s criteria, and in India always ask for ADA and GeneXpert in a lymphocytic exudate.
