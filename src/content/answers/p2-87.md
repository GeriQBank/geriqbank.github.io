## Answer at a glance

1. Define it, correcting for albumin.
2. Causes: PTH-dependent and PTH-independent.
3. Clinical features.
4. Evaluation, with PTH as the branch point.
5. Acute management.
6. Primary hyperparathyroidism.

## Definition

Corrected calcium above 10.5 mg/dL (2.6 mmol/L). It is mild up to 12, moderate at 12–14, and severe above 14.

Correct for albumin: corrected calcium = measured calcium + 0.8 × (4 − albumin in g/dL). Ionised calcium is best.

## Causes

Over 90% are primary hyperparathyroidism (outpatients) or cancer (inpatients).

- **PTH-dependent** (PTH high or inappropriately normal): primary hyperparathyroidism (a parathyroid adenoma in most), tertiary hyperparathyroidism in CKD, familial hypocalciuric hypercalcaemia, lithium.
- **PTH-independent** (PTH suppressed):
  - Cancer: PTHrP from squamous cancers and breast or renal cancer; bone metastases; myeloma; calcitriol from lymphoma.
  - Vitamin D excess; granulomatous disease (sarcoidosis, TB).
  - Hyperthyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, immobilisation, milk-alkali syndrome, vitamin A excess, thiazides.

## Clinical features

“Bones, stones, abdominal groans, psychic moans”: thirst and polyuria, dehydration, constipation, nausea, abdominal pain, pancreatitis, peptic ulcer, weakness, renal stones, AKI, short QT. In older adults, delirium and dehydration dominate.

## Evaluation

- Repeat and correct the calcium, then check PTH, which is the key branch point.
- If PTH is suppressed: PTHrP, 25-OH and 1,25-(OH)₂ vitamin D, electrophoresis with free light chains, TSH, and chest imaging.
- Also phosphate, ALP, renal function and ECG.
- Urine calcium: a very low calcium-to-creatinine clearance ratio (below 0.01) suggests familial hypocalciuric hypercalcaemia.

## Acute management

For severe or symptomatic hypercalcaemia:

- IV normal saline at 200–300 mL/h, watching for heart failure.
- Stop thiazides, calcium, vitamin D and lithium.
- Calcitonin 4 IU/kg every 12 hours works fast but wears off within about 2 days.
- Zoledronic acid 4 mg IV (adjust for kidney function), or denosumab 120 mg SC in renal failure or when bisphosphonates fail.
- Steroids for calcitriol-driven causes (lymphoma, granulomas, vitamin D toxicity).
- Dialysis if severe with renal failure.
- Loop diuretics only after volume is restored.

## Primary hyperparathyroidism

- Parathyroidectomy if symptomatic, or if calcium is more than 1 mg/dL above normal, T-score is −2.5 or below or there’s a vertebral fracture, eGFR is below 60, there are stones or hypercalciuria, or age is under 50.
- Otherwise monitor; cinacalcet if surgery isn’t possible.

## One-liner

New confusion and constipation in an older person means checking the calcium, and then the PTH.
