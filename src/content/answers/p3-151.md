## Principles

- Individualise; don’t decide by age alone.
- The benefit of breast or colorectal screening takes about 10 years to appear, so it rarely helps those expected to live less than that. Tools such as ePrognosis help estimate life expectancy.
- Weigh function, comorbidity and cognition, and whether they’d accept treatment.
- **Harms:** false positives, overdiagnosis, complications of follow-up tests such as colonoscopy, and anxiety.
- Decide together with the patient.

## Recommendations (US Preventive Services Task Force)

- **Breast:** mammography every 2 years from 40 to 74; individualise after 75.
- **Colorectal:** routinely from 45 to 75, selectively from 76 to 85, not after 85.
- **Cervical:** stop after 65 if previous screening was adequate and negative.
- **Prostate (PSA):** an individual decision from 55 to 69; not recommended from 70.
- **Lung:** yearly low-dose CT from 50 to 80 for people with 20 pack-years who smoke now or quit within 15 years.

## India

The national NCD programme screens adults from 30 for oral, breast (clinical examination) and cervical (visual inspection with acetic acid) cancers.

## Geriatric approach

Screen those who are fit and willing; stop when life expectancy is limited or they wouldn’t accept treatment; focus on symptoms and early diagnosis instead.

## Advice for Mr X

- Acknowledge his worry, and ask what he fears and about family history.
- Estimate his life expectancy from health and function. A fit 80-year-old may live a decade; a frail one, much less.
- Explain the trade-off: screening takes years to pay off, and brings false alarms, overdiagnosis and procedure risks.
- **At 80, specifically:**
  - Colorectal: a stool test (FIT) or colonoscopy only if he is fit, has never been screened, and would accept treatment.
  - PSA: not recommended.
  - Lung: low-dose CT only if he meets the smoking criteria.
  - Others: not routine.
- **Teach the symptoms to report promptly:** blood in stool or urine, a change in bowel habit, weight loss, persistent cough or hoarseness, difficulty swallowing, a new lump. At his age, prompt diagnosis of symptoms matters more than screening.
- Record his choice and revisit it as his health changes. Make sure higher-yield prevention is done: vaccines, falls, blood pressure.

## One-liner

Screen the patient, not the age; if they won’t live ten years, the test is unlikely to help them.
