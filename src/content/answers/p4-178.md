## Answer at a glance

1. Causes: mechanical, stenosis, fracture, serious and referred.
2. Red flags.
3. Evaluation.
4. Management by cause.

## Causes

- **Mechanical or degenerative (most):** spondylosis, facet joint arthritis, disc degeneration, degenerative spondylolisthesis.
- **Lumbar canal stenosis:** neurogenic claudication, with leg pain or numbness on walking or standing, eased by sitting or bending forward (the “shopping-cart sign”).
- **Osteoporotic vertebral fracture:** sudden pain after minor strain.
- **Serious causes:**
  - Metastases (prostate, breast, lung, kidney, thyroid) and myeloma.
  - Infection: vertebral osteomyelitis, discitis, **TB spine (Pott’s)**, epidural abscess.
  - Cauda equina syndrome.
  - Inflammatory: PMR, DISH.
  - Paget’s disease.
- **Referred pain:** **abdominal aortic aneurysm**, kidney (stones, pyelonephritis), pancreas, retroperitoneum; hip OA can mimic back pain.

## Red flags

- New pain after 50; history of cancer; weight loss; fever.
- Night or rest pain; steroids or immunosuppression; trauma.
- Neurological deficit, bladder or bowel dysfunction, saddle anaesthesia.
- A pulsatile abdominal mass.

## Evaluation

- **Examination:** neurological, straight-leg raise and femoral stretch tests, abdomen (aneurysm), hips, peripheral pulses (vascular claudication).
- **If red flags:** blood count, ESR/CRP, calcium, ALP, PSA, serum electrophoresis; X-ray; **urgent MRI** for cauda equina, infection, cancer or cord compression.
- DXA if a fracture is suspected.

## Management

- **Non-specific mechanical pain:**
  - Reassure, stay active (no bed rest), physiotherapy and exercise (core strengthening, yoga, tai chi), heat.
  - Topical NSAIDs, or a short course of oral NSAIDs with caution; paracetamol helps little.
  - Duloxetine for chronic pain; avoid long-term opioids; CBT-based pain programmes.
- **Spinal stenosis:** flexion-based physiotherapy and analgesia. Epidural steroids help little; decompression surgery for severe or progressive cases.
- **Vertebral fracture:** analgesia, early mobilisation, osteoporosis treatment; vertebroplasty only for selected refractory pain.
- **Specific causes:**
  - Antibiotics or anti-TB treatment.
  - Oncology and radiotherapy; dexamethasone and urgent decompression for metastatic cord compression.
  - Emergency surgery for cauda equina syndrome.
- **Older-adult cautions:** analgesic side effects, falls and polypharmacy; walking aids.

## One-liner

New back pain after 50 earns a red-flag screen; don’t forget the aorta, the bones and cancer.
