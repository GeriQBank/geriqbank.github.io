## Answer at a glance

1. Definition, and why every UTI in a man is complicated.
2. Evaluation: history, examination and tests.
3. Common causes.
4. Advice and treatment.

## Definition

2 or more UTIs in 6 months, or 3 or more in a year. **In men, every UTI counts as complicated**, so look for a cause. Three hospital admissions in 6 months certainly need investigation.

## Evaluation

- **Confirm true UTIs:** symptoms plus positive cultures, not just bacteriuria. The same organism each time suggests a hidden focus (prostatitis, stone); different organisms suggest reinfection.
- **History:** LUTS, retention, catheters or instrumentation, diabetes (and SGLT2 inhibitors), fluid intake, faecal incontinence, perineal pain (prostatitis), haematuria.
- **Examination:** rectal exam (prostate size, tenderness, nodules), bladder, genitalia.
- **Tests:**
  - Urine cultures, post-void residual, renal function, HbA1c.
  - PSA once the infection has settled, since infection raises it.
  - Ultrasound of kidneys, bladder and prostate; CT urogram if stones or haematuria.
  - Uroflowmetry and cystoscopy through a urology referral.
  - The 2-glass test for chronic bacterial prostatitis.

## Common causes

BPH with incomplete emptying, urethral stricture, chronic bacterial prostatitis, stones, bladder diverticulum, neurogenic bladder (diabetes), catheters, poorly controlled diabetes.

## Advice and treatment

- **Fix the cause:** relieve obstruction (alpha-blocker, 5-alpha reductase inhibitor, or TURP); intermittent self-catheterisation for a large residual; treat stones or strictures.
- **Chronic bacterial prostatitis:** a 4–6-week course of a fluoroquinolone (which penetrates the prostate), or co-trimoxazole. In older adults, fluoroquinolones carry risks: tendon rupture, aortic aneurysm, QT prolongation, delirium.
- **General measures:** enough fluids, avoid constipation, double voiding, good diabetes control, avoid unnecessary catheters, and stop drugs that cause retention.
- **Prophylaxis:** consider low-dose antibiotics only when no correctable cause is found, and review at 6 months. Nitrofurantoin isn’t suitable if eGFR is below 30. Weigh the risks of resistance and C. difficile.
- **Non-antibiotic options:** these are mostly studied in women. Methenamine hippurate matched antibiotic prophylaxis in ALTAR; D-mannose didn’t work in MERIT; cranberry evidence is weak.
- Don’t treat asymptomatic bacteriuria, and guide every course by culture.

## One-liner

In a man, a recurrent UTI is a symptom; find the prostate, stone or residual urine behind it.
