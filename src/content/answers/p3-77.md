## Answer at a glance

1. Diagnosis and spirometric grades.
2. From the ABCD grid to ABE.
3. Initial and follow-up treatment.
4. Non-drug care, and tips for older adults.

## Diagnosis

Post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC below 0.70, with symptoms or exposure. In the elderly, the fixed ratio over-diagnoses, so the lower limit of normal is an alternative.

## Spirometric grade

GOLD 1 (FEV1 80% or more), GOLD 2 (50–79%), GOLD 3 (30–49%), GOLD 4 (below 30%).

## From ABCD to ABE

- **Old ABCD grid (to 2022):** symptoms (mMRC 2 or more, or CAT 10 or more) against exacerbations (2 or more, or any hospitalisation).
- **New ABE (GOLD 2023 onwards):** groups C and D merged into **E**, defined by exacerbations. Groups A and B are split by symptoms.

## Initial treatment

- **A:** a bronchodilator, preferably a long-acting one.
- **B:** LABA plus LAMA.
- **E:** LABA plus LAMA; add an inhaled steroid (triple therapy) if eosinophils are 300 or more.
- ICS plus LABA alone is no longer advised.

## Follow-up

- **Persistent breathlessness:** LABA plus LAMA.
- **Exacerbations:** triple therapy if eosinophils are 100 or more, roflumilast, or azithromycin.
- Dupilumab (GOLD 2025) for high eosinophils with chronic bronchitis.

## Non-drug care

- Stopping smoking.
- **Vaccines:** influenza, pneumococcal, RSV, COVID.
- Pulmonary rehabilitation and inhaler technique.
- Long-term oxygen if PaO₂ is 55 mmHg or less, or SpO₂ 88% or less; home NIV for chronic hypercapnia.

## In older adults

Choose a device they can actually use (spacer or soft-mist inhaler), and mind anticholinergic load.

## One-liner

GOLD 2023 merged C and D into E, so exacerbations now define the group.
