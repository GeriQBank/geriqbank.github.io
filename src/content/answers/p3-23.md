## Answer at a glance

1. Define it, and carotid sinus syndrome.
2. Who gets it, and triggers.
3. Diagnosis by carotid sinus massage, with its precautions.
4. Management by type.

## Definition

An exaggerated response to carotid sinus massage. It can be **cardioinhibitory** (asystole of 3 s or more), **vasodepressor** (systolic fall of 50 mmHg or more) or mixed. **Carotid sinus syndrome** is when this reproduces the patient’s spontaneous syncope.

## Who gets it

Mainly older men with coronary disease or hypertension. It is an important cause of unexplained falls, because patients often don’t remember losing consciousness.

## Triggers

Turning the head, tight collars, shaving, though often none is found.

## Diagnosis

- Carotid sinus massage for 10 s on each side, lying and then upright on a tilt table, with continuous ECG and beat-to-beat blood pressure.
- Avoid it after a TIA or stroke in the past 3 months, or with a carotid bruit, unless a Doppler has excluded significant stenosis.

## Management

- **Cardioinhibitory type with recurrent syncope:** dual-chamber pacemaker.
- **Vasodepressor type:** avoid triggers, stop vasodilators, and add fluids and salt.
- Falls prevention for everyone.

## One-liner

In unexplained falls, massage the carotid sinus standing as well as lying.
