## Answer at a glance

1. Features and classification criteria.
2. Differentials and investigations.
3. Steroid treatment, taper and steroid-sparing options.
4. Warning signs of giant cell arteritis.

## Features

Over 50 (usually over 60), more in women. Bilateral shoulder and hip girdle pain, morning stiffness over 45 minutes, raised ESR or CRP, fatigue and weight loss. About 15–20% also have giant cell arteritis.

## EULAR/ACR 2012 criteria

Morning stiffness, hip pain, negative RF and anti-CCP, no other joint involvement, plus optional ultrasound findings.

## Differentials

Late-onset rheumatoid arthritis, myositis, hypothyroidism, cancer, endocarditis, statin myopathy, CPPD, fibromyalgia.

## Investigations

ESR, CRP, blood count, liver and renal function, calcium, TSH, CK (normal in PMR), RF and anti-CCP, electrophoresis, urinalysis, chest X-ray; ultrasound may show subdeltoid bursitis.

## Treatment

- Prednisolone 12.5–25 mg a day, usually 15 mg. The response should be dramatic within days; if not, reconsider the diagnosis.
- Taper to 10 mg by 4–8 weeks, then by 1 mg every 4–8 weeks, for 1–2 years in total. Relapses are common.
- **Steroid-sparing options:** methotrexate, or IL-6 blockade (sarilumab) for relapsing disease.
- Bone protection, and monitor glucose, blood pressure and eyes.

## Teach the warning signs of GCA

Headache, jaw pain, visual symptoms. These are an emergency.

## One-liner

No dramatic steroid response within a week means rethinking PMR.
