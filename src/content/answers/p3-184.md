## Why it matters

Potentially inappropriate medicines are common in older adults and cause adverse drug reactions, falls, delirium and admissions.

## Explicit (criteria-based) tools

- **Beers Criteria (AGS 2023),** five lists:
  - Drugs to avoid.
  - Drug–disease interactions.
  - Use with caution.
  - Drug–drug interactions.
  - Renal dose adjustment.
  - It is US-based, and some listed drugs aren’t used elsewhere.
- **STOPP/START version 3 (2023):** 133 STOPP criteria (drugs to stop) and 57 START criteria (omissions to fix), arranged by body system.
- **Specialised lists:**
  - STOPPFrail (frail, limited life expectancy).
  - STOPPFall (fall-risk drugs).
  - FORTA (grades A–D).
  - Country lists such as EU(7)-PIM and PRISCUS.
- **Burden scores:** Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden scale; Drug Burden Index (anticholinergic plus sedative load).

## Implicit (judgement-based) tools

- **Medication Appropriateness Index:** 10 questions per drug (indication, effectiveness, dose, directions, interactions, duplication, duration, cost).
- Structured medication review, for example the 7-step polypharmacy review.

## Deprescribing and decision support

- Deprescribing frameworks: CEASE, deprescribing.org algorithms.
- Electronic decision support builds these criteria into prescribing.

## Pros and cons

- **Explicit tools:** quick and reproducible, but not individualised, and country-specific.
- **Implicit tools:** tailored to the patient, but slow and dependent on expertise.
- Use both.

## One-liner

Beers and STOPP flag what to stop, START finds what’s missing, and judgement decides.
