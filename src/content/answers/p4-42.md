## Answer at a glance

1. Recognising the terminal phase.
2. Communication and decisions.
3. Physical symptoms and emergencies.
4. Care in the last days.
5. Psychological, social and spiritual needs.
6. Ethical and legal issues, family and bereavement.

## Recognising the terminal phase

- Progressive decline, poor performance status (ECOG 3–4), weight loss, falling intake, rising dependence, repeated admissions.
- The “surprise question”: would you be surprised if this patient died in the next 12 months? Tools such as SPICT help.
- **Last days:** bed-bound, drowsy, unable to take oral fluids or medicines, mottled skin, altered breathing.

## Communication and decisions

- Break news honestly (SPIKES), discuss prognosis, and agree goals of care.
- Handle collusion, where the family asks you not to tell the patient.
- Assess capacity, since brain metastases can impair it.
- Advance care planning: ceilings of treatment, do-not-resuscitate decisions, and the preferred place of care and death.

## Physical symptoms

- **Pain:** the WHO analgesic ladder, with regular oral morphine, breakthrough doses and laxatives.
- **Breathlessness:** opioids, a fan, oxygen if hypoxic, and drainage or pleurodesis for effusions.
- **Brain metastases:** dexamethasone for headache and raised pressure, levetiracetam for seizures, radiotherapy if appropriate.
- **Liver metastases:** capsular pain (steroids), jaundice and itch.
- **Nausea:** treat by cause, with haloperidol, metoclopramide, ondansetron or dexamethasone.
- **Other symptoms:** anorexia and cachexia, constipation, delirium, fatigue.

## Emergencies

- **Hypercalcaemia:** fluids and bisphosphonate.
- **Spinal cord compression:** high-dose dexamethasone, urgent MRI and radiotherapy.
- **Superior vena cava obstruction:** steroids, stenting or radiotherapy.

## The last days

- Stop non-essential drugs, such as statins and antihypertensives, and use the subcutaneous route.
- Prescribe anticipatory medicines for pain, breathlessness, agitation, nausea and respiratory secretions (glycopyrronium or hyoscine butylbromide).
- Mouth care and positioning.
- Artificial hydration only if it helps.
- Support the family to be present.

## Psychological

Anxiety, depression and fear; counselling, and treatment where needed.

## Social

Financial strain, caregiver burden, legal matters (a will, property), and support schemes.

## Spiritual

Meaning, faith and rituals, which are often central for Indian families.

## Ethical and legal

Advance directives; withdrawing futile treatment (the 2018 and 2023 Supreme Court guidelines); decisions about nutrition and hydration; avoiding both over-treatment and under-treatment; access to morphine.

## Family and bereavement

Support after the death, and watch for complicated grief.

## Team and setting

Palliative care team, home care or hospice, good coordination and documentation.

## One-liner

In terminal illness, the goal shifts from adding days to life to adding life to days.
