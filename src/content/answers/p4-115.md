## Diagnosis

- Ischaemic symptoms with persistent ST elevation in two contiguous leads: 1 mm or more, or in V2–V3, 2 mm or more in men 40 and over and 1.5 mm or more in women.
- Or a “STEMI equivalent”: posterior MI, de Winter pattern, Sgarbossa criteria in LBBB or pacing.

## Older adults

Atypical presentations (breathlessness, syncope, confusion), delays, higher mortality and more complications. **Age alone never rules out reperfusion.**

## First steps

ECG within 10 minutes; aspirin 150–300 mg; oxygen only if SpO₂ is below 90%; cautious IV opioid; nitrates unless hypotensive or RV infarct; monitoring with a defibrillator at hand.

## Reperfusion

- **Primary PCI** within 12 hours of onset, if achievable within 120 minutes of diagnosis. Use radial access (fewer bleeds, which matters in the elderly) and drug-eluting stents.
- **Fibrinolysis** if PCI isn’t possible in time:
  - Tenecteplase, at **half dose from age 75** (STREAM).
  - Then angiography in 2–24 hours, or rescue PCI if ST elevation hasn’t resolved by at least half.
- **Late presenters** (12–48 hours) with ongoing symptoms can still have PCI.

## Antithrombotics

- Aspirin plus ticagrelor or prasugrel. **Avoid prasugrel at 75 and over** (or use 5 mg). Clopidogrel with fibrinolysis or high bleeding risk.
- Heparin during PCI. With fibrinolysis, enoxaparin with no IV bolus and a reduced dose at 75 and over.

## After the event

- **Lipids:** high-intensity statin with or without ezetimibe (LDL below 55 mg/dL).
- **RAAS blockade:** ACE inhibitor or ARB; an MRA if EF is 40% or less.
- **Beta-blockers:** routine use brought no benefit when EF was preserved (REDUCE-AMI, 2024).
- **SGLT2 inhibitors:** not routine (EMPACT-MI, DAPA-MI).
- **Colchicine:** its role is now uncertain (CLEAR SYNERGY, 2024).
- **Complete revascularisation of non-culprit lesions,** physiology-guided, which benefited people 75 and over (FIRE).
- Cardiac rehabilitation.

## Complications

- Arrhythmias (VF, AV block in inferior MI).
- **Cardiogenic shock:** culprit-only PCI; the Impella device cut mortality in selected patients (DanGer Shock, 2024).
- **Mechanical rupture:** VSD, papillary muscle or free wall, commoner in older women.
- LV thrombus; pericarditis.

## Recent advances

- STEMI networks with pre-hospital ECG transmission; radial access; modern stents; imaging-guided PCI.
- The pharmaco-invasive strategy; half-dose tenecteplase in the elderly.
- Complete revascularisation in older adults; mechanical support in shock.
- The “occlusion MI” concept; AI-read ECGs.

## One-liner

In STEMI time is muscle at any age; primary PCI within 120 minutes, half-dose tenecteplase at 75 and over when the cath lab is far, and frailty, not birthdays, decides the rest.
