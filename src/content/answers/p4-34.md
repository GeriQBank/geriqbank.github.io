## What it is

An evidence-based, multicomponent programme to prevent delirium and functional decline in hospitalised older adults (Inouye, Yale; NEJM 1999).

## Who

Patients aged 70 and over with at least one risk factor: cognitive impairment, sleep deprivation, immobility, visual or hearing impairment, dehydration.

## Team

An elder life specialist, trained volunteers, a geriatric nurse specialist, a geriatrician, and the interdisciplinary team.

## Core interventions

One per risk factor:

- **Daily visitor and orientation:** orientation board, reorientation, communication.
- **Therapeutic activities:** cognitive stimulation (current events, reminiscence, word games).
- **Early mobilisation:** walking or active exercises three times a day, minimising immobilising equipment.
- **Vision protocol:** glasses, magnifiers, lighting.
- **Hearing protocol:** hearing aids, amplifiers, ear-wax removal.
- **Fluids and feeding help:** hydration, mealtime company.
- **Non-drug sleep protocol:** warm drink, relaxing music, massage, noise reduction, rescheduled medications and observations.

## Outcomes

Less incident delirium (about half the odds in a 2018 meta-analysis), fewer falls, less functional decline, shorter stays, lower costs.

## Adaptations

Post-operative and emergency department versions, and family-delivered HELP. Family bystanders make it especially feasible in Indian hospitals.

## One-liner

HELP proves delirium prevention is mostly glasses, hearing aids, water, walking and sleep, delivered every day by a team.
