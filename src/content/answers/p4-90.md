## Answer at a glance

## Definition (DSM-5-TR)

An acute (hours to days), fluctuating disturbance of attention and awareness, with another cognitive disturbance. It isn’t explained by pre-existing dementia or coma, and has a physiological cause.

## Scale

About a third of older medical inpatients, up to half or more after hip fracture surgery, and higher in ventilated ICU patients. **Most cases are missed.**

## Subtypes

Hyperactive, **hypoactive (commonest in the elderly, easily missed, worse prognosis)**, mixed.

## Aetiology (Inouye’s model)

Predisposing vulnerability multiplied by precipitating insults.

- **Predisposing:** age, dementia, frailty, sensory impairment, depression, multimorbidity, alcohol.
- **Precipitating:**
  - Infection, drugs, surgery, pain, dehydration, low sodium or high calcium, hypoxia.
  - Urinary retention, constipation, sleep loss, restraints, catheters, a change of environment.
  - Stroke, MI, withdrawal states.
- **Mnemonic PINCH ME:** pain, infection, nutrition, constipation, hydration, medication, environment.

## Pathogenesis

- Neurotransmitter imbalance: acetylcholine deficit, dopamine excess, GABA and glutamate changes.
- Neuroinflammation: cytokines crossing the blood–brain barrier, microglial activation.
- An aberrant stress response (cortisol); failing cerebral metabolism; network disconnection.

## Clinical features

Acute onset; fluctuation; **inattention** (months of the year backwards); disorganised thinking; altered alertness; visual hallucinations; sleep–wake reversal; emotional lability.

## Screening

- **4AT** (4 or more suggests delirium).
- **CAM:** acute onset with a fluctuating course, plus inattention, plus disorganised thinking or altered consciousness.
- CAM-ICU in intensive care.
- Always a collateral history.

## Differential diagnosis

- Dementia: insidious onset, attention preserved early, except Lewy body disease, which fluctuates and hallucinates.
- Depression: mimics hypoactive delirium.
- Late-onset psychosis.
- **Non-convulsive status epilepticus; Wernicke’s encephalopathy.**
- Stroke, especially Wernicke’s aphasia; transient global amnesia.

## Investigations

Guided by findings:

- **Bedside:** vital signs, glucose, bladder scan, rectal exam.
- **Bloods:** count, sodium, calcium, magnesium, renal and liver function, CRP.
- **For infection:** urinalysis only with urinary symptoms (asymptomatic bacteriuria is common); cultures; chest X-ray.
- **Heart and lungs:** ECG and troponin; blood gas if hypoxic.
- **Drug levels.**
- **CT head** for focal signs, falls or head injury on anticoagulants, or when no cause is found. Lumbar puncture if meningitis is possible; EEG if non-convulsive status is suspected.

## Management

- **Treat every cause** (usually more than one); review drugs (see the [drug-induced delirium answer](../../paper-3/183/)).
- **Non-drug, multicomponent care:**
  - Reorientation, familiar faces, glasses and hearing aids.
  - Hydration, nutrition, early mobilisation, undisturbed nights, pain control.
  - Bowel and bladder care; no restraints, avoid catheters; a calm, consistent setting.
- **Drugs only for severe distress or danger**, when non-drug measures fail:
  - Low-dose haloperidol (0.5 mg).
  - In Parkinson’s or Lewy body disease, avoid haloperidol; use low-dose quetiapine.
  - Antipsychotics don’t shorten delirium. **No benzodiazepines** except for alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal.
- Safety, capacity and family involvement.

## Prevention

Multicomponent programmes ([HELP](../34/)) prevent about a third of cases. No drug prophylaxis is recommended.

## Prognosis

Longer stays, falls, institutionalisation, death, and lasting cognitive decline or new dementia.

## One-liner

Delirium is an emergency of attention; find the causes (usually several), stop the drugs, and treat with people before pills.
