## Definition

RA starting at 60 or over (elderly-onset RA).

## How it differs from younger-onset RA

- A more equal sex ratio; more acute onset with fever, weight loss and fatigue.
- Large joints such as **shoulders (a PMR-like start)**.
- Higher ESR; more often seronegative.
- Sometimes an RS3PE-like picture with pitting oedema of the hands.

## Differentials

PMR, RS3PE, CPPD (“pseudo-RA”), gout, erosive osteoarthritis, paraneoplastic arthritis, **chikungunya arthritis** (India), hypothyroidism, haemochromatosis.

## Diagnosis

- 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria (6 or more of 10: joints, serology, acute-phase reactants, duration of 6 weeks or more).
- RF rises with age even in healthy people, so **anti-CCP is more specific**.
- Ultrasound or MRI for synovitis and erosions.

## Treatment (treat to target)

- **Methotrexate first:** dose for the kidneys (avoid if eGFR is below 30), give folic acid, and monitor counts and liver function. Combine with hydroxychloroquine or sulfasalazine.
- Short, low-dose steroid bridges, with bone protection.
- **Biologics:** TNF inhibitors; abatacept (a favourable infection profile in older adults); tocilizumab; rituximab.
- **JAK inhibitors:** caution at 65 and over with cardiovascular, VTE or cancer risk (ORAL Surveillance).
- **Before biologics:** screen for TB and hepatitis B, and vaccinate (influenza, pneumococcal, recombinant zoster).

## Comorbidities

Cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis, interstitial lung disease, falls, polypharmacy.

## One-liner

In older adults RA can begin like PMR; check anti-CCP, watch the small joints, and treat to target with methotrexate dosed for the kidneys.
