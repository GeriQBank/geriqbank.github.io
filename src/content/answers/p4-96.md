## What it is

A parasomnia in which REM sleep loses its normal muscle atonia, so patients act out vivid, often violent dreams (punching, kicking, shouting). Typically men over 50.

## Causes

- **Isolated RBD strongly predicts α-synucleinopathies.** Most patients develop Parkinson’s disease, Lewy body dementia or MSA within 10–15 years (roughly 6% a year).
- **Secondary causes:** antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, mirtazapine, tricyclics), alcohol withdrawal, narcolepsy, brainstem lesions.

## Features

- Episodes in the second half of the night; injuries to self or bed partner (falls from bed, fractures, even subdurals). The patient is often unaware.
- Prodromal clues: loss of smell, constipation, depression, postural hypotension.

## Diagnosis

- **Video-polysomnography** showing REM sleep without atonia, plus dream enactment (ICSD-3).
- Screening with the RBD questionnaire.
- **Mimics:** sleep apnoea (“pseudo-RBD”), NREM parasomnias, nocturnal seizures, periodic limb movements.

## Management

- **Bedroom safety:** the partner sleeps separately, mattress on the floor, remove sharp objects, padding.
- Review and switch antidepressants if possible.
- **Melatonin 3–12 mg at bedtime is first-line in older adults.** Clonazepam works but brings falls, sedation, confusion and worse apnoea.
- Rivastigmine where MCI or Lewy body dementia coexist.
- Counsel sensitively about neurodegeneration risk, and follow up for parkinsonism and cognitive change.

## One-liner

An older man who punches in his sleep may be dreaming of Parkinson’s years ahead; secure the bedroom, try melatonin, and follow him up.
