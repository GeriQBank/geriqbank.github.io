## Purpose

Screening, supporting diagnosis, staging and monitoring. Choose the tool by setting, time available, education and language.

## Brief screens (under 5 minutes)

- **Mini-Cog:** 3-word recall plus a clock; below 3 of 5 is abnormal.
- **Abbreviated Mental Test:** 10 items; below 8 is abnormal.
- **Others:** clock drawing, 6CIT, GPCOG.

## Standard tests

- **MMSE:** 30 points; below 24 is typical. It misses MCI and executive deficits and is education-biased.
- **MoCA:** 30 points; below 26 is abnormal, with 1 point added for 12 or fewer years of education. Better for MCI and executive function.
- **ACE-III:** 100 points, with cut-offs of 88 and 82.

## Low literacy and Indian settings

Hindi Mental State Examination, RUDAS.

## Informant and staging tools

- **Informant:** AD8 (2 or more is abnormal), IQCODE.
- **Staging:** Clinical Dementia Rating (0–3), Global Deterioration Scale and FAST (1–7).

## Pros and cons

- **MMSE:**
  - Pros: widely known, about 10 minutes, good for tracking change.
  - Cons: copyrighted; biased by education and language; misses MCI and executive deficits; floor effect in severe dementia.
- **MoCA:**
  - Pros: sensitive for MCI; covers executive and visuospatial skills; available in Hindi, Tamil and other languages.
  - Cons: now needs training and certification; less specific; still affected by education; 10–15 minutes.
- **Mini-Cog:**
  - Pros: 3 minutes, little education bias, good for primary care.
  - Cons: limited detail; not for staging.
- **AMT:**
  - Pros: quick, suits hospital wards.
  - Cons: few domains, heavy on orientation.
- **ACE-III:**
  - Pros: a detailed domain profile that helps separate Alzheimer’s from frontotemporal dementia.
  - Cons: 15–20 minutes and needs training.
- **HMSE and RUDAS:**
  - Pros: suitable for low literacy and fairer across cultures.
  - Cons: fewer validation studies; less sensitive to mild impairment.
- **Informant tools (AD8, IQCODE):**
  - Pros: capture change over time, with no education bias.
  - Cons: depend on a reliable, available informant.
- **Clock drawing:**
  - Pros: quick; tests executive and visuospatial function.
  - Cons: many scoring systems; affected by vision and motor problems.
- **For all tools:** no score diagnoses dementia by itself. Adjust for education, language and sensory loss, and exclude delirium first.

## Screen for delirium first

Use the 4AT or CAM before interpreting any cognitive score.

## Pitfalls

Poor vision or hearing, low education, language, depression and delirium all lower scores. Use formal neuropsychological testing when screens conflict.

## One-liner

Screening tests only screen; dementia is diagnosed by cognitive decline plus loss of function.
