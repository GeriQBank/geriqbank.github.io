## Answer at a glance

1. Define and grade it.
2. Why it is common in older adults.
3. Clinical features.
4. A four-step approach.
5. Management, with safe correction limits.

## Definition

Sodium below 135 mmol/L. Mild is 130–134, moderate 125–129, and profound below 125. Acute means under 48 hours; chronic means longer.

## Why it is common in older adults

Poorer free-water excretion, drugs, low solute intake (a “tea and toast” diet), and illnesses that trigger ADH.

**Culprit drugs:** thiazides (especially in older women), SSRIs, carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine, antipsychotics, PPIs, tramadol, desmopressin.

## Clinical features

Even mild chronic hyponatraemia causes unsteadiness, attention deficits, falls and fractures. Severe cases cause confusion, seizures and coma.

## Approach

1. **Confirm it’s hypotonic** (serum osmolality below 275 mOsm/kg). Exclude hyperglycaemia and pseudohyponatraemia from high lipids or paraproteins.
2. **Urine osmolality:** below 100 suggests primary polydipsia or low solute intake.
3. **Urine sodium:** below 30 mmol/L suggests low effective volume (dehydration, heart failure, cirrhosis). Above 30 suggests SIADH, diuretics, adrenal insufficiency, hypothyroidism or salt wasting.
4. **Volume status, TSH, morning cortisol and a drug review.**

## Management

- **Severe symptoms** (seizures, drowsiness): 150 mL of 3% saline over 20 minutes, repeated until sodium rises by 5 mmol/L.
- **Correct slowly:** no more than 10 mmol/L in 24 hours, or 8 in high-risk patients (alcohol, malnutrition, low potassium, liver disease), to avoid osmotic demyelination. If overcorrected, re-lower with 5% dextrose, with or without desmopressin.
- **Treat by cause:**
  - Stop culprit drugs.
  - Isotonic saline if hypovolaemic.
  - Fluid restriction and diuretics in heart failure or cirrhosis.
  - For SIADH: fluid restriction, oral urea, or salt with a loop diuretic.
  - Hydrocortisone or levothyroxine where those are the cause.

## One-liner

Check the sodium in every older person who falls, and correct it slowly.
