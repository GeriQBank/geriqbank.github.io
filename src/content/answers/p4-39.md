## Definitions

- **Active euthanasia:** a doctor deliberately ends a patient’s life, at their request, to relieve suffering.
- **Physician-assisted suicide:** a doctor provides the means, and the patient takes them.
- **Passive euthanasia** (as used in India): withholding or withdrawing life-sustaining treatment.
- **Not euthanasia:** stopping futile treatment, respecting a refusal, or palliative sedation for refractory symptoms (the doctrine of double effect).

## Around the world

Euthanasia or assisted dying is legal in countries such as the Netherlands, Belgium, Canada, Spain, Colombia and New Zealand, and in Australian states. Assisted suicide is legal in Switzerland and several US states.

## In India

Active euthanasia and assisted suicide are illegal, as abetment of suicide is a crime. Passive euthanasia is legal (Aruna Shanbaug 2011, Common Cause 2018, simplified in 2023).

## Arguments for

Autonomy, relief of unbearable suffering, dignity.

## Arguments against

The sanctity of life; the slippery slope; pressure on vulnerable people (old, disabled, poor) who feel like a burden; poor access to palliative care; uncertain prognosis; treatable depression; loss of trust in doctors.

## The geriatric approach

A request to die usually signals uncontrolled symptoms, depression, fear of being a burden, or loneliness. Explore it; treat the symptoms and any depression; involve palliative care; assess capacity; plan ahead; and respect a capable person’s refusal of treatment.

## One-liner

Behind most requests to die is a symptom, a fear or a depression that can be treated, so look for it first.
