## Answer at a glance

1. Definition, and excluding pseudohyperkalaemia.
2. Causes in older adults and diabetics.
3. Investigations, including the ECG.
4. Emergency treatment in four steps.
5. Chronic and mild hyperkalaemia.

## Definition

Potassium above 5.5 mmol/L; mild at 5.5–5.9, moderate at 6.0–6.4, severe at 6.5 or more.

## Exclude pseudohyperkalaemia first

Haemolysis, delayed processing, fist clenching, very high platelets or white cells. Repeat the sample, but don’t delay treatment if the ECG is abnormal.

## Causes in older adults and diabetics

- **Reduced excretion:** AKI or CKD.
  - Drugs: ACE inhibitors and ARBs, spironolactone, amiloride, NSAIDs, trimethoprim, heparin.
  - **Type 4 RTA** (low renin and aldosterone), which is common in diabetes.
  - Addison’s disease.
- **Shift out of cells:** insulin deficiency and hyperglycaemia (DKA, HHS), acidosis, beta-blockers, digoxin toxicity, tissue breakdown (rhabdomyolysis, tumour lysis, GI bleeding).
- **Intake:** potassium supplements, salt substitutes, and high-potassium foods such as bananas and coconut water in CKD.

## Investigations

- Repeat potassium.
- **ECG:** peaked T waves, flattened P waves, long PR, wide QRS, sine waves, then VF or asystole; bradycardia.
- Renal function, glucose, venous gas (pH, bicarbonate), CK, blood count, digoxin level.
- Urine potassium, and cortisol or renin/aldosterone if indicated.
- A full drug review.

## Emergency treatment (6.5 or more, or any ECG changes)

1. **Protect the heart:** 30 mL of IV calcium gluconate 10% over 5–10 minutes; repeat if the ECG changes persist.
2. **Shift potassium into cells:** 10 units of soluble insulin with 25 g glucose, then monitor glucose for 6 hours, as hypoglycaemia is common in the elderly and in CKD. Add nebulised salbutamol 10–20 mg (caution in heart disease), and bicarbonate only if acidotic.
3. **Remove potassium:** stop culprit drugs; give a loop diuretic if volume allows; use a binder (sodium zirconium cyclosilicate or patiromer); dialysis if refractory.
4. **Monitor:** potassium at 1, 2, 4, 6 and 24 hours, with cardiac monitoring.

## Chronic or mild hyperkalaemia

- Dietary advice, and no salt substitutes.
- Review drugs. In heart failure or CKD, reduce RAAS inhibitors, or add a binder, rather than stopping them.
- Diuretics, oral bicarbonate for acidosis, and fludrocortisone for type 4 RTA (watch blood pressure and heart failure).
- Better glucose control. SGLT2 inhibitors actually lower the risk of hyperkalaemia.

## One-liner

Calcium protects the heart, insulin buys time, but only removal fixes the potassium.
