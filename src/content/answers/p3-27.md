## Answer at a glance

## Core features (McKeith 2017)

Fluctuating cognition, recurrent well-formed visual hallucinations, REM sleep behaviour disorder, and parkinsonism. Attention, executive and visuospatial deficits come early.

## Supportive features

Severe sensitivity to antipsychotics, falls, syncope, autonomic failure, loss of smell.

## Biomarkers

Reduced dopamine transporter uptake on SPECT or PET, reduced cardiac MIBG uptake, and REM sleep without atonia on polysomnography.

## The one-year rule

Dementia before, or within a year of, parkinsonism means Lewy body dementia. Parkinsonism for more than a year before dementia means Parkinson’s disease dementia.

## Treatment

- Cholinesterase inhibitors help both cognition and hallucinations.
- Levodopa cautiously, as it can worsen hallucinations.
- **Avoid typical antipsychotics;** reactions can be fatal. If an antipsychotic is unavoidable, use low-dose quetiapine.
- Melatonin first for REM sleep behaviour disorder; manage orthostatic hypotension; prevent falls.

## Course

Progressive, usually faster than Alzheimer’s.

## Counselling the daughter

- It is mostly sporadic. First-degree relatives have only a slightly higher risk, and genetic testing isn’t routine.
- There’s no proven prevention, but brain-healthy habits (exercise, blood pressure control, hearing care, no smoking, staying socially active) lower overall dementia risk.
- Practically: expect fluctuations and hallucinations. Carry a note warning other doctors against antipsychotics, and plan care and support early.

## One-liner

In Lewy body dementia, the wrong antipsychotic can kill.
