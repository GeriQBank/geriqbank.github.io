## What it is

The commonest cause of vertigo in older adults. Displaced otoconia enter a semicircular canal, the posterior canal in most cases.

## Features

Brief spinning vertigo, under a minute, triggered by position changes such as rolling in bed or looking up. There is no hearing loss and no neurological signs.

## Diagnosis

- **Dix–Hallpike test:** after a few seconds’ delay, torsional up-beating nystagmus that lasts under a minute and fatigues on repeating.
- **Supine roll test** for the horizontal canal.

## Central red flags

Neurological signs, pure vertical or down-beating nystagmus, nystagmus with no delay or that doesn’t fatigue, or severe imbalance. Any of these needs imaging.

## Treatment

- **Repositioning manoeuvres:** Epley or Semont for the posterior canal; barbecue roll or Gufoni for the horizontal canal.
- Brandt–Daroff exercises at home, and vestibular rehabilitation for leftover unsteadiness.
- Avoid long-term vestibular sedatives, which cause falls.
- Check neck and back mobility before doing manoeuvres in older adults.

## Recurrence

Common, around 15% a year.

## One-liner

Treat BPPV with a manoeuvre, not a medicine.
