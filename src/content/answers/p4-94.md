## The newer classes

- SSRIs (see the [SSRI answer](../../paper-2/250/)).
- SNRIs: venlafaxine, duloxetine, desvenlafaxine.
- Mirtazapine; bupropion; trazodone; agomelatine.
- Vortioxetine and vilazodone; esketamine nasal spray.

## Advantages and shared risks

- **Advantages:** much safer than tricyclics in overdose, with far fewer anticholinergic effects.
- **Shared older-adult risks:** hyponatraemia, falls, bleeding, QT prolongation, serotonin syndrome, interactions.

## Drug by drug

- **Venlafaxine:** dose-related hypertension, harsh discontinuation symptoms, low sodium.
- **Duloxetine:** also treats neuropathic and osteoarthritis pain; nausea. Avoid if CrCl is below 30 or with liver disease.
- **Mirtazapine:** helps poor sleep and appetite, with few interactions and little sexual dysfunction; but sedation and falls.
- **Bupropion:** no weight gain or sexual dysfunction, and activating; but lowers the seizure threshold and can raise blood pressure.
- **Trazodone:** low doses for sleep; postural hypotension, sedation, rarely priapism.
- **Agomelatine:** improves sleep but needs liver tests; not recommended at 75 and over.
- **Vortioxetine:** well tolerated in trials in older adults, and may help cognitive symptoms; nausea.
- **Esketamine:** for treatment-resistant depression, given under supervision; causes dissociation and a blood pressure rise. Its trial in people over 65 missed its target.

## Principles

- Start low and go slow, but reach an adequate dose.
- Allow 4–8 weeks, up to 12 in older adults.
- Check sodium at 2–4 weeks; review falls.
- Continue for at least a year, longer if depression recurs.

## One-liner

Pick the antidepressant by its side effects: mirtazapine for the thin and sleepless, duloxetine for depression with pain, sertraline or escitalopram for most, and always check the sodium.
