## Background

- India had about 104 million people aged 60 and over in 2011 (8.6%), expected to reach about a fifth of the population by 2050.
- Multimorbidity and disability are rising, joint families are weakening, and geriatric services were scarce.
- **Policy basis:** Article 41 of the Constitution, the National Policy on Older Persons (1999), the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act (2007), and the UN Madrid Plan of Action on Ageing (2002).
- Launched by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare in 2010–11.

## Vision

Accessible, affordable, good-quality, comprehensive long-term care for older people, and an enabling “society for all ages” that promotes active and healthy ageing.

## Objectives

- Easy access to promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative care through community-based primary care.
- Early identification and management of older people’s health problems.
- Training of doctors, paramedics and family caregivers.
- A referral chain from the community up to regional centres.
- Convergence with other programmes, departments and NGOs.

## Services at each level

- **Sub-centre (Health and Wellness Centre):** health education, home visits to bed-bound older people by trained workers, simple aids, support groups, referral.
- **PHC:** a weekly geriatric clinic run by a trained medical officer, with screening, assessment and basic medicines.
- **CHC:** a twice-weekly geriatric clinic and a rehabilitation unit (physiotherapy).
- **District hospital:** a geriatric clinic, a **10-bed geriatric ward**, rehabilitation, laboratory support, and onward referral.
- **Regional Geriatric Centres (19, in medical colleges):** geriatric OPD, a 30-bed ward, specialist care, MD training and research.
- **National Centres for Ageing (2):** AIIMS New Delhi and Madras Medical College, Chennai, for advanced care, training and research.
- Elderly care is now also a service package in Ayushman Arogya Mandirs.

## Expected outcomes

Better access, earlier diagnosis, less disability, trained staff, public awareness, and national data. LASI was carried out under NPHCE.

## As doctor in charge of a district hospital

1. **Access:** a separate registration counter and queue for older people, ramps, wheelchairs, seating, clear signs, and a geriatric clinic on fixed days.
2. **The ward:** make the 10 beds elder-friendly: low beds with rails, anti-skid floors, good lighting, grab bars, commodes and call bells, with delirium and falls protocols.
3. **People:** a nodal officer; training in comprehensive geriatric assessment for doctors, nurses and physiotherapists; a weekly team meeting.
4. **Rehabilitation:** physiotherapy and occupational therapy, and aids through Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana camps.
5. **Links:** down to CHCs, PHCs and the ANM/ASHA home-visit teams; up to the Regional Geriatric Centre; palliative care integration.
6. **Money and data:** a budget through the NHM Programme Implementation Plan, plus monthly indicators: OPD numbers, admissions, falls, referrals.
7. **Community:** camps on 1 October (International Day of Older Persons) and 15 June (World Elder Abuse Awareness Day); partnerships with NGOs; linking patients to PM-JAY, pensions and Elderline (14567).

## One-liner

NPHCE is a ladder of care, with home visits at the bottom, a geriatric ward in every district, and national centres at the top.
