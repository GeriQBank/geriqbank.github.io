## Answer at a glance

1. Risk factors.
2. Diagnosis, with the age-adjusted D-dimer.
3. Treatment and its duration.
4. Prevention in hospital.

## Risk factors (Virchow’s triad)

Immobility, hospital stay, surgery (hip fracture, joint replacement), cancer, stroke, heart failure, previous VTE, obesity, thrombophilia, oestrogens, dehydration.

## Diagnosis

- Wells score plus D-dimer. Over 50, use the age-adjusted cut-off: age × 10 µg/L.
- Compression ultrasound.

## Treatment

- **DOACs:** apixaban and rivaroxaban can start straight away; dabigatran and edoxaban need 5 days of heparin first.
- **Cancer:** LMWH or a suitable DOAC.
- **Warfarin** (INR 2–3) where DOACs are unsuitable.
- Dose for the kidneys.
- **Duration:** 3 months if provoked by a temporary factor; consider extended treatment if unprovoked, weighing bleeding risk; for as long as cancer is active.
- Falls alone don’t rule out anticoagulation.
- Thrombolysis only for limb-threatening clots. An IVC filter only when anticoagulation is impossible.

## Prevention

- Risk-assess every admission (Padua for medical patients, Caprini for surgical).
- Early mobilisation.
- LMWH, fondaparinux or unfractionated heparin prophylaxis; DOACs after hip or knee replacement.
- Intermittent pneumatic compression when bleeding risk is high. After stroke, use this rather than stockings.

## One-liner

Use the age-adjusted D-dimer after 50; it saves needless scans.
