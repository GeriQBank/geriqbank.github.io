## Answer at a glance

1. Why it matters, and the anorexia of ageing.
2. Causes.
3. Evaluation.
4. Management.

## Significance

Loss of 5% or more of body weight over 6–12 months predicts disability and death. Her loss over 3 months is more concerning.

## Anorexia of ageing

Reduced taste and smell, early satiety, and hormonal shifts (CCK, ghrelin, leptin) make older adults vulnerable.

## Causes

“MEALS ON WHEELS”, or the 9 Ds: dentition, dysgeusia, dysphagia, diarrhoea, depression, disease, dementia, dysfunction, drugs.

- **Cancer** (about a quarter): GI, lung, haematological.
- **Non-malignant GI:** peptic ulcer, malabsorption, chronic pancreatitis, dysphagia, poor dentition.
- **Psychiatric:** **depression, the commonest reversible cause**; bereavement, anxiety, alcohol; dementia.
- **Endocrine:** hyperthyroidism (often “apathetic”), uncontrolled diabetes, adrenal insufficiency, hypercalcaemia.
- **Organ failure:** heart (cardiac cachexia), COPD, CKD, liver.
- **Infections:** TB (India), HIV, endocarditis.
- **Drugs:** metformin, GLP-1 agonists, digoxin, SSRIs, cholinesterase inhibitors, levodopa, antibiotics, opioids and NSAIDs (dyspepsia), polypharmacy itself.
- **Neurological:** Parkinson’s disease, stroke with dysphagia.
- **Inflammatory:** PMR/GCA, RA.
- **Social:** poverty, isolation, unable to shop or cook, neglect or elder abuse.
- **Unexplained** in about a quarter.

## Evaluation

- **Confirm** with documented weights, BMI and MNA-SF.
- **History:**
  - Appetite: low, or normal/high (hyperthyroidism, malabsorption, diabetes).
  - Diet recall, dentition, swallowing, GI symptoms.
  - Mood (GDS), cognition, function (shopping, cooking).
  - Alcohol and drugs; finances and support; fever and night sweats.
- **Examination:** mouth, thyroid, nodes, breasts, abdomen, rectal exam, muscle wasting, oedema, mood and cognition.
- **First tests:**
  - Blood count, ESR/CRP, electrolytes, calcium, glucose/HbA1c.
  - Renal and liver function, albumin, TSH.
  - Urinalysis, HIV, chest X-ray, FIT, abdominal ultrasound.
- **Then guided by findings:** endoscopy and colonoscopy, CT chest, abdomen and pelvis. If the examination and first tests are normal, cancer is unlikely; watchful waiting with regular weights is reasonable.

## Management

- **Treat the cause:** depression (mirtazapine helps appetite and sleep), dental care, swallowing therapy, deprescribing.
- **Nutrition:**
  - Oral energy-protein supplements and fortified foods.
  - Favourite foods, small frequent meals, help with eating, eating with others, meal delivery.
  - **Liberalise restrictive diabetic or low-salt diets.**
  - Replace vitamin D and B12.
- **Resistance exercise** to rebuild muscle.
- **Avoid appetite stimulants** such as megestrol (Beers: thrombosis and death); use mirtazapine only if she is depressed.
- Tube feeding only for selected reversible situations, not in advanced dementia.
- Weigh her monthly.

## One-liner

In an older person weight loss is a symptom, not a diagnosis; look for depression, cancer and the drug chart, and feed while you search.
