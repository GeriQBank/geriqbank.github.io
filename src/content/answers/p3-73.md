## Answer at a glance

1. Causes and pathophysiology.
2. Presentation, including in older adults.
3. Diagnosis.
4. Risk stratification.
5. Treatment, including reperfusion and duration.
6. Older adults.

## Causes

Virchow’s triad. Risk factors as for DVT: immobility, surgery (hip fracture), cancer, stroke, heart failure, previous VTE, obesity, thrombophilia, oestrogens, dehydration. Most emboli come from leg veins.

## Pathophysiology

The sudden rise in RV afterload dilates the RV and makes it ischaemic, shifts the septum and starves the LV, causing hypotension. V/Q mismatch causes hypoxaemia.

## Presentation

- Breathlessness (commonest), pleuritic pain, haemoptysis, syncope, tachycardia, hypoxia, DVT signs.
- **In older adults:** syncope, confusion, falls or unexplained tachypnoea.

## Diagnosis

- **Clinical probability:** Wells or revised Geneva. (The PERC rule doesn’t apply over 50.)
- **D-dimer** if not high probability, with the **age-adjusted cut-off (age × 10 µg/L) over 50** (ADJUST-PE), or the YEARS algorithm.
- **CT pulmonary angiography;** a V/Q scan if contrast is unsafe; leg ultrasound; **bedside echo if unstable**.
- **Supporting tests:**
  - ECG: tachycardia, RV strain, new RBBB, S1Q3T3.
  - Chest X-ray: often normal.
  - Blood gas: hypoxaemia, hypocapnia.

## Risk stratification (ESC 2019)

- **High risk** (shock): reperfusion.
- **Intermediate:** sPESI 1 or more, or RV dysfunction or raised troponin. Intermediate-high (both) means monitoring and rescue thrombolysis if they deteriorate.
- **Low risk** (sPESI 0): early discharge. Note that age over 80 scores a point, so octogenarians are never “low” by sPESI.

## Treatment

- **Support:** oxygen, cautious fluids (an overfilled RV fails), noradrenaline.
- **Anticoagulate immediately** if probability is intermediate or high, even before imaging.
  - LMWH, or a DOAC: apixaban or rivaroxaban from the start; dabigatran or edoxaban after 5 days of heparin.
  - Unfractionated heparin if reperfusion is likely, or with severe renal failure.
- **Reperfusion for high-risk PE:**
  - Alteplase 100 mg over 2 hours. **Bleeding rises with age** (intracranial haemorrhage about 2% in PEITHO).
  - Catheter-directed therapy or surgical embolectomy if thrombolysis is contraindicated.
- **IVC filter** only if anticoagulation is impossible.
- **Duration:** 3 months if provoked; extended (reduced-dose DOAC possible) if unprovoked; as long as cancer is active.

## Older adults

Dose DOACs for the kidneys, review interacting drugs, and don’t withhold anticoagulation for frailty alone. Persistent breathlessness at 3 months calls for an echo and V/Q scan to look for CTEPH.

## One-liner

In older adults PE can masquerade as syncope or confusion; use the age-adjusted D-dimer, image with CTPA, and anticoagulate without delay.
