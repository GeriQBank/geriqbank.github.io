## Answer at a glance

1. Benefits.
2. WHO 2020 recommendations and types of exercise.
3. Possible harms, and pre-exercise screening.
4. The exercise prescription, and the verdict.

## Benefits

- Lower mortality, heart disease, diabetes and hypertension.
- Exercise cuts the rate of falls by about a quarter.
- Stronger muscle and bone.
- Better cognition, mood, sleep and independence, and frailty can reverse.

## WHO 2020 advice for people 65 and over

- 150–300 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity (or 75–150 minutes vigorous).
- Strength training on 2 or more days a week.
- Balance and strength training on 3 or more days a week to prevent falls.
- Less sitting; any activity beats none.

## Types

Aerobic (walking, cycling, swimming), resistance (bands, weights, chair rises), balance (tai chi, yoga), flexibility.

## Possible harms, mostly avoidable

- Muscle and joint injury, and falls while exercising.
- Rare cardiac events with sudden vigorous exertion in unfit people.
- Hypoglycaemia on insulin or sulfonylureas.
- Dehydration and heat illness.
- Vertebral fracture from forward bending in severe osteoporosis.

## Screening

Most people don’t need a stress test before moderate exercise. Check for symptoms and unstable conditions. Don’t exercise with unstable angina, uncontrolled arrhythmia, decompensated heart failure, symptomatic severe aortic stenosis, blood pressure over 180/110, or an acute illness.

## Prescription

Start low and progress slowly, supervised at first; warm up and cool down; fluids and good footwear; check blood sugar in diabetes.

## Verdict

The benefits far outweigh the risks.

## One-liner

For older adults, the most dangerous amount of exercise is none.
