## Why older adults are vulnerable

- Less total body water.
- Weaker thirst; poorer urine concentration and sodium conservation; lower GFR.
- Lower renin and aldosterone (a hyperkalaemia risk).
- Drugs, dependence on others for fluids, cognitive impairment.

## Water and sodium

### Dehydration and hypernatraemia (sodium above 145)

- **Causes:** poor intake (dementia, dysphagia, dependence), fever, diarrhoea, diuretics, hyperglycaemia, lithium-induced diabetes insipidus.
- **Presentation:** confusion, falls, AKI and constipation. Clinical signs are unreliable in the elderly; serum osmolality above 300 is the best marker.
- **Treatment:** oral fluids, 5% dextrose or 0.45% saline, or subcutaneous fluids. Lower chronic sodium by no more than 10–12 mmol/L a day.

### Hyponatraemia

See the [hyponatraemia answer](../83/). The key points are to stop thiazides and SSRIs, correct by no more than 10 a day (8 if high-risk), and give 3% saline for severe symptoms.

### Overload

Heart failure, CKD, too much IV fluid.

## Potassium

### Hypokalaemia (below 3.5)

- **Causes:** diuretics, diarrhoea, vomiting, laxatives, poor intake, low magnesium, steroids.
- **Effects:** weakness, ileus, arrhythmias (U waves), and worse digoxin toxicity.
- **Treatment:** oral potassium chloride. Give IV if below 2.5 or symptomatic, at no more than 10 mmol/h peripherally, and correct magnesium.

### Hyperkalaemia

See the [hyperkalaemia answer](../../paper-4/139/): calcium gluconate, insulin–glucose, salbutamol, binders, dialysis.

## Calcium

### Hypercalcaemia

See the [hypercalcaemia answer](../87/).

### Hypocalcaemia

- **Causes:** vitamin D deficiency, after neck surgery, low magnesium, CKD, and after zoledronic acid or denosumab.
- **Features:** tetany, Chvostek’s and Trousseau’s signs, and a long QT.
- **Treatment:** IV calcium gluconate if symptomatic, then oral calcium and vitamin D.

## Magnesium

Low magnesium comes from long-term PPIs, diuretics, alcohol and diarrhoea. It makes low potassium and calcium resistant to treatment and causes torsades. Give IV magnesium sulphate if severe.

## Phosphate

- **Low phosphate** follows refeeding, alcohol and DKA treatment, causing weakness, respiratory failure, rhabdomyolysis and haemolysis.
- **High phosphate** occurs in CKD and needs binders.

## IV fluid principles

NICE’s 5 Rs: resuscitation, routine maintenance, replacement, redistribution, reassessment.

- **Maintenance:** 25–30 mL/kg/day of water, or 20–25 in older, frail, heart failure or CKD patients. Add about 1 mmol/kg/day each of sodium, potassium and chloride, and 50–100 g of glucose.
- Prefer balanced crystalloids and avoid overload.
- Subcutaneous fluids (hypodermoclysis) for mild dehydration.
- Daily weights, electrolytes and fluid charts.

## One-liner

In older adults, water and salt go wrong quietly; check the drugs, weigh the patient, and correct sodium slowly.
