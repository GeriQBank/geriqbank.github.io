## Definition

The ability to make a particular decision at a particular time. It is decision-specific and can fluctuate. Capacity is a clinical judgement; “competence” is the legal term.

## The four abilities (Appelbaum and Grisso)

1. **Understand** the relevant information.
2. **Appreciate** how it applies to one’s own situation.
3. **Reason** by comparing options and consequences.
4. **Communicate** a consistent choice.

## Principles

Presume capacity; the bar rises with the risk of the decision; an unwise choice isn’t proof of incapacity.

## Factors affecting it

Dementia and delirium, depression, psychosis or mania, sensory loss, language and literacy, sedating drugs, pain, fatigue, time of day, stress, and pressure from others.

## Assessment

- **Optimise first:** treat delirium; use hearing aids, glasses and an interpreter; choose a quiet room and the best time of day; use simple language, visual aids and teach-back.
- Interview around the four abilities, with open questions about this specific decision.
- Tools such as MacCAT-T and the Aid to Capacity Evaluation help. An MMSE score alone doesn’t decide capacity.
- Document the questions, answers and conclusion; reassess if capacity fluctuates; ask psychiatry in difficult cases.

## Law in India

The Mental Healthcare Act 2017 defines capacity (understanding, appreciating consequences, communicating). It states that a decision isn’t incapable just because others think it unwise, and it allows advance directives and nominated representatives. Without capacity, a surrogate decides in the person’s best interests, by the least restrictive option.

## One-liner

Capacity is about the decision, not the diagnosis.
