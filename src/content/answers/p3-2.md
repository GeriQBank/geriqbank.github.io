## Answer at a glance

1. Why function matters.
2. The three levels of activities of daily living.
3. Basic ADL, then instrumental ADL scales.
4. Performance-based measures.

## Why it matters

Function predicts outcomes such as hospital stay, placement and death better than a list of diagnoses, and it sets rehabilitation goals.

## Levels

Basic ADL (self-care), instrumental ADL (living independently), advanced ADL (social and work roles).

## Basic ADL

- **Katz Index:** bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, continence, feeding.
- **Barthel Index:** 10 items, scored 0–100; the standard in stroke and rehabilitation.

## Instrumental ADL

The Lawton–Brody scale covers telephone, shopping, cooking, housekeeping, laundry, transport, medicines and finances. IADLs fail first and are sensitive to early cognitive decline.

## Performance-based measures

- **Gait speed:** below 0.8 m/s predicts poor outcomes.
- **Timed Up and Go:** 13.5 s or more suggests fall risk.
- Short Physical Performance Battery (0–12), handgrip, 30-second chair stand, 6-minute walk.
- **Balance:** Tinetti and Berg scales.

## Global and specialty scales

Functional Independence Measure (18 items, for rehabilitation); Karnofsky and ECOG (oncology).

## Practical tips

Observe rather than only ask; use an informant when cognition is impaired; adapt IADLs culturally, since some tasks were never part of a person’s role; watch for ceiling and floor effects.

## One-liner

Ask about money and medicines early, because they slip before bathing and dressing.
