## Answer at a glance

1. Causes, led by bacterial overgrowth.
2. Presentation.
3. Investigations.
4. Treatment by cause.

## Causes

- **Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth**, the commonest in the elderly: low acid (PPIs), diverticula, strictures, poor motility, diabetes.
- Late-onset coeliac disease.
- **Pancreatic exocrine insufficiency:** chronic pancreatitis, cancer, diabetes, surgery.
- Bile acid malabsorption.
- **Drugs:** metformin (B12), orlistat, colchicine, olmesartan enteropathy.
- Mesenteric ischaemia, post-gastrectomy, lactose intolerance, giardiasis, Whipple’s disease, amyloidosis, lymphoma.

## Presentation (often subtle)

Weight loss, anaemia, diarrhoea or steatorrhoea, bloating, osteomalacia, neuropathy, oedema, bruising (vitamin K).

## Investigations

- Blood count, iron, folate, B12, albumin, calcium, vitamin D, INR, magnesium.
- Faecal elastase.
- Coeliac serology (tTG-IgA with total IgA), then duodenal biopsy.
- Glucose or lactulose breath test for bacterial overgrowth.
- SeHCAT, or a trial of a bile acid binder.
- CT pancreas and endoscopy with biopsies.

## Treatment

- **Overgrowth:** rifaximin, and stop the PPI if possible.
- **Coeliac:** a gluten-free diet.
- **Pancreatic insufficiency:** enzymes with meals, starting around 25,000–50,000 lipase units.
- **Bile acid malabsorption:** colesevelam or cholestyramine.
- Replace deficiencies, involve a dietitian, and protect bone.

## One-liner

Bloating and low B12 in an older person on a PPI suggest bacterial overgrowth.
