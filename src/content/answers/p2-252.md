## Definition (Rome IV)

Bothersome postprandial fullness, early satiation, epigastric pain or epigastric burning, with no structural cause on investigation, including endoscopy. Symptoms for the last 3 months, starting at least 6 months before diagnosis.

## Subtypes

- **Postprandial distress syndrome:** fullness or early satiation.
- **Epigastric pain syndrome:** pain or burning.
- They often overlap.

## Mechanisms

Poor gastric accommodation, delayed emptying, visceral hypersensitivity, low-grade duodenal inflammation (eosinophils, mast cells), H. pylori, post-infectious change, and the gut–brain axis.

## In older adults, “functional” must be earned

- **New dyspepsia at 60 or over needs endoscopy** (ACG/CAG).
- **Other alarm features:** weight loss, dysphagia, persistent vomiting, bleeding or anaemia, a mass, family history of GI cancer.
- **Review drugs:** NSAIDs, aspirin, bisphosphonates, iron, potassium, metformin, GLP-1 agonists, digoxin, theophylline.
- **Exclude:** biliary pain, pancreatic disease, angina, diabetic gastroparesis.

## Evaluation

- Blood count.
- Test and treat H. pylori if young without alarm features; otherwise endoscopy with biopsies.
- Ultrasound for biliary pain; ECG if symptoms come with exertion.

## Management

- Explanation and reassurance; small, lower-fat meals; stop culprit drugs.
- Eradicate H. pylori if present (it helps about 1 in 12).
- A PPI for 4–8 weeks, especially for pain-predominant symptoms.
- **Prokinetics** for postprandial distress: itopride, or acotiamide where available. Domperidone needs QT caution, and metoclopramide shouldn’t be used long-term (tardive dyskinesia).
- **Neuromodulators:** low-dose amitriptyline (but anticholinergic in the elderly), or mirtazapine if weight loss and poor sleep coexist.
- CBT or gut-directed hypnotherapy.
- Review long-term PPIs (C. difficile, fractures, low B12 and magnesium).

## One-liner

In older adults “functional” is earned only once the endoscope and the drug chart are clean.
