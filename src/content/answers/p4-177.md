## Answer at a glance

1. Mechanism.
2. Drugs.
3. Benefits beyond glucose.
4. Adverse effects and contraindications.
5. Use in older adults.

## Mechanism

Incretin mimetics:

- Glucose-dependent insulin release and glucagon suppression.
- Slower gastric emptying and central satiety, causing weight loss.

## Drugs

- Exenatide, liraglutide (daily), dulaglutide (weekly).
- Semaglutide: weekly injection or daily tablet.
- The dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist tirzepatide (weekly); both it and semaglutide are now marketed in India.

## Benefits

- HbA1c falls by about 1–1.5%, with little hypoglycaemia on its own.
- **Weight loss:** about 15% with semaglutide 2.4 mg (STEP-1), about 20% with tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1).
- **Heart:** fewer major cardiovascular events in diabetes with cardiovascular disease (LEADER, SUSTAIN-6, REWIND), and in obesity without diabetes (SELECT, 20%).
- **Kidneys:** FLOW (semaglutide).
- **HFpEF with obesity:** STEP-HFpEF, SUMMIT.
- **Obstructive sleep apnoea:** tirzepatide.

## Adverse effects

- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea or constipation (reduced by slow dose escalation), causing dehydration and AKI.
- Gallstones; rarely pancreatitis.
- Worsening retinopathy with rapid glucose falls.
- Aspiration risk during anaesthesia, from retained stomach contents.
- A very rare optic neuropathy (NAION) signal.
- **Contraindicated** with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2.

## Older adults

- **The weight lost includes muscle,** so avoid in the underweight, frail or malnourished. Combine with protein and resistance exercise.
- GI effects can tip a frail person into poor intake.
- Weigh cost and injection dexterity.
- The low hypoglycaemia risk is a real advantage for robust older adults with cardiovascular disease or obesity.

## One-liner

GLP-1 agonists protect the heart and kidneys and melt weight, but in a frail older adult the weight they melt may be muscle.
