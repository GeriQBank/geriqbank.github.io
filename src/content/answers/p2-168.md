## Answer at a glance

## Normal ageing of sleep

Less total and deep sleep, more awakenings, earlier sleep and waking times, and more napping. **Insomnia itself isn’t normal ageing.**

## Common disorders

Insomnia, sleep apnoea, restless legs, REM sleep behaviour disorder, circadian rhythm disorders, nocturia.

## Insomnia

Difficulty falling or staying asleep, or early waking, on 3 or more nights a week for 3 months or more, with daytime impairment.

## Approach for a recently retired 65-year-old

- **A 2-week sleep diary**, plus the partner’s account.
- **Lifestyle:** naps, caffeine, alcohol, screens, and the loss of routine, purpose and structure after retirement.
- **Mood:** depression, anxiety, grief (PHQ-9, GAD-7).
- **Physical causes:** pain, nocturia, snoring or apnoeas (STOP-BANG), restless legs.
- **Drugs:** night-time diuretics, beta-blockers, steroids, SSRIs, decongestants.
- **Sleep study** if apnoea or REM behaviour disorder is suspected.

## Management

- **CBT for insomnia first:** sleep restriction, stimulus control, sleep hygiene, cognitive work, relaxation. It works well in older adults, including digital versions.
- **Rebuild a routine:**
  - A fixed wake time and morning daylight.
  - Daytime activity, social life and exercise (not late).
  - Short early naps only.
  - No alcohol as a sleeping aid.
- **Treat causes:** depression, pain, evening fluids for nocturia, CPAP for apnoea, iron for restless legs if ferritin is below 75.
- **Drugs, short-term only:**
  - **Avoid** benzodiazepines and Z-drugs (falls, confusion, fractures) and sedating antihistamines.
  - **Options:** prolonged-release melatonin 2 mg, low-dose doxepin (3–6 mg), or an orexin antagonist (suvorexant, lemborexant, daridorexant).

## One-liner

Retirement took away the alarm clock, not the need for one; fix the wake time, add daylight and activity, and use CBT-I before pills.
