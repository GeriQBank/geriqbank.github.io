## Answer at a glance

1. Definitions, including lower fever thresholds in frail older adults.
2. Causes in older adults.
3. Evaluation: history, examination, and first- and second-line tests.
4. Management, and when empirical treatment is justified.
5. Prognosis.

## Definitions

- **Classic (Petersdorf–Beeson 1961; revised by Durack and Street 1991):** temperature over 38.3 °C on several occasions for more than 3 weeks, undiagnosed after 3 days in hospital or 3 outpatient visits.
- **Other categories:** nosocomial, neutropenic and HIV-associated.
- **In frail older adults, fever runs lower:** a single oral reading over 37.8 °C, repeated readings over 37.2 °C, or a rise over 1.1 °C above baseline.

## Causes in older adults

These differ from the young:

- **Infection (about a third):**
  - TB, especially extrapulmonary or miliary in India.
  - Intra-abdominal abscess, endocarditis, vertebral osteomyelitis, prosthesis infection, prostatitis.
  - In India also: enteric fever, scrub typhus, brucellosis, malaria, leishmaniasis.
- **Inflammatory (about a quarter to a third):** **giant cell arteritis/PMR, the commonest in the elderly**, other vasculitis, late-onset RA, sarcoidosis.
- **Cancer (15–20%):** lymphoma, leukaemia and MDS, renal cell carcinoma, hepatoma, colon cancer, metastases.
- **Miscellaneous:** drug fever (beta-lactams, anticonvulsants, allopurinol), PE/DVT, thyroiditis, haematoma, pseudogout.
- **No diagnosis (10–20%):** usually a benign course.

## Evaluation

- **Confirm the fever** with a chart and exclude factitious fever.
- **History:** every drug (including over-the-counter), TB contact, travel, animals, dental work, prostheses and valves.
- **Repeated examination:** temporal arteries, fundi, teeth and sinuses, murmurs, nodes, liver and spleen, joints, skin, prostate, pressure sores.

## First-line tests

- Blood count and smear, ESR/CRP, LFT, renal function, LDH, CK.
- Urinalysis and culture; 3 blood cultures before antibiotics; chest X-ray.
- ANA, RF, serum electrophoresis, HIV and hepatitis serology, ferritin.
- Sputum AFB/GeneXpert.
- Local infections: malaria test, scrub typhus IgM, dengue, leptospira and brucella serology.

## Second-line tests

- CT chest, abdomen and pelvis.
- Echo (TOE if endocarditis is possible).
- Temporal artery ultrasound or biopsy.
- **FDG-PET/CT,** often the most useful next step.
- Bone marrow aspiration, biopsy and culture (TB, lymphoma, leishmania, brucella).
- Liver or lymph node biopsy, excising the node where possible.

## Management

- **Avoid empirical antibiotics or steroids in a stable patient,** because they mask the diagnosis.
- **Exceptions:**
  - Unstable or neutropenic patients get empirical antimicrobials.
  - Suspected GCA threatening vision gets steroids at once.
  - An empirical trial of anti-TB treatment is reasonable in selected patients once biopsies are done.
- Stop non-essential drugs; drug fever settles within about 72 hours.
- Support hydration and nutrition, and prevent delirium.

## Prognosis

Worse with older age and cancer; undiagnosed FUO usually does well.

## One-liner

In older adults FUO is rarely exotic: think TB, abscess, endocarditis, GCA and lymphoma, and chase clues rather than order every test.
