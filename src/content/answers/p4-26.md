## Answer at a glance

1. Definition, and who benefits.
2. Models of home-based care.
3. Components.
4. Benefits and challenges.

## Definition

Health and social care given at home: medical, nursing, rehabilitation, palliative and personal care.

## Who benefits

Housebound or bed-bound older people, frailty, advanced dementia, recovery after discharge, end-of-life care, and chronic diseases needing monitoring.

## Models

- Home-based primary care and home visits.
- Hospital at home for selected acute illness.
- Home rehabilitation after stroke, fracture or surgery.
- Home palliative care. Kerala’s community-run Neighbourhood Network in Palliative Care is a well-known Indian model.
- NPHCE home visits by trained health workers.
- Teleconsultation, such as eSanjeevani, to support care at home.

## Components

- Comprehensive assessment at home, and supervised medicines.
- Nursing (wounds, catheters, feeding tubes), physiotherapy and nutrition.
- **Equipment:** a hospital bed, air mattress, commode, wheelchair.
- Home safety changes, caregiver training, social support, and an emergency plan.

## Benefits

Most older people prefer it; it means fewer admissions and readmissions, and less delirium, falls and hospital infection; it often costs less and preserves dignity.

## Challenges

Caregiver burden, too few trained staff, coordination, out-of-pocket costs, quality standards, and slower access in emergencies.

## One-liner

Home is the ward older people prefer, and with the right team it’s often the safest one too.
