## Answer at a glance

1. Define reversible dementia.
2. List the causes.
3. Name the investigations that rule them out.
4. Treat the cause and reassess.

## Definition

Cognitive impairment caused by a treatable condition. Early treatment can halt or improve the decline, though full reversal is uncommon.

## Causes

- **Drugs:** anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, opioids, antiepileptics.
- **Depression.**
- **Endocrine and nutritional:** hypothyroidism, B12 or thiamine deficiency.
- **Structural:** normal pressure hydrocephalus, chronic subdural haematoma, benign tumours such as a frontal meningioma.
- **Infection:** neurosyphilis, HIV.
- **Metabolic:** hyponatraemia, hypercalcaemia, liver or kidney failure.
- **Other:** obstructive sleep apnoea, alcohol.

## Investigations that rule them out

- **Blood tests:** blood count, sodium, calcium, glucose, renal and liver function, TSH, B12 and folate.
- Syphilis and HIV serology where there is risk.
- CT or MRI brain.
- EEG if cognition fluctuates or seizures occur.
- Lumbar puncture for suspected infection or an NPH tap test.
- Sleep study for suspected obstructive sleep apnoea.

## Management

Treat the cause: stop the culprit drug, start thyroxine or B12 replacement, shunt selected NPH after a positive tap test, drain a chronic subdural haematoma, use CPAP for sleep apnoea, or treat depression. Reassess cognition at 3–6 months.

<section class="traps">

## Examiner’s traps

- Calling it “pseudodementia” and stopping there, when depression and dementia often coexist
- Missing NPH by not examining gait, which usually fails first

</section>

## One-liner

Check TSH and B12 in every new cognitive complaint.
