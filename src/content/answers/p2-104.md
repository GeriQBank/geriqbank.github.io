## Definition

Raised TSH with normal free T4. Mild when TSH is 4.5–9.9, severe at 10 or above.

## Burden

Common, affecting up to about 15% of older women.

## TSH rises with age

The upper reference limit is around 7.5 mIU/L above 80, so a mild rise may be normal ageing.

## Causes

Hashimoto’s (anti-TPO positive), recovering thyroiditis, amiodarone, lithium, previous radioiodine or surgery, recovery from illness, assay interference.

## Evaluation

- Repeat TSH and free T4 after 6–12 weeks, because many cases are transient.
- Anti-TPO antibodies predict progression.
- Check lipids and symptoms, and look for heart failure.

## Evidence

In the TRUST trial (people aged 65 and over, TSH 4.6–19.9), levothyroxine didn’t improve symptoms or tiredness. A TSH of 10 or more is linked to coronary events and heart failure.

## Management

- **Age 70–80 and over with TSH below 10:** usually don’t treat; recheck TSH every 6–12 months.
- **TSH 10 or more:** consider levothyroxine, especially with symptoms, heart failure, high cardiovascular risk or positive antibodies.
- **If treating:** start low (25 mcg), titrate slowly (especially with coronary disease), and aim for an age-appropriate TSH (about 4–6 above 70). Avoid overtreatment, which causes AF and bone loss.

## One-liner

A TSH of 7 in an 80-year-old is often just age, so repeat it before reaching for levothyroxine.
