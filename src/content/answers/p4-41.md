## SPIKES (Baile and Buckman)

- **S, Setting:** a private room; sit down; phone off; enough time; the right people present, with the patient’s agreement; hearing aids, glasses or an interpreter if needed.
- **P, Perception:** find out what they already know (“What have you been told so far?”).
- **I, Invitation:** ask how much they want to know, and who they want with them.
- **K, Knowledge:** give a warning shot, then the news in plain words and small chunks, without jargon, checking understanding as you go.
- **E, Emotions:** allow silence and acknowledge feelings; don’t rush to reassure. NURSE helps: Name, Understand, Respect, Support, Explore.
- **S, Strategy and summary:** agree a plan, discuss realistic hopes, summarise, and arrange follow-up.

## In older adults, and in India

- Families often ask doctors not to tell the patient (collusion). Explore the family’s fears, but ask the patient how much they want to know and who should be involved; the patient has the right to know.
- Allow for hearing, vision and cognition: slow down, repeat, and write down the key points.
- Assess capacity, and respect cultural and religious beliefs.

## Avoid

Corridor conversations, jargon, false reassurance, “nothing more can be done”, and exact timelines you can’t be sure of.

## Afterwards

Document the conversation, update the team, involve palliative care, and follow up.

## One-liner

Ask before you tell: find out what they know, and what they want to know.
