## Answer at a glance

1. Drugs and mechanism.
2. Uses.
3. Choice and dosing in older adults.
4. Side effects, especially in older adults.
5. Interactions and duration of treatment.

## Drugs

Fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, fluvoxamine.

## Mechanism

They block the serotonin transporter (SERT), raising synaptic serotonin, with later receptor adaptation. The antidepressant effect takes 2–4 weeks, often longer in older adults.

## Uses

- Depression, generalised anxiety, panic, OCD, PTSD, social anxiety.
- Post-stroke depression; agitation in dementia (citalopram).

## Choice in older adults

- **Sertraline or escitalopram/citalopram,** which have fewer interactions.
- Avoid paroxetine (anticholinergic; Beers) and fluoxetine (long half-life, interactions).
- Start at half dose and titrate slowly.
- QT limits: citalopram no more than 20 mg a day over 60; escitalopram no more than 10 mg in the elderly.

## Side effects

- **Common:** nausea, headache, insomnia or drowsiness, sweating, sexual dysfunction.
- **Older-adult risks:**
  - **Hyponatraemia (SIADH):** check sodium at baseline and at 2–4 weeks.
  - **Falls and fractures.**
  - **GI bleeding,** especially with NSAIDs, aspirin or anticoagulants; add a PPI.
  - QT prolongation; bradycardia; extrapyramidal effects; apathy.
  - **Serotonin syndrome** with tramadol, linezolid, triptans or MAOIs.
- **Discontinuation syndrome:** worst with paroxetine, so taper.

## Interactions

- Fluoxetine and paroxetine inhibit CYP2D6 (metoprolol, tamoxifen, codeine).
- Fluvoxamine inhibits CYP1A2 (theophylline, clozapine).

## Duration

6–12 months after remission for a first episode; 2 years or more in older adults at risk of relapse.

## One-liner

In older adults, choose sertraline or escitalopram, start low, and check the sodium at two weeks.
