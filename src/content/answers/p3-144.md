## Answer at a glance

1. Define and classify (WHO 2022).
2. Etiology and pathogenesis.
3. Clinical features and differential diagnosis.
4. Investigations and risk scoring.
5. Treatment by risk, fitness and goals.

## Definition

Clonal stem-cell neoplasms with ineffective blood formation, causing cytopenias, dysplasia and a risk of progression to AML. The median age at diagnosis is over 70.

## Classification (WHO 2022, now called “myelodysplastic neoplasms”)

- **Genetically defined:** low blasts with isolated del(5q); low blasts with an SF3B1 mutation; biallelic TP53 inactivation.
- **Morphologically defined:** low blasts; hypoplastic; with fibrosis; and increased blasts, either IB1 (5–9% in marrow or 2–4% in blood) or IB2 (10–19% in marrow, 5–19% in blood, or Auer rods).
- The alternative ICC 2022 system calls 10–19% blasts “MDS/AML”.

## Etiology

Mostly age-related, often growing out of clonal haematopoiesis. Also therapy-related (after alkylating agents, topoisomerase inhibitors or radiotherapy), benzene, smoking, and inherited predisposition such as DDX41 or GATA2.

## Pathogenesis

Somatic mutations affecting splicing (SF3B1, SRSF2, U2AF1), epigenetics (TET2, DNMT3A, ASXL1) or TP53, in an inflamed marrow. Progenitors die by apoptosis, so a busy marrow yields few mature cells.

## Clinical features

Anaemia symptoms often put down to “old age”, infections, bleeding and incidental macrocytosis; sometimes autoimmune features.

## Differential diagnosis

B12, folate or copper deficiency; alcohol; drugs such as methotrexate, azathioprine and hydroxyurea; HIV; liver or thyroid disease; anaemia of CKD; aplastic anaemia; PNH; myelofibrosis; AML; clonal cytopenia of undetermined significance.

## Investigations

- **Blood count and smear:** macrocytosis, hypogranular or pseudo-Pelger neutrophils.
- Reticulocytes, B12, folate, copper, ferritin, serum EPO.
- **Marrow aspirate and biopsy:** dysplasia in 10% or more of a lineage, blast count, ring sideroblasts on iron stain, fibrosis.
- Karyotype and an NGS mutation panel.

## Risk scoring

IPSS-R uses karyotype, blasts, haemoglobin, platelets and neutrophils; IPSS-M adds mutations.

## Treatment (by risk, fitness and goals)

- **Lower risk:** observe if stable; transfuse; chelate iron if transfusion burden is heavy; ESA if serum EPO is below 500 U/L; luspatercept, especially with an SF3B1 mutation or ring sideroblasts; lenalidomide for del(5q); imetelstat for transfusion dependence after an ESA; immunosuppression for hypoplastic MDS.
- **Higher risk:** azacitidine (which improves survival) or decitabine; reduced-intensity allogeneic transplant for fit patients, since age alone doesn’t exclude it.
- **Always:** manage infections, give platelets for bleeding, and involve palliative care where appropriate.

## Examiner’s traps

- Blaming macrocytic anaemia on age without doing a marrow
- Treating before the cytogenetic risk is known

## One-liner

Unexplained macrocytic anaemia with normal B12, folate and TSH in an older adult needs a bone marrow.
