## Answer at a glance

1. Reframe the problem.
2. Assess: his view, barriers, nutrition, control and harms, the family.
3. Individualise the goals.
4. Liberalise the diet and adjust medicines.
5. Communicate and follow up.

## Reframe first

Ask whether the advice still fits his frailty, rather than only why he isn’t following it.

## Assess (CGA-style)

- **His view:** what he understands and believes, his food preferences and culture (rice, sweets, festivals), what food means to him, and his capacity to choose.
- **Barriers:** cognitive impairment, depression, poor appetite, dentition or swallowing, taste changes, money, who cooks, and functional limits.
- **Nutrition:** weight trend, MNA-SF, sarcopenia. **In moderate frailty, undernutrition is often a bigger threat than high glucose.**
- **Control and harms:** HbA1c, glucose logs, hypoglycaemia, blood pressure including standing, kidney function.
- **The family dynamic:** the daughter’s worries alongside his autonomy.

## Individualise the goals

- HbA1c below 8.0% for “complex/intermediate” health (ADA older-adult guidance). Avoid hypoglycaemia above all.
- A less strict blood pressure target, for example below 140/90, avoiding postural drops.

## Liberalise the diet

- Regular meals with enough protein (1.0–1.2 g/kg).
- Limit sugary drinks and sweets rather than banning foods.
- Moderate salt rather than very low salt (very low-salt diets risk poor intake and hyponatraemia).

## Adjust medicines instead of policing meals

- Stop sulfonylureas and prefer drugs with low hypoglycaemia risk (DPP-4 inhibitors, simplified basal insulin).
- Review antihypertensives for postural drops.

## Communicate

- Shared decision-making and motivational interviewing.
- Educate the daughter about priorities in frailty, which also reduces conflict.
- Dietitian referral and a simple written plan.
- Treat barriers: dental care, depression, meal support.

## Follow up

Weight, glucose and blood pressure in 1–3 months.

## One-liner

In moderate frailty a liberal diet and fewer drugs usually beat a strict diet nobody can follow; negotiate goals, don’t police meals.
