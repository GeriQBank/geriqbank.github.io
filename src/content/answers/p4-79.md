## Answer at a glance

1. The spectrum of nutritional disorders, and the burden.
2. Causes: the 9 Ds and more.
3. Screening and diagnosis (GLIM).
4. Assessment.
5. Management, including refeeding syndrome and obesity.

## The spectrum

Undernutrition (protein–energy malnutrition), micronutrient deficiency, dehydration, and overnutrition, including sarcopenic obesity.

## Burden

Malnutrition affects a minority of older people at home but 20–50% of those in hospital. In India, roughly a fifth of older adults are underweight (LASI).

## Causes: the “9 Ds”

Dentition, dysgeusia (altered taste), dysphagia, diarrhoea, depression, disease, dementia, dysfunction (dependence), drugs. Plus the anorexia of ageing (less taste and smell, early fullness), poverty, living alone, bereavement, and hospital factors such as fasting for tests and no help at meals.

## Screening

The MNA-Short Form in the community; MUST or NRS-2002 in hospital.

## Diagnosis (GLIM 2019)

At least one phenotypic and one causal criterion.

- **Phenotypic:**
  - Weight loss over 5% in 6 months, or over 10% beyond 6 months.
  - Low BMI: in Asians, below 18.5 under 70 and below 20 from 70.
  - Low muscle mass.
- **Causal:** reduced intake or absorption, or disease-related inflammation.

## Assessment

- Diet history and weight trend; BMI; mid-arm and calf circumference; grip strength.
- Mouth, teeth and swallowing; cognition and mood; function; social situation.
- **Blood tests:** count, electrolytes, glucose, renal, liver and thyroid function, B12, folate, iron, vitamin D, zinc.
- Albumin reflects illness and inflammation more than nutrition.

## Management

- **Treat the causes:** depression, loose dentures, swallowing problems, constipation, and appetite-killing drugs.
- **Food first:** fortified foods, small frequent energy-dense meals, favourite foods, help with eating, protected mealtimes.
- **Targets:** about 30 kcal/kg a day and 1.0–1.2 g/kg of protein, more when ill.
- **Oral nutritional supplements** of at least 400 kcal and 30 g protein a day when food isn’t enough.
- **Replace micronutrients:** B12, vitamin D, iron, folate, thiamine.
- **Tube feeding** when the gut works and it will help, such as dysphagia after stroke, but not in advanced dementia.
- **Refeeding syndrome:** in high-risk patients, start slowly, give thiamine, and monitor potassium, phosphate and magnesium.
- **Resistance exercise** alongside nutrition, to rebuild muscle.
- **Social support:** meal services, community kitchens, and the Annapurna scheme.
- **Drugs:** avoid megestrol; use mirtazapine only if the person is also depressed.

## Obesity

Modest weight loss only, with exercise and enough protein to protect muscle.

## Monitoring

Weight at every visit; food charts in hospital.

## One-liner

Weigh every older patient at every visit, because unintended weight loss is a warning sign.
