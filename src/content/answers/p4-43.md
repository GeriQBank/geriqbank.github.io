## Answer at a glance

## Definition (WHO)

An approach that improves quality of life for patients and families facing life-threatening illness. It works by preventing and relieving suffering through early identification and impeccable assessment and treatment of pain and other physical, psychosocial and spiritual problems.

It belongs **early, alongside disease treatment**, and in non-cancer illness too: heart failure, COPD, dementia, CKD, frailty.

## Principles (WHO)

- Relieve pain and distressing symptoms.
- Affirm life and regard dying as normal; neither hasten nor postpone death.
- Integrate psychological and spiritual care.
- Help patients live as actively as possible.
- Support families during illness and bereavement.
- Work as a team; start early.

## In geriatrics

- Multimorbidity, dementia and uncertain trajectories (cancer, organ failure, frailty and dementia).
- Identify patients with the “surprise question” and SPICT.

## Interventions that reduce suffering

- **Physical:**
  - **Pain:** a modified WHO ladder; morphine if kidney function is normal, fentanyl or buprenorphine in CKD; always a laxative with opioids.
  - **Breathlessness:** low-dose opioids, a fan, positioning.
  - **Nausea:** antiemetics chosen by cause.
  - **Others:** constipation, delirium, fatigue, anorexia, mouth care, pressure care, itch.
- **Psychological:** screen for and treat depression and anxiety; counselling; dignity therapy.
- **Social:** caregiver support, finances, home care, respite.
- **Spiritual:** explore meaning and faith; spiritual care.
- **Communication and decisions:**
  - Honest prognosis and breaking bad news; advance care planning; goals of care.
  - **Stop burdensome or non-beneficial treatment:** tube feeding in advanced dementia, futile CPR.
- **End of life and after:** care in the last days, anticipatory prescribing, preferred place of death, bereavement support.

## Models of care

Hospital palliative teams, hospices, home-based care. Kerala’s Neighbourhood Network in Palliative Care is a community model.

**India:** the National Programme for Palliative Care, and the 2014 NDPS amendment, which eased access to oral morphine.

## One-liner

Palliative care isn’t the last resort but the right care alongside cure: control symptoms, talk honestly and support the family, from diagnosis, not just the last week.
