## Why it matters

About 1 in 3 people over 65 fall each year, risking injury, fear of falling and loss of independence.

## Screening (World Falls Guidelines 2022; CDC STEADI)

- Ask everyone three questions: have you fallen in the past year? Do you feel unsteady? Do you worry about falling?
- **High risk:** a fall with injury, 2 or more falls, frailty, lying on the floor unable to get up, or suspected syncope. These people need a full multifactorial assessment.
- Otherwise, check gait speed (below 0.8 m/s) or the Timed Up and Go (over 15 s) to find those at intermediate risk, who need an exercise programme.

## Performance tests

- **Timed Up and Go:** cut-offs of 12–15 s are used.
- **Gait speed:** below 0.8 m/s is abnormal.
- **Chair rises:** the 30-second chair stand, or five sit-to-stands (over 15 s is abnormal).
- **4-stage balance test:** unable to hold a tandem stance for 10 s is abnormal.
- **Berg Balance Scale:** below 45 of 56.
- **Tinetti POMA:** below 19 of 28.
- **Functional reach:** under 15 cm.
- **SPPB.**

## Rating scales

- **In hospital:** Morse Fall Scale (45 or more is high risk), STRATIFY, Hendrich II.
- **General:** Downton Fall Risk Index (3 or more is high risk).
- **Fear of falling:** Falls Efficacy Scale-International (FES-I).

## Multifactorial assessment

- Falls history and medicines that increase fall risk.
- Vision, orthostatic blood pressure, cognition, continence.
- Feet and footwear, gait, balance and strength.
- Home hazards, osteoporosis and fracture risk, heart problems and syncope, fear of falling.

## Limitations

The tools predict only modestly. NICE advises against using falls prediction scores for inpatients aged 65 and over; instead, assess and address every risk factor.

## One-liner

Ask three questions and time one walk, then assess everything that could make them fall.
