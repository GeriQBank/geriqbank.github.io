## Answer at a glance

1. The biological framework (2024 criteria).
2. CSF, PET, blood, MRI and genetic markers.
3. Uses and limitations.

## Framework

Alzheimer’s is now defined biologically (Alzheimer’s Association criteria 2024, building on the 2018 AT(N) framework).

- **Core biomarkers** (amyloid and phosphorylated tau) establish the disease.
- Other markers add neurodegeneration (N), inflammation (I), vascular (V) and α-synuclein (S) information.

## CSF

Low Aβ42 (and a low Aβ42/40 ratio), high p-tau181 and p-tau217, high total tau.

## PET

Amyloid PET; tau PET for staging; FDG-PET showing temporoparietal hypometabolism.

## Blood

Plasma p-tau217 is the most accurate, alongside the Aβ42/40 ratio, and the first blood test was cleared in the US in 2025. Neurofilament light and GFAP mark damage and inflammation but aren’t specific.

## MRI

Medial temporal and hippocampal atrophy, and small-vessel disease.

## Genetics

APOE ε4 raises risk, and ARIA risk with anti-amyloid drugs, but doesn’t diagnose. APP, PSEN1 and PSEN2 mutations cause early-onset familial disease.

## Uses

Confirming amyloid before anti-amyloid therapy, telling Alzheimer’s from other dementias, and research. They are not for screening people without symptoms.

## Limitations

Cost and limited availability in India, variation between laboratories, and positive results in many cognitively normal older people.

## One-liner

Biomarkers show the biology, not the destiny; amyloid without symptoms is not dementia.
