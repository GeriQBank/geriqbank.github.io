## Answer at a glance

1. Define frailty and its burden.
2. Pathophysiology, including the cycle of frailty.
3. Diagnosis: phenotype, deficit accumulation, Clinical Frailty Scale, quick screens.
4. Evaluation by comprehensive geriatric assessment.
5. Prevention and management, clinical uses and outcomes.

## Definition

Increased vulnerability to stressors, caused by age-related loss of reserve across several body systems. It overlaps with disability and multimorbidity but is not the same as either.

## Burden

About 1 in 10 community-dwelling adults over 65 are frail, and many more are pre-frail. Prevalence rises with age.

## Pathophysiology

- Chronic inflammation (IL-6, CRP).
- Endocrine decline (testosterone, IGF-1, DHEA-S, vitamin D) with relatively high cortisol.
- Sarcopenia at the core, plus anorexia of ageing, insulin resistance, immune senescence and mitochondrial dysfunction.
- **Fried’s cycle of frailty:** undernutrition, then sarcopenia, then less strength, less activity and lower energy use, which feeds back into more undernutrition.

## Diagnosis

- **Fried phenotype:** unintentional weight loss (over 4.5 kg in a year), exhaustion, weak grip, slow gait and low activity. Three or more is frail; 1–2 is pre-frail.
- **Rockwood Frailty Index:** the proportion of listed deficits present; above 0.25 is frail.
- **Clinical Frailty Scale (1–9):** 5 or more is frail. Score the patient’s baseline from 2 weeks before any acute illness.
- **Quick screens:** FRAIL scale (3–5 is frail), PRISMA-7, Edmonton Frail Scale, gait speed.

## Evaluation

A comprehensive geriatric assessment to find treatable drivers: depression, heart failure, anaemia, thyroid disease, undernutrition, polypharmacy, sensory loss, social isolation.

## Prevention and management (ICFSR guidance)

- Multicomponent exercise with progressive resistance training, which has the strongest evidence.
- Protein and energy supplements when weight is falling.
- Vitamin D only if deficient.
- Deprescribing, treating causes of fatigue, and social support.
- WHO ICOPE for community programmes.

## Clinical uses

Risk stratification before surgery, chemotherapy or dialysis; prehabilitation; goals-of-care discussions.

## Outcomes if unaddressed

Falls, disability, hospitalisation, institutional care, death.

<section class="traps">

## Examiner’s traps

- Equating frailty with disability
- Scoring the Clinical Frailty Scale on the acutely ill day instead of the baseline

</section>

## One-liner

Frailty is a state, not a fate; exercise and protein can move people back towards robust.
