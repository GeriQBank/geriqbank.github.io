## Answer at a glance

1. Assessment of lower urinary tract symptoms.
2. Treatment, step by step.
3. What to do with a PSA of 10.

## Assessment

- IPSS score, and separate storage from voiding symptoms.
- Drug review, digital rectal exam, urinalysis, creatinine.
- PSA, after counselling.
- Frequency–volume chart for nocturia, post-void residual, uroflowmetry (Qmax below 10 mL/s suggests obstruction).

## Treatment

- **Mild symptoms:** watchful waiting and lifestyle changes (fluid timing, caffeine, alcohol).
- **Alpha-blocker** (tamsulosin, silodosin); watch for postural hypotension.
- **5-alpha reductase inhibitor** (finasteride, dutasteride) for large prostates. It cuts the risk of retention and surgery, and **halves the PSA**.
- Combination therapy for large glands.
- Beta-3 agonist or antimuscarinic for storage symptoms, after checking the residual.
- Tadalafil 5 mg as an option.
- Avoid desmopressin in the elderly (hyponatraemia).
- **Surgery** (TURP, HoLEP) for refractory symptoms, retention, stones, recurrent UTI, renal impairment or haematuria.

## A PSA of 10 ng/mL

1. Repeat it, excluding UTI, recent instrumentation and retention, and adjusting for any 5-alpha reductase inhibitor.
2. Consider life expectancy and his wishes. Investigate mainly if he’s expected to live more than 10 years.
3. If proceeding: multiparametric MRI first, then targeted plus systematic biopsy (transperineal) for PI-RADS 3 or above. A PSA density above 0.15 adds concern.
4. An abnormal rectal exam means biopsy regardless.

## One-liner

MRI before biopsy, and ask whether the result would change his life.
