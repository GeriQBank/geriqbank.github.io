## Purpose

Better mobility, balance, safety and independence, with fewer falls and less caregiver burden.

## Mobility aids

- **Walking stick:** held in the hand **opposite** the weak or painful leg. The handle should be at wrist-crease height, with the elbow slightly bent. A quad stick is steadier but slower.
- **Crutches:** axillary crutches risk axillary nerve compression; elbow crutches are an alternative.
- **Walking frames:** a standard pick-up frame is the most stable but slow. Two-wheeled frames, and rollators (four wheels, brakes and a seat), suit people with limited endurance but need enough cognition to use the brakes.
- **Wheelchairs:** manual or powered; transfer chairs; scooters.
- Every aid must be fitted and taught by a physiotherapist, and checked regularly, including its rubber tips.

## Other assistive devices

- **Bathroom:** grab bars, raised toilet seat, shower chair, non-slip mats, commode.
- **Dressing and daily tasks:** long shoehorn, sock aid, reacher, button hook, elastic laces.
- **Eating:** built-up cutlery, plate guards, spill-proof cups.
- **Transfers:** bed rails, transfer boards, hoists.
- **Senses:** spectacles, magnifiers, hearing aids, amplified phones.
- **Safety:** personal alarms and fall detectors, pill organisers, GPS trackers for people who wander.
- **Orthoses:** knee braces, ankle-foot orthoses; hip protectors.
- Continence aids.

## Prescribing principles

Assess function, cognition and the home (an occupational therapist’s home visit helps), choose the simplest aid that works, fit it, train the person, and follow up.

## In India

Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana provides free aids (walking sticks, crutches, walkers, tripods, wheelchairs, hearing aids, spectacles, dentures) to eligible older people. ALIMCO manufactures many of them.

## Pitfalls

Ill-fitting aids cause falls, and so does a stick in the wrong hand. Rollators are unsafe if the person can’t use the brakes. Devices often go unused because of stigma.

## One-liner

The right aid, fitted and taught, keeps an older person upright; the wrong one trips them up.
